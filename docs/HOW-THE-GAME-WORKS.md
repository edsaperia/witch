# How Witch works today

How the game behaves right now, as the rules code runs it (`src/rules/`), with the numbers from the config files. Knob names are in `backticks` the first time a number appears; most are in `config/tuning.json`. Where something couldn't be confirmed in the code, it says "(unsure)".

---

## 1. The big picture

You're a witch throwing a rave in a magical forest. The party starts at your home dancefloor and spreads out one area at a time. Each new area gets a **soundsystem**, which wakes that area's wild animals. They're offended they weren't invited, so they march on it to shut it down. You fly about inviting animals with 💌s, build a party crew, and park them where they're needed to defend. Every party ends eventually. When the last soundsystem and the home speakers fall, **the party's over**: the run winds down into a peaceful afterparty, with no game-over screen.

**The map** is a round island of **home and 10 areas**, one of each kind she knows, growing by one for each kind whose island she's found (wave 2 D, `rules/rosters.ts` `knownKinds`: its first areas keep their places and kinds, each new kind an area further out; `map.islandAreas` 10 its least; Ed, 2026-10-11, the journey's first step: `?island=10|20|30|40|50` for bigger ones, `?island=off` for the old map of about 100, `map.radius` 5.6), the areas nearest home, each about 168 m across (`areaSize` 28 × `areaScale` 6). It has 32 area types, each with its own species: moor/badger, fern forest/boar, muddy forest/snail, stone shrine/fox, tangly forest/ram, wispy forest/woodlouse, hazel forest/hedgehog, garden/squirrel, twiggy forest/wolf, ancient/stag, Norway/stoat, alder/snake, meadow/hare, old oaks/owl, berry thicket/bear, wetland/toad, stream/otter, rocky slope/lynx, bog/elk, deadwood/raven, cave mouth/bat, grassland/mole, beaver pond/beaver, log pile/beetle, heath/moth, old pinewood/marten, ravine/salamander, meadow/wildcat, holly thicket/spider, honeysuckle tangle/dormouse, fen/newt and heronry/heron. The island grows out from home over its areas, more irregular the further out its phase (Ed, 2026-10-11: home near-round; phase 1 gently lobed; 2 bays and a peninsula; 3 arms and inlets; 4 and 5 strongly irregular; `rules/islandGrowth.ts`, the tuning's `map.shapes`), and its coast runs round its own areas (its playable ones and every area within about 180 m of them), following their ragged borders rather than a circle (the land map, `rules/land.ts`). From phase 1 an island has lakes: a few of its areas are water (never home or its neighbours), with small shores rather than beaches, water lighter than the sea, and koi in some. She can fly over a lake in the treetops but can't land on it, or come down over it at all (the press does nothing but a soft "nope"; Ed, 2026-10-23); on foot she, and every creature, stops at the shore, and creatures go round. A beach runs round the coast. Fly out past it and she lands and lies down to stargaze, and now and then there's a beach witch to cuddle up with (an Easter egg).

**Home** is the middle area: the dancefloor, a ring of 12 speakers (`dancefloor.speakers.count`) and the treehouse. Home has no animals and no legend.

## 2. How a run plays out

0. **The new opening (on by default since 2026-10-21; `?opening=old` keeps the old start).** A plain first load (its link naming no seed or island) opens on the **sea chart** instead of the creator (Ed, 2026-10-20; `app/opening.ts`, the storyboard at previews/ed/opening-mock/ on claude/ed-shots).
   - A first game has only home to sail to: what's unlocked is home and what she's found.
   - The chart's corner holds **What's new** and **Options**. The zoo is reached through the bestiary's book in the treehouse.
   - Home's night is made under the chart, and its art is drawn meanwhile.
   - **Choosing home** goes on into that night, not a new one. The chart's flight carries on over the forest, up to frame the treehouse, and in to her **bedroom**, where she wakes beside her bed (`rules/opening.ts`). Any key skips the flight.
   - **The wake-up** (`rules/wakeUp.ts`, the page's card and dark in `render/wakeUp.ts`). She lands in bed in the dark, with her phone's alarm ringing:
     - **E** snoozes it for 5 s, as often as you like; the card counts the snoozes and when it rings again.
     - **WASD** (or a rise) gets her up: she stretches (3 s), the room lights, and she stands beside the bed. WASD walks.
     - The first time she comes to the **dressing table**, its mirror opens by itself (skin, hair and earrings).
     - The **curtains** stay shut until the island is ready (its art drawn once). E there says "not yet" until then; after that, E opens them.
     - Until the curtains open she can't leave the bedroom, the bed doesn't end the night, and no key casts the spell. Nothing gets her ahead of the build.
   - **The DJ intro** (Ed's decision 2) replaces the party spell before the party has started. Out through the house to her decks, she presses **E** behind them: she's seated, the spell is cast there, and its 6.5 s needle-drop routine plays. As she steps off the decks the party is on. After a knockout, everything is as it was.
   - **Every morning is the same wake-up** (Ed, 2026-10-26, replacing the shorter one he'd chosen for later loads and after the bed): the alarm rings, E snoozes it, a move starts the 3-second stretch and then she's up. Only a game's first morning (a fresh load, no night played yet) differs: no pile of last night's clothes, her hat on the hats' floor, and her dressing guided. The dressing table's mirror is marked only on her first-ever visit.
   - **After the bed:** she sleeps in the dark while the next island is made, then wakes in her bedroom, the same wake-up.
   - **Controls** live on the pause page (a ❔ Controls button while paused). What's new and Options are in the chart's corner, and the zoo is reached through the bestiary's book.
   - Rooms are all lit alike; no path is lit to the decks.
   - Any other island (on a later game) starts its night in place, as a sail does. Nothing is recorded on the chart, since the boot's night under it was never played.
1. **Dress her up.** The run opens in the character creator. When the scroll unrolls, click it (or press Enter) to **cast the party spell**. The cast takes 1.2 s, and she can't move until it's done.
2. **At the decks.** She sits behind the decks in the treehouse until you first move or rise.
3. **Boot-up.** When she leaves the decks a pulse runs from the treehouse down to the home ring and round it at 4 m/s (`boot.speed`: the ley pulse's speed till Ed slowed the waves to 1 m/s on 2026-10-18, the boot kept as it was), turning each little runestone into a speaker as it passes: the first about 3 s in, the whole 179 m path in **about 45 s**. The animals still attack during it, but no waves come.
4. **Waves.** When the boot ends, the ley pulse sets off along the route at a **constant 1 m/s** (`leyLines.pulseSpeed`; Ed, 2026-10-18: "Default starting wave speed is 1m/s"; 4 before; times the party's tempo, so a knockdown's BPM speeds it; Ed, 2026-10-09: "Pure constant speed"), and **a wave lands as it reaches the next stone**: each gap is that link's length over the speed, no fixed interval. Links run about 30 m to 600 m, so at 1 m/s gaps run from half a minute to ten minutes. The dancefloor switches on at the first wave. The pause page's **Waves** slider (pinned at its top, beside Volume) sets the speed live, 0 (the waves held) to 6 m/s, and this browser remembers it; `?pulse=<m/s>` or `?pulse=off` sets it for one load. (The bedroom's Waves dropdown is gone. The Player dropdown, a human or a bot playing the run, shows in the bedroom's top right only with the debug overlay on, whose ` key now works in the bedroom too: Ed, 2026-10-11.)
5. **Each wave goes to the next stone on the route** (`party.areasPerWave` 1). It doesn't take every neighbour, just the next area along a route planned for the whole map (`party.picker` "route", `party.route` "spiral"): outward from home, ring by ring, crossing itself as little as possible. The ley line traces that same route, so you always know what's next. The waves keep that schedule whatever you do: each takes the next stone on the route, never skipping one.
   - If that stone is still wild, the wave **partifies** its area (below).
   - If you've **converted** it already with the spell, so its soundsystem plays, the wave **does nothing to the rules**: no animal is enraged and no second soundsystem comes. It **celebrates** instead (the `waveCelebrate` event: fireworks and that soundsystem's lasers). The music's next step, the ley line and the countdown carry on as normal.
   - If its early soundsystem has been lost, the wave passes it quietly.
6. **The soundsystem spell** (Ed, 2026-10-28; rules/stoneStacks.ts) converts a stone **without waiting for its wave**. Every wild animal belongs to its area's runestone, its sigil in the **stack** above the stone (friends above enemies, each group babies at the top, then youths, adults and the legend at the bottom; one that runs off leaves it). Inviting one turns its sigil **friend-coloured in the stack**: no rune at its feet. Nothing clears by itself any more: she stands within **4 m** of the stone on foot (`cast.radius`) and **holds the sigil button** (E; the pad's square) for **6 s** (`cast.time`). A **hit stops the cast**, and holding on does nothing till she lets go and holds again; let go, her progress there drains at **0.15 of a cast a second** (`cast.drain`), and holding again resumes from where it is. Each witch's cast is her own (two at one stone fill no faster). The spell's music-note sigil shows within 12 m of the stone (`cast.show`). Full, the stone converts just as a wave would (the `areaCleared` event, below). **For now this is off by default** (`cast.on` false, till the view of the stacks lands; `?cast=1` turns it on), and the old rule plays: an area clears itself when none of its own wild young and adults is left.
7. **A new soundsystem** rises at the area's runestone. It has **500 health** (`combat.soundsystemHealth`; Ed, 2026-10-08, from 4000: shorter games, falls that cascade, and you must stop a swarm before it arrives). Home is its ring of **12 speakers**, each **500 health** (`combat.speakerHealth`; Ed, 2026-10-08). When it arrives:
   - the area's wild young and adults become **enraged** and **besiege** it (none in a friendly area: there every animal turns friendly, young and adults too). Besiegers march at **half their top speed** (`combat.marchShare` 0.5 of their kind's fight run: a wolf about 7 m/s; Ed, 2026-10-11, "they should visibly hurry"), on their own area's soundsystem and marching on alike, and run at full speed once within 40 m. Against a soundsystem or speaker, a charging kind (boar, elk, ram, hedgehog...) doesn't charge and a swooper (owl, raven, bat) doesn't swoop: each lands and uses its ordinary attack (Ed, 2026-10-11: a charge struck it once a run, a swoop once a dive). At her and her animals they charge and swoop as ever;
   - its wild babies turn **happy** on the spot and join the party, whether you cleared it or its wave transformed it (Ed, 2026-10-07: "Wild babies become party babies when the pulse transforms an uncleared area");
   - its happy animals come and **dance** round it, or at the area's party spots, and **every friend sigil falls and scatters** over its runestone clearing (`cast.scatter`: free hex cells of its open ground out to 40 m, never in the wedge just north of the soundsystem, where its sprite would hide them), the legend's too (unless it's angry), **loose** for any witch to pick up with E, which leashes the animal. Loose sigils stay pick-up-able even if that soundsystem later falls.
   - A **legend's sigil**, picked up, doesn't leash the legend (it sleeps on, till its quest): it's too heavy to drag and pulls her nowhere, but she **can't go further from the legend than the distance its sigil lay from it** when she picked it up. Putting it down (E, anywhere, as the next sigil she puts down) frees her; knocked out, it falls back at the legend's feet.
8. **Losing a soundsystem**: the area is **ruined** and its party ends for good. Its happy babies run off the map for good (not one whose sigil still lies loose: it waits for her), and its besiegers march on to the **next-nearest standing soundsystem** that's **no further from the centre** than they are (Ed, 2026-10-11: enraged animals march inward; one they're within 10 m of already counts), home's speakers always included, at the besiegers' march (below). A loss touches neither the wave countdown nor the tempo (Ed, 2026-10-08: "Losing a soundsystem no longer touches the wave countdown", "doesn't affect the bpm").
9. **The party's over** once the home ring's last speaker is down, whatever soundsystems still stand (Ed, 2026-10-10; rules/partyOver.ts):
   - the waves stop and nothing fights;
   - the lights and music wind down over 6 s (`partyOver.ease`);
   - any soundsystem still standing switches off as the lights go out across the map: silent and dark, still standing, not destroyed;
   - every animal (yours too, and the ones that ran off) walks home or is simply there, asleep;
   - you can wander the map safely.
10. **The night won**: the end of the route (Ed, 2026-10-10, 2026-10-11; rules/win.ts, tuning `win`):
   - **Last call:** while the next wave's stone is the route's last, the wave pointer shows 🎉 instead of 🎶.
   - **Stragglers first:** once the last wave has landed, or as soon as **every area on the island is cleared** before its wave (Ed, 2026-10-12: the waves still to come would only celebrate, so nothing waits for the wave's pulse to reach the route's end), while any enraged animal lives, a pointer shows the way to the one **nearest the centre** (re-picked four times a second). Enraged **legends don't count**: they never hold the win up.
   - **The victory lap:** with none left, at once, **two** fast pulses (sparklers) set off from wherever the wave's pulse is on the ley line (Ed, 2026-10-12), one racing back to home and one on to the route's end, both at **120 m/s** (`win.lapSpeed`), the pointer following the one going home with 🎉, each stone either passes shooting **fireworks** (the outward one lighting the line as it goes), the waves stopping as it sets off, the music on to its **victory** section from that block (the music style's `victory`; the drop until it has one).
   - **The win**, as the lap reaches home:
     - the night's legends (the dealt kinds', `map.legendKinds`) wake and come home to dance as **party legends**, dancing in place on the dancefloor's far (north) side, beyond the conga and behind it all from the camera: in an arc 12 m outside the conga's ring, 14 m apart, clear of the treehouse, a row further out if many (`win.legendGap`, `legendRow`, `legendSpace`, `legendArc`); they don't join the conga;
     - an enraged legend calms and goes back to sleep in its clearing instead;
     - the win itself finds **no islands** (Ed, 2026-10-18: "Just winning an island does not unlock more islands"): the islands found are those whose legends' quests you did tonight, and the won night is what keeps them on the sea chart;
     - every other animal on the island becomes a party animal, and they all join **one conga line** round the dancefloor (2.5 m/s, 2.4 m apart, a circle at least 34 m across or as big as the line needs: `win.conga*`), walking to their places (out of her sight, they're simply there); her leash lets go of hers to join it;
     - **fireworks** from every soundsystem still standing;
     - **the island's sigil flares on the dancefloor** (its kind's, home's the World Tree: rules/rosters.ts), drawn on again over 3 s and held 16 s (`win.flareDraw`, `win.flare`), then the party's patterns again;
     - each woken legend's **dream bubble shows its home island**: its coast with its sigil, the picture the sea chart shows;
     - nothing fights and no more waves come.
   - About 20 s later (`win.settleAfter`) the win settles: the **islands found tonight**, one for each legend whose quest you did this night, are saved to the sea chart (a night lost keeps none of them: Ed, 2026-10-18), and the guidance pin leads her home: 🧭 to the map table while one of them isn't yet seen on the chart, then 💤 to bed. There's no end card (Ed, 2026-10-26: "Remove the end cards").
   - Losing still comes first: if home's speakers all fall before the lap reaches home, the party's over as above.

Home's speakers are each a soundsystem in the fight: animals marching on home go for the nearest speaker still standing, and on to the next nearest when it falls. Each shows its own damage (damaged at half its health, destroyed at none). Home is lost, and the next wave hurried, only when the last speaker falls; and with it the party's over.

## 3. The witch

**Two modes.** One button switches between them. Rising takes 0.7 s (`riseTime`) and landing 0.55 s (`descendTime`). After she lands she **can't rise again for 3 s** (`riseCooldown`; Ed, 2026-10-11: rising takes her out of ground mode, and out of reach, at once, so players rose instead of dodging); a press before then does nothing.
- **On the ground**, under the trees, at **19.25 m/s** (`groundSpeed`). It's snappy, she stops dead, and it's where all the action is: inviting, sigils, fighting. Firing 💌s slows her to 0.8× (`invites.fireSlow`).
- **In the treetops**, above the canopy, at **48 m/s** (`treetopSpeed`). Holding a direction builds a boost up to **1.7×** over 2 s (`treetop.boost`, `treetop.boostTime`), and she has momentum and wide swoops. Sharp turns bleed the boost. From up here you only see the ground through clearings. **She can't be hurt in the treetops**: every attack in the code only targets her on the ground. She can't invite or place sigils from up here either. In the treetops the sigil button cycles her stack instead.

**Health.** She has **4 hits** (`witchHealth.hits`), and every blow costs one, whatever it was: the first three each leave a glowing claw slash on her, and the fourth knocks her out. She gets one back every **20 s** (`witchHealth.repairTime`), but the timer restarts at every hit, so to heal you have to get right out of the fight. After a hit there's **0.5 s of grace** (`witchHealth.grace`) when nothing else lands, so a pack striking together costs one hit, not four.

**Knockback and stagger.** A blow throws her back (2.5 m plus a share of the attack's own knockback, at least 12 m from a charge, at most 14 m: `witch.knock`). She's then staggered for 0.25–1 s, with no moving, blinking or 💌s. After a stagger she can't be staggered again for 1 s.

**The blink (dodge).** Ground only. She vanishes and reappears **10 m** away (`dash.distance`), **the way she's moving** (her steering, on the pad, keyboard and mouse, and touch alike; Ed, 2026-10-11: not the way she's aiming), and standing still, the way she's facing (up or down the screen when she's turned that way, else left or right). `dash.toCursor` (off) brings back blinking toward the cursor. She can blink **once a second** (`dash.cooldown` 1). She's untouchable only for the blink's 0.05 s (`dash.gone`), so it's for slipping out of a shot's path, not for tanking. A press up to 0.2 s early is remembered (`dash.buffer`). She never lands inside a tree, rock, soundsystem, speaker or the treehouse.

**Her broom: three parts and their sets** (the Workshop's rules, Ed 2026-10-23; `rules/broomParts.ts`, `config/broom-parts.json`). Her broom has three slots, the **tip**, the **chassis** and the **exhaust**, one part in each. A first game's broom is **bare** (every slot empty): the numbers above, and **no quick take-off** (a take-off adds 2 s to the shared cooldown and a landing 3 s; the old plain broom's halving is gone, won back by the Jet nozzles). Each of the nine parts changes one or two numbers, up and down (each part's own share over the bare broom's number, parts adding up):
- tips: **Missile nose** (treetop speed ×1.1, turning ×0.8), **Curled tip** (blinks ×1.5, each dodge's recharge ×1.3), **Hobby-horse head** (turning ×1.4, a sharp turn bleeds boost half as fast, boost peak −0.2);
- chassis: **Jet-bike fairing** (ground speed ×1.15, ground pick-up −8), **Ladder** (rise and come down ×0.65, ground speed ×0.9), **Canoe hull** (her sigils pull ×0.65, treetop pick-up ×0.75);
- exhausts: **Jet nozzles** (take-off and landing cooldowns halved, glides ×1.8 to a stop), **Twig bristles** (one more dodge, boost builds ×1.5 slower), **Oar blade** (ground pick-up +12, blinks ×0.8).

A **complete set** gives a broom spell, cast with R if it's active: **Rocket** (Missile nose, Jet-bike fairing, Jet nozzles) **Speed**, 2.2× speed for 4 s, then 12 s to recharge (`spells.speed`); **Hedge** (Curled tip, Ladder, Twig bristles) **Stealth**; **Ferry** (Hobby-horse head, Canoe hull, Oar blade) **Ley jump**; **Joust** (Hobby-horse head, Jet-bike fairing) **Battering**; **Pile driver** (Ladder, Jet nozzles) **Ground pound**. No broom completes two sets. With no set complete there's no broom spell. Parts are found in wrecks (section 11, tonight's find): a part found is hers but not fitted; she fits it at the **Workshop's bay** (section 11), where it goes on her broom at once, its stats and its set's spell with it, mid-night too, and is kept for the nights after. `?parts=missile,ladder,nozzles` fits any parts for one load (for testing; `?broom=` and the broom stand's Power row are gone).

**Sigil weight.** Every sigil she *carries* (not ones placed on the ground) pulls on her by how taut its leash is (`leash.weight`). The first 2.5 "weight units" are free. Past that:
- moving away from the pull is slower, and she drifts towards it;
- rising takes longer;
- over the treetops she's slowly pulled down.

Babies weigh 0.3, young 0.8, adults 2 and legends 3 (`leash.weight.levels`). So you can't just drag a huge army everywhere.

**Grounded by her followers** (Ed, 2026-10-18: "One adult stops you taking off. Three young do.", the numbers indicative: "if creatures are light or heavy you can extrapolate"). When the animals *following* her (their sigils in her stack) **weigh an adult's worth or more** together, she **can't take off** (`leash.grounded.weight`, 2: one adult of normal strength; three young weigh 2.4, two 1.6; each counts its level's weight times its species' strength, so a light species' adult may not ground her and a heavy one's young counts for more; 0 turns it off). Put their sigils down (parked) and she's free to go; babies never count. Press rise anyway and **she tries**: she lifts a metre and a half off the ground and is **pulled straight back down**, with a thud as she lands, while **the leashes of the followers weighing her down flash from dotted to solid lines** (taut, twice in about half a second, then dotted again; Ed's playtest, v3893). Babies' and parked animals' leashes don't flash. The hop is drawn only: she never leaves the ground, so it's no dodge, and it spends none of the shared cooldown. It's a weight, not a pull: it holds however close they stand, and apart from the sigil weight above (which still slows a take-off it allows). **Up in the treetops she stays up**: if a young grows up while she flies, or she's coming down, nothing pulls her out of the sky; the rule only stops her next take-off from the ground.

**Knocked out.** At zero hits she collapses.
- **Every animal she carried is let go happy** (#1268, 2026-10-27; `rules/knockout.ts` `letGoHappy`): no sigil goes down; each idles within **6 m** of where it was dropped (`knockout.dropRadius`), dancing if that's a party area, until she comes back and leashes it again.
- She sparkles out and back in **behind her decks** (the teleport takes 1.6 s, `knockout.teleport`) with full health. She scratches there until **6 s** after she went down (`knockout.respawn.base`), the same every time: repeat knockdowns no longer wait longer (Ed, 2026-10-07: "We can remove the extra knockdown time", since each knockdown costs a BPM instead; `respawn.step` 0).
- **She drops her hat** where she fell, and a 🎩 pointer shows the way back to it. Stand on it and press the sigil button to put it back on. If she gets knocked out again before picking it up, no second hat drops. (The hat doesn't change any number that could be found in the rules.)
- **The beat speeds up** (Ed, 2026-10-07: "the BPM goes up by 1 each time you die": the DJ's off the decks and the crowd gets restless; 2026-10-08: "Knockdowns increase the bpm by 5"). Each knockdown adds **5 BPM** to the party's tempo for the rest of the run (`knockout.bpmStep`; `knockout.bpmCap`, if set, is the most it can add), eased in from the next bar line over a bar (`beat.knockBeats` 4). The music follows it, and the wave countdown and the ley line's pulse run that much faster (the tempo with the bonus over the wave's own), so every wave comes a little sooner. The tempo shows on the wave pointer (the sparkler's marker) and on a little display over her decks (Ed: "The BPM is shown on the sparkler marker, and above the decks"), both pulsing as it rises.

## 5. Creatures

**Levels.** Every species comes in four levels: baby, young, adult and legend.

| Level | Health | Damage a second | Attacks? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baby | 30 | 0 | never attacks, and is never attacked |
| Young | 45 | 3 | its species' attack |
| Adult | 160 | 10 | the same attack grown (one more in its pattern, a fifth bigger, a tenth further) |
| Legend | 480 | 12 | see Legends |

(`config/combat.json` `levels`.) Every attack is telegraphed with a wind-up, and you can step aside or blink out of it. A charge that misses leaves the animal winded for 1.2 s, which is a free window for 💌s (`fight.charge.miss`).

**Each species has its own attack** (#1306 to #1326, 2026-10-27; on by default, `attacks.species`; `?attacks=old` brings back the old flat bites and bolts). Attacks are built from a small **grammar** (`config/attacks.json`, `rules/attacks/engine.ts`, `docs/plans/attack-grammar.md`) of independent pieces:
- the **cast**: its wind-up (0.6 s unless it says otherwise), its cooldown, and the body's **tell** (rearing, crouching, coiling, spreading, glowing or stamping);
- the **telegraph** you see before it lands: a lane, a landing spot, a ring, a cone, a beam line, a countdown, an arming mine, the next link of a chain, a tether line or a lock-on;
- what it **throws** (single, a burst, a fan, a ring, a spray, a wall or a volley) and **what that is** (a bolt, a lob, something running along the ground, a beam, an area, a lingering zone, a mine, a seed, or a melee strike);
- how it **flies** (arcing, curving, waving, spiralling, homing, boomeranging, orbiting; at a steady speed, speeding up, slowing down or stop-go), how far, and whether it pierces, bounces or sticks;
- what a **hit** does (damage as a share of its level's budget; slow, stun, knockback, pull, root or burn), and what it sets off when it hits, lands, bounces or runs out (up to 3 deep and 64 pieces a cast);
- how a **pack** plays it together: chained, relayed, amplified, tethered between two of them, or triangulated.
Whatever it's made of, every attack warns you at least **0.45 s** before its first harm (`attacks.reactionFloor`); one that wouldn't has its warning lengthened. Each species' own attack is in `config/species-attacks.json` (`docs/plans/attack-species.md`): no two in a biome alike, and the further out its island's phase, the more pieces it combines (home about 0.7 of a piece's worth, phase 1 1.7, phase 2 2.0, phase 3 2.5, phase 4 2.7). At home the hedgehog throws a ring of 6 quills, the squirrel lobs an acorn, the fox sends a curving foxfire wisp, the mole heaves the ground where she stood and the snail leaves a slowing slime patch; on phase 1 islands the beaver rolls 3 logs side by side and the stag's stamps march out in a line; on phase 2 the owl's feather homes in and speeds up, the otter's pebble skims twice and the ravens relay feathers through each other; on phase 3 the crocodile's whirlpool pulls and the dragonfly's spark chains; on phase 4 two unicorns stretch a rainbow tether between them, the polar bear brings icicle rain, three crabs mark a triangle that erupts and the hydra's homing venom leaves pools. A kind's own moves (charges, leaps, swoops, burrowing) and its pack's specials are unchanged: the species attack is its ordinary strike, and a melee one is its lunge. Against soundsystems every kind still uses its old attack, so sieges are as they were. A legend (angry, or happy and helping her) throws its kind's attack exaggerated from up to 420 m (`legendOf`, rules/attacks/species.ts): a huge slow bomb, its landing shown from the windup's start (about 5 s), bursting where it lands into its kind's own attack two sizes up with more in it (a single shot becomes three, a lunge a great slam ring), each piece at half its share; a beam kind sweeps one giant beam across its whole reach after a long charge. A happy legend's bombs are guided onto the enraged it throws at; an angry one's at her come down where she stood. The charging legends still charge, and a legend bombarding a soundsystem still throws its old bomb.

**Momentum** (#1303, #1330, 2026-10-27; `rules/momentum.ts`, `creatureMomentum`). A creature chasing her can't turn on a sixpence: its heading swings at most so many degrees a second, less the faster it goes, and it steers for where she was a moment ago, not where she is. Heavy kinds turn 220° a second and lag 0.4 s, most kinds 420° and 0.25 s, nimble and swarming ones 650° and 0.15 s; the boar turns slower still, the cheetah swings wide at speed, and the wolf reads her a little ahead. It holds through the run-in and the wind-up, but **a lunge, once committed, goes where the wind-up aimed**, reaching up to 1.25 times its usual length if the run-in left it short. So sidestepping late beats a heavy kind; nimble ones follow you round. Only chasing her: fights between creatures, legends and marchers move as before. The pause page has sliders for it.

**What attacks look like** (#1305 to #1335; `config/attack-look.json`, `rules/attackLook.ts`; `?look=old` the old red dots and lines). Every attack is drawn in one of **twelve elements**: fire, frost, venom, moonlight, storm, earth, shadow, bloom, water, sand, wood and wind, each with its own four colours for the core, its trail, the impact, bits, the mark it leaves, beams and areas. The element is the attack's own (foxfire is moonlight, a seed or thorn wood, slime venom), else its species', else its biome's (temperate earth, volcanic and hot fire, cold and mountains frost, mangrove water, jungle bloom, badlands sand, cold rainforest wind), else moonlight. Some draw as the thing itself: an acorn, a pinecone, a log, a pebble, a feather, an icicle. They grow with level (young 0.75, adult 1, legend 1.6), and slows, stuns, knockbacks and confusion each have their own mark. **Every wild attack warns the same way**: white marching dashes edged in dark (never red), flashing in its last 0.25 s. `?lookdemo=telegraphs|elements|species` loops them round her to look at.

**Wild flyers swoop** (the owl, the bat and the raven; `movement.json` `swoop`, `rules/movement.ts` `stepSwoop`). Hostile ones rise and circle her out of reach, drawn up in the air over their shadows: 💌s pass under them, and no blow or talk reaches them. Then each telegraphs, with a red ring tightening round its shadow, and dives along a straight line at where she'll be when it gets there (`lead` of her velocity at the moment it lets go). Its aim locks `dodge.a.commit` (0.18 s) before it drops, as a strike's does: a bat's dive reaches her in about a tenth of a second, so the blink that beats it comes as its aim locks. Walking straight on gets her caught; turning or blinking as it lets go beats it. It skims low at the bottom and climbs back up. Only in the dive and at the bottom can it be hit or invited.
- **Owl:** a slow, wide circle 8 m up, then one heavy dive after a 1.5 s telegraph, open for the dive and 2 s at the bottom.
- **Bat:** fast and erratic, 6 m up, with three quick dives a bout, each telegraphed for 0.5 s with a screech, open for 1 s at the bottom.
- **Raven:** climbs to nearly twice its 9 m while it telegraphs, so its shadow grows, then dives hard and throws her back.

Her own flyers, leashed or happy, fight on the ground as before.

**Who lives where.** Every area is peopled from the very start (Ed, 2026-10-07; no growth on a clock any more, and no animal appears out of sight). Each has **two babies** (`population.byRoute.babies` 2, at most `babyCap` 2) and its **hostile swarm**: its young and adults, the ones a wave enrages and sets marching (Ed, 2026-10-08; `population.swarm`). Its strength grows with the area's wave, on **one master curve over waves 1 to 64** (Ed, 2026-10-10, 2026-10-11; `swarm.waves`): wave 64 brings the final swarm, so every island plays the curve from wave 1 as far as it has areas (a 10-area island stops at about 1.5 young), and a bigger map holds there after; and **picking a species never changes the challenge** (Ed's swarm plan, 2026-10-08). Each species is in a **class** (`swarm.classes`): **strong** (bear, boar, elk, stag, badger, ram, beaver: at most **6**, each twice as tough and hard-hitting), **medium** (the rest: at most 12) and **light** (hedgehog, mole, dormouse, newt, toad, snail, salamander, bat, moth, beetle, spider, woodlouse: at most **16**, three-quarters as strong). The challenge is a medium swarm of **1** creature at wave 1, all young, growing to **12** at wave 64 (`swarm.endAverage`, along `swarm.curve` 1.3), by then mostly adults (`swarm.youngShare` 1.0 → 0.2). A swarm's power is its total health times its total damage a second; each area gets the swarm of its species whose power comes nearest, a **mix of young and adults** wherever one is within 10% (`swarm.mixTolerance`). At wave 64 a swarm deals about **105 damage a second**: a soundsystem lasts about 5 s. The witch doesn't come into it: she takes three hits whatever hits her. A 10-area island holds about 15 young and adults all told, a 50-area one about 210, the old map of about 100 areas about 750 (with the master curve; about 540 before). So the areas the party reaches late are the dangerous ones. (`population.growth` is the old per-wave growth: the game no longer reads it, the balance simulators still do.) About **a fifth of the areas** also have a sleeping legend (`legends.share` 0.2; Ed, 2026-10-08: "more like 1/5", half before), with a wild baby of its own kind in its circle. Each lies in **an area of its own** (#1171, 2026-10-26; `legends.areas`): grown out of the island beside an area of its kind, with that area's kind and look, its circle at its heart, and no runestone, soundsystem or stop on the route; the pair counts as one area for the legend's rage and for who attacks and defends it.

**States.** Each animal is in one of these:
- **Wild**: as found. It roams its own area and attacks you on the ground once you're in its area or within its attack's range. It goes for your party animals within 30 m (`combat.aggro`). It ignores happy animals. **The hunt** (Ed, 2026-10-07; `hunt.on`): once you've landed in its area and its watch is over, every one of that area's own awake young and adults hunts you while you're in the area, wherever in it they are, never giving up or napping. **A 💌 wakes the area** (Ed, 2026-10-08; `wildWatch.inviteWakes`): the moment one of your letters lands on any of its wild animals, its watch is over and its young and adults, napping or not, come for you at once by these same rules (so only while you're in the area). Over the treetops there they hold (they can't reach you) and come on again as soon as you land. It stops when it's invited (or enraged by a wave, or knocked down and run off). Leave the area and they lose interest, as before (Ed: "they should lose interest and go home if you leave their area, as they did before"): they give up 30 m past their edge (`combat.leaveArea`) and walk home. Come back while the area still remembers you (`wildWatch.forget`, 20 s after you were last on the ground in it) and they hunt you again at once; later, its watch plays again. Knocked out, they all give up, walk home and roam again, and your next visit starts with the watch (DECISION FOR ED). Babies don't fight, and the legend and its circle's baby keep to their own rules. Any other wild animal that goes for you (one from a neighbouring area, say) gives up if you rise, or once you're 30 m past its area's edge (`combat.leaveArea`), then walks home.
- **Happy**: won over with 💌s. It stays in its own area, defends it against enraged animals, and dances at its soundsystem once there is one. Its sigil lies as a little rune at its feet.
- **Leashed**: yours for good. It follows you or guards a sigil, and fights wild and enraged animals.
- **Enraged**: a wave put a soundsystem in its area. It has red eyes, besieges, attacks you and happy and leashed animals, **can't be invited and blocks 💌s**. Babies, happy and leashed animals are never enraged.
- **No health bars: the 💌 ring is the one measure** (Ed, 2026-10-28; `rules/orbit.ts`). A creature's ring has a slot for each 💌 its rank takes (`invites.hits`). Your 💌s fill slots; so does a blow from one of your animals, as an orbiting **❤️**, a harder blow more of them (a blow's damage over the creature's old health: it takes as many blows to fill as it used to take to knock down). Hearts fade exactly as 💌s do, in **one queue** with them (Ed, 2026-10-28: "the same speed as 💌s fade ... in the same fade 'queue'"): the slots fade one at a time, newest first (the ring's top slot falls first, as 💌s always have), whichever kind each is, 4 s after the last to join (`states.affection.drainDelay`) and then **2% of a full ring a second** (`invites.drain` 0.02; Ed, 2026-10-28, from 3%: a full ring empties in about 50 s). One of yours or a happy one hit by its enemies takes **💧s** instead, the same way, and they never fade: only a berry (one berry takes them all, and counts toward growing up only when it had none) or a full heal (befriended, picked up, the party's end) clears them; a happy legend's mending and the Nursing hat take them off slowly. Her stack shows a creature's berries as berry-coloured dots right of its sigil and its 💧s as blue dots left of it.
- **Stunned**: all its slots full, a wild animal is **stunned** for **20 s** (`combat.daze`): lying still, nothing attacking it. **Your next 💌 converts it** (the 💌 that fills the last slot only stuns). Left alone, it gets up wild. A hostile one (enraged, besieging) can't be converted: stunned, it **runs away** for good, a big 💧 over it. One of yours or a happy one whose slots fill with 💧s runs away too, for good; one of yours is lost for the run, its sigil gone. Running away, they go at a baby's run (`leash.babies.farSpeed`). A wild area legend can be stunned (not invited): it sinks back to sleep.

No animal ever fights its own kind. Nobody dies: they "run off".

**Other behaviour**:
- Idle wild animals sometimes nap: 30% chance at each pause, for 20–60 s (`naps`). Landing on the ground in their area wakes them, with a 1.2 s yawn first. **Letters wake them too** (Ed, 2026-10-11): a 💌 that reaches a napping wild animal rouses it at once, wherever you are (the next area over, or the treetops' edge), and lands as an ordinary invitation, waking its area as any 💌 does. (Before, letters flew straight through a sleeper until you landed in its area.) The party's-over sleep at the end of a run is untouched.
- Curious kinds' babies come up to look at you, and skittish ones keep about 12 m off (`notice`).

**Pack combat (on by default since 2026-10-20, Ed: "Turn pack combat on by default")** (Ed, 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-19; `docs/backlog/combat.md` build step 1; `rules/combat/packs.ts`, the kinds as data, each creature's `pack` in its `art/creatures/<id>/creature.js`, the rest in `config/packs.json`). The pause page's *Pack combat* toggle (the tuning's `combat.packs.on`, or `?set=combat.packs.on=false`) turns it off for testing; with it off, fights go exactly as described above. So far it covers home's ten kinds (hedgehog, squirrel, hare, fox, marten, stoat, mole, snail, dormouse, badger) and Phase 1's eleven (wolf, boar, bat, raven, newt, moth, heron, beetle, woodlouse, otter, toad; Echo, 2026-10-19); a kind with no `pack` fights as before. With it on, a pack (one kind, one side, one target, young and adults) fights in phases (not at a soundsystem or speaker, which doesn't fight back: there each fights as before, its own attack at its full rate; Ed, 2026-10-20):
- **Approach.** It closes in as before, till one of it is within 6 m of its reach (`limits.engage`).
- **The pack tell.** Every member glows white and its call plays (a placeholder: white motes and a ring) for its rhythm's `tell` (0.5 s tight, 0.75 s steady, 1 s slow).
- **The combo.** Roles come from position, every step: the nearest healthy member is the **lunger** (its melee, a lane), the one behind her (else the furthest) throws the **special**, the rest are **shooters** (a magic bolt in its kind's own reach; a young one fires one small, slow bolt, an adult a volley of up to 3). The lunger goes first; the shots go as it commits, **aimed where a dodge would go** (just after a blink, where it put her; else ahead of her by half a blink); the special lands last, a gap later, and only once one of them has struck. Each member strikes once a combo. At most its kind's **pressure** may wind up or strike at her at once (1 for the badger, 2 for the small fast kinds, 3 for the light swarms), and never more than one lane and two lines; and, as for every creature, no more than the dodge's tokens allow (`dodge.a`: one at a time), so its strikes at her come one after another, the combo running on while its members wait their turn (Ed, 2026-10-20). Between creatures only the pressure counts.
- **The lull.** It falls back by its kind's **retreat pattern** (below) for its rhythm's lull, longer for a bigger pack (`perMember`) and for a young one (`young.lull`): 2.6 s for five of a tight kind, about 4.8 s for five slow ones. It strikes nothing then. Her own pack never retreats: it holds where it is for `hers.hold` (1.2 s) and goes again.
- **Again**, from the tell, until the pack is beaten, invited or she's gone.

**Kinds keep their own moves in a pack** (Ed, 2026-10-20: "Kinds keep their movement.json moves within pack combat: charges, dives, burrowing, screeches, slime and the wolves surrounding her"). A member's strike, its turn come, is its kind's own move (`config/movement.json`) where it has one, taking the pack's turn and pressure and a dodge token like any strike at her, so they still come one at a time: a charger (the boar, the hedgehog, the woodlouse, the heron) **charges** as its lunge, waiting its turn where its charge sets off from; the toad **leaps** and slams down; the mole **burrows** in and comes up striking (its strike, at the surface, takes the token); a wild bat or raven **circles aloft** through the tell and the lull and **dives** as its strike (a bout's first dive on its pack's turn, a lane, the rest of its bout each a token as before). Its pack engages from that move's reach. The bat's melee is its own **screech**, a pulse all round it (hers, on the ground; a wild one dives). A kiting kind (the raven) strikes with its shot, never a lunge, keeping its distance. The snail's **slime trail** runs on as it goes, besides its wall. And its kind's **tactic** places it: the wolves **surround** her (or pincer her), the foxes and martens go round to her back, a raven volley keeps its line and fires on the beat; only the member whose lunge it is goes straight in. The stampede, at her, makes it every member's turn to charge for 4 s, one after another as the tokens free (her pack's on the wild: all at once). (Hit and run is the pack's own lull.)

**Answers to what she does:** a 💌 reaching one of them brings its special onto her at once, or, for a covering kind, a packmate digs in beside the one she's inviting; running away brings the cut-off (`dodge.c`, every pack kind); one of her party animals going for a member splits the pack, the half nearest it turning on it. **Hurt members** (under half health) leave the combo and keep 18 m off while a healthy packmate fights; a lone one, or a pack all hurt, fights on, so every fight comes to an end (Ed, 2026-10-20); **a loss** (one knocked down, invited or run off) sends the rest into a scattering lull at once.

## 6. Inviting with 💌s

- **Throwing.** On the ground, hold fire to throw one spinning 💌 every **0.55 s** (`invites.cooldown`, `invites.burst` 1). It flies at **31.2 m/s** for **33 m** (`invites.speed`, `invites.range`). It bends gently towards an invitable animal ahead (within 10 m and 30° of straight ahead, `invites.homingRange`, `invites.homingCone`). Trees never stop it.
- **What it hits.** It lands on the first wild (or dazed) animal in its path. **Enraged animals and legends stop it dead.** Your own and happy animals let it pass.
- **The meter.** Each 💌 that lands adds one to the animal's ring. A full ring takes **4 hits for a baby, 7 for a young, 14 for an adult** (`invites.hits`; a legend's 36). Every hit counts, with no per-animal cooldown, so how fast you invite is just how fast you fire. That's roughly 2 s, 4 s and 8 s of steady hits. If you stop, the ring starts draining 4 s after the last hit at 2% of a full ring a second (`invites.drain`; `config/states.json` `affection.drainDelay`), one slot at a time, newest first, ❤️s and 💌s alike.
- **Winning over.** A full ring makes a wild animal **happy** (hearts), and its **rune** pops out at its feet straight away. Stand within 5.6 m of it (`leash.runeRadius`) and press **E** to leash it. Within 8 m it even trots over to you (`leash.runePull`).
- **Getting hit doesn't set an invite back.** The hit is the cost.
- **Seen as the animals are** (Ed, 2026-10-19). A 💌 in flight, one resting on the ground and an animal's ring are hidden by whatever stands in front of them, as the animal is: trunks, crowns, rocks and scenery, and they show through her see-through cut-away where an animal does. A ring goes exactly when its animal does. Smoke veils them as it veils an animal. Her speech bubbles, the animals' replies and the hearts stay on top. (`render/occlusion.ts`; `?occlusion=off` draws them over everything, as before.)

## 7. Sigils, leashing, posse

- **The stack.** Every leashed animal's sigil floats above her head, newest at the bottom.
- **Rank colours** (#1291, #1300, #1334, 2026-10-27; `render/leash/ranks.ts`, `leash.ranks`). Every sigil, in her stack, put down, or a happy animal's rune, is a bare glyph in its **rank's colour**: a baby's turquoise, a young's purple, an adult's red, a legend's yellow. Embers rise off it, more and higher by rank (3, 5, 8, 12 of them). A put-down glyph is 5 m across. A young one in her stack shows its berries as two rows of dots, the eaten ones bright, all pulsing once it's ready to grow.
- **Runestones by rank.** Each area's runestone glows in the colour of the **highest rank living in its area now** (counting a legend at home there; not hers on her leash, nor those gone, fleeing, travelling or dancing elsewhere), so you can read from afar what an area holds. An empty area's stone is unlit grey.
- **Placing.** On the ground, **E puts the bottom sigil down** as a glowing rune (a **leash point**), and **E on one of yours picks it back up**. Q moves the bottom sigil to the top. Sigils go down on a **world-fixed hex grid**, 6 m between neighbouring cells (`leash.hex.size`; Ed, 2026-10-11: so a cluster of them reads cleanly; 4 m before #1334, which `?sigils=old` brings back with the old sigils). The cell nearest her decides: if one of her sigils is in it, the press picks it up; if it's free, the bottom sigil goes down in its middle. Nothing fizzles. Cells under a runestone or soundsystem (`leash.hex.stone`) or one of home's speakers (`leash.hex.speaker`) are skipped: the sigil goes down on the nearest free cell that isn't, within two cells, or not at all. Relic sigils and her hat still go down where she stands. A parked animal rests **north** of its sigil, and a happy animal's rune lies **south** of it, by its size: the sigil's top just below its feet (its body radius, half the sigil with its backing, and `leash.runeGap`, 0.5 m: `rules/runeOffset.ts`; Ed, 2026-10-13), so the whole sigil stays in sight.
  - When several things are under her, she picks up in this order: her hat, then her own sigil, then a relic's sigil, then a happy animal's rune.
- **Leash length.** An animal roams within **8 m** of its leash point (`leash.length`) and runs back at its own pace if it falls behind. It never teleports.
- **Guarding.** Parked animals guard their sigil, taking on any wild or enraged animal of another kind within **40 m** (`guard.radius`). One parked with its sigil in the **home area** guards the whole home area instead: any wild or enraged animal anywhere in it, and nothing outside it.
- **Posse and travelling** (`config/travel.json`). A party animal within **60 m** of her while she's on the ground, or near its sigil, is in her **posse**: it fights. Anywhere else it's **travelling**. A traveller is invisible to enemies, fights nothing and eats nothing, and walks round area edges rather than through their middles. While she's in the treetops, everything following her is travelling.

## 8. Legends

**Asleep in their circles.** A legend lies half sunk in its own small clearing, mossed over like a boulder. The clearing is about 9 m across, bigger for big species (`legendClearing`), ringed with the area's tallest trees. **Waves don't wake legends.**

**Dreams and quests** (Ed, 2026-10-18: "Legends accept any level animal for their quest, at which point they fight for you and unlock their island"). Stand within 60 m on the ground (`dreams.range`) and you see what a sleeping legend dreams of: one other **kind**, any age (its bare sigil, no age frame). The dream points **deeper in**: a kind that lives in an area **later on the route** than the legend's own, within 6 areas (`legends.questCap`; `legends.questLater`). If none of those is in reach, it's the kind of the nearest later area; a legend with no later area dreams of any kind. The gamble is how hard that later area is to reach and fight through.
- **The early easy quest** is the exception: one legend among the first three areas the waves reach dreams of the kind of another of those three (`legends.earlyQuest`).
- **Put down the sigil of an animal of that kind, any age, inside its circle**, while the legend sleeps (or is restless), and the quest is done **on the spot**, wherever the animal is; it walks over to its sigil afterwards. **Only putting the sigil down does it** (Ed, 2026-10-19: "The reward should be given when the sigil is placed, not when the animal enters the circle"): one of yours of that kind walking into the circle, following you or parked, does nothing. The animal stays yours.
- **The legend wakes happy at once and fights for you** (below), and **its island is found tonight**. The island is kept on the sea chart **only if the night is won** (Ed, 2026-10-18: "The island is only kept if the night is won"); lose the night and tonight's islands are not kept.
- **No boon** (Ed, 2026-10-18: "Finishing quests no longer gives boons").
- **The circle's panel** (Ed, 2026-10-19: short and plain, one idea a line, the action said exactly; `rules/legendCircle.ts` `circleLines`):
  - sleeping: "A sleeping legend: the hare." / "Put down [sigil] a fox's sigil, any age, inside this circle." / "It will wake, fight for you and show you its island." / "Leave none of its kind in its area and it grows restless.";
  - restless: "A restless legend: the hare.", the same ask, then "Bring one of its kind back to its area to calm it.";
  - angry: "An angry legend: the hare!" / "Wear it down, or bring one of its kind back to its area, and it will settle.";
  - happy: "Your ally: the hare." / "It fights for you and guards its area." / (its quest done) "Its island is found: win the night to keep it.";
  - a wrong kind's sigil put down inside: "Not this one: it wants a fox's sigil, and you put down an elk's.";
  - once the party's over: "A sleeping legend: the hare." / "The party's over: it sleeps on."
- If the area's wave hasn't come yet, the ley line also moves on from it early, and the area counts as **friendly**.
- Drop the sigil just outside the circle and the circle flashes as a hint.
- **Once the party's over, no legend takes a quest** (Ed, 2026-10-18: permanent unlocks only happen while the party is on): its dream closes and the panel says it sleeps on.

**Restless and angry.**
- Each legend checks every **5 s** whether any of its own kind is in its area. That counts any state: wild, happy, your parked ones, babies.
- With none, it grows **restless** (a nightmare bubble, from sad to furious). After **60 s** of that it turns **angry** (`config/legends.json` `angryAfter`).
- An angry legend shoots or lobs at you and your posse on the ground from up to **420 m** (`legends.json` `attack.range`), a volley every 15 s. Six species charge instead: boar, elk, stag, ram, hedgehog and woodlouse. They take a long, curving run, then walk home.
- With no witch in reach, an angry legend **bombards the nearest standing soundsystem** within 420 m, at **150 damage a hit** (`legends.bombard`).
- It **calms back to sleep as soon as one of its kind is back** in its area. Worn down in a fight, it also goes back to sleep. Either way it walks home to its spot first.
- (A "stomp" variant, where a legend turning angry flattens its own area's soundsystem, exists but is switched off: `legends.stomp.on` false.)

**Relics.** Giant half-buried bottles lie on each map, **one per 10 areas** the waves reach, at least one (`legends.json` `relics.perAreas`; Ed, 2026-10-11): one just inside the edge of home, the rest scattered far out, at least 150 m apart. Each is one of the six bottles, mirrored or not, in one of six colours (its glass and glowing liquid; `relics.colours`): **72 kinds** (Ed, 2026-10-11). A map never holds the same bottle in the same colour twice, mirrored or not, while there are looks to spare (36). You can spot their glint from the treetops only through gaps in the canopy.
- **A flask is a growth spurt** (Ed, 2026-10-18: "All flasks give growth spurt (all creatures following you become adults)"). Stand on its sigil and press E to **drink it on the spot**: every animal **following you** (on your stack, not parked at a sigil) younger than an adult becomes an **adult at once**, with the evolving's flash and sparkle burst on each. With nothing following you to grow it does nothing (a refusal's sound) and the flask stays where it lies. It's not carried, and it no longer wakes a legend.

**Happy legends.** A legend is made happy by doing its quest. It gives **no buff** (Ed, 2026-10-18). It defends its area, shooting enraged animals from up to **420 m** (`legends.happyRange`), and heals to full over **120 s** (`legends.json` `healTime`).
- **The party-legend Easter egg** (`legends.partyEgg` on): 100 💌s (`legends.partyHits`) turn a happy legend into a **party legend**. You can pick up its rune, but it never moves, and it pins you within 6.8 m of it (`legends.partyReach`). Totally useless, as intended.

**Buffs**: none. Legend buffs were designed 5 Oct (#87), switched off 18 and 24 Oct, and removed 28 Oct.

**Slowed time in a calm circle.** While you stand on the ground inside the circle of a **sleeping or restless** legend, the **whole world slows to a tenth of its speed** (`legendCircle.slow.scale` 0.1, easing over 0.5 s). That includes the wave countdown, the music, every animal and every siege. You keep full speed.
- No attack reaches you in there from outside.
- **Enraged animals can't enter** a calm circle at all.
- A 💌 that leaves the circle from inside vanishes.

## 9. Berries and evolving

- **Where berries grow.** Each area has berry bushes in small patches of 4 to 7 bushes, with about 11–17 berries per area at this area size (`berries.perArea` [5, 8] and `bushesPerArea` 10, scaled up for 168 m areas; halved on 2026-10-27).
- **Who eats them.** Your leashed young (never wild ones, never legends) nip off to eat a berry within 4.5 m of their path or their sigil (`berries.detour`), as long as it stays inside their leash. A sigil dropped in a patch makes a feeding spot. **Babies** eat only when their sigil is put down with a berry within 10 m (`berries.babyReach`), and a young one following her stops **one berry short** of adult: it eats the last only at a sigil she's put down (#1274).
- **Regrowing.** An eaten berry regrows at once on a random free bush somewhere else on the map, so the total never changes.
- **Evolving.** An animal evolves once it has eaten enough, on the music's next bar line: **1 berry from baby to young** (`berries.babyCost`), **then 3, 4 or 8 from young to adult** for a light, medium or strong kind (#1290; `berries.cost`: the fighting value it gains over 7.25, so a berry buys the same strength whatever the kind). **Evolving stops at adult.**
- **Healing.** A berry also heals a hurt animal to full, and hurt adults still go looking for them.

## 10. Music, the beat and the ley lines

- **The track.** One generated, synthesised track (`config/music-style.json`), seeded per run. **It's rendered ahead** (#1275 to #1292, 2026-10-27; `platform/audio/musicAhead.ts`): a bar at a time, off the page's thread, queued about 6 s ahead through the same mix, so a hitch in the game no longer cuts the beat. The hooks (a siege, a party area, an angry legend near) and a legend's layer stay live so they answer at once, and the whole track goes live while the world is slowed (the treehouse, a legend's circle) or the Music Lab is open. A change (a wave, a knockdown) drops the bars not yet heard and draws them again. After a stall the game's beat catches up to the music's, so the dancefloor stays in time. `?music=live` plays it all live, as before.
  - During the boot it plays the intro, filling out as each speaker turns on.
  - Each wave brings the next step of the arc: Forest, Clearing, Lanterns, Night drive, Industrial, Breakbeat, Frenzy, Phonk, then Finale for every wave after that.
  - The tempo climbs from **120 to 140 bpm** and eases in over a few bars. A rising build leads into each wave.
  - Being knocked out plays a breakdown, and a siege nearby adds its own siege layers.
  - The music is full within 25 m of a playing soundsystem and muffled further off (`music.nearDist`, `music.farDist`).
  - In a sleeping legend's circle it goes muffled and slow, and the legend's own layer comes in.
- **The beat** drives the dancing animals, the lasers, the dancefloor tiles and the evolutions. The dancefloor gets brighter as more areas join the party: level steps at 3, 8 and 15 areas (`dancefloor.levels`).
- **The ley line** is a glowing line on the ground. It runs from the treehouse through every runestone, in the order the waves will reach them: the whole route, all the time. None of it shows before the boot's pulse, going round the home ring, reaches the point where the line leaves the ring (about a third of the way round). There it branches off, an extension of the boot's ring line, and its tip runs out to the first stone, reaching it as the boot ends. After that it grows on, reaching the third stone as wave 1 lands, then 3 links per wave (`leyLines.reveal`). A pulse runs along the current stretch with the wave countdown. A stone counts as reached when its wave comes, its area is cleared or its quest is done, whichever is first. Only the next stone gets an on-screen pointer.

The dream ends by itself or at any key, and her alarm rings. Nothing of a loan is saved till then (`app/loan.ts`), so a reload mid-night keeps nothing. Its outline is written in the stars, seen through the telescope in its direction from the treehouse. With everything found, there's no find.
- **Party witches**: one extra witch flies in to dance on the dancefloor for every soundsystem playing.
- **Home's relic**: one relic always lies just inside the edge of home, so a new player stumbles on it.
- **Friendly babies**: a soundsystem turns its area's babies happy, including the baby in the legend's circle, which then dances inside the circle.
- **Animal strength**: a species' strength comes from its swarm class: strong ×2, medium ×1, light ×0.75 (`population.swarm.classes`; `combat.json` `strength.species` is empty, for one-off exceptions).
- **The quality ladder** (#1286 to #1298, 2026-10-27; `rules/ladder.ts`, `app/ladder.ts`). Each second the game measures how long its frames take on the GPU against a 60 fps budget. After 3 slow seconds in a row it steps down a rung, giving up the least visible thing first: the far forest edge dithered, then 10% fewer trees, tall trees squeezed, leaves still and no trunk shadows, the cut-away's crowns solid, 20% fewer trees, a steady 30 fps, and last chunkier pixels. After 10 roomy seconds it climbs back, but only to a rung it knows it can afford, and three reversals in a minute stop the climbing for the night. Some rungs change at once and some at the next load. It remembers the rung for each GPU. The pause page shows the rung and lets you pin one or turn it off; `?ladder=off|<n>|auto`.
- **The Fight Arena** (#1304, #1307, #1311, 2026-10-27; `app/fightArena.ts`, `ui/fightArena.ts`). The pause page's **⚔ Fight arena** opens, in a new tab, her alone against a wild pack in the home clearing: no waves, no spell. The seed rolls the pack (1 in 8 a lone legend; otherwise one kind, or 1 in 3 two, 2 to 6 of each, young or adult, 8 at most), and the pack and seed go in the link (`?fight=<seed>&pack=wolf*4@2,...`), so a link replays the fight. Its panel: a new random pack or one typed in, fight again (J), copy the link; speed at 1×, ½×, ¼×, paused or a frame at a time; and each telegraph's time to impact, red under the 0.45 s floor. Its **attack editor** is a form made from the grammar's schema, plus the raw JSON: set it and the pack fights with it at once, any warning the floor lengthened shown. ✎ loads a kind's own attack to edit, Own puts them back, the attack rides in the link (`?attack=`), and a library keeps named and favourite attacks in this browser.
- **Link switches**: every `?name=value` the game reads is in `docs/LINK-SWITCHES.md`, written from `config/link-switches.json`.
