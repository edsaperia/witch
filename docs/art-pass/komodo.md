# Komodo — skeleton target

Target: Komodo dragon, not crocodile or generic iguana. Adult and patterned
juvenile level0, toward/away, stationary/walking. [Method](l7-method.md).
[Generated plate](https://github.com/edsaperia/witch/blob/claude/ed-shots/previews/art-pass/komodo/refs-l7-v1.png).

## Identifying features

1. Powerful long low trunk and substantial belly with muscular hip/shoulder roots.
2. Distinct long monitor neck leading to a low wedge-shaped head.
3. Long thick-rooted tapering tail, broadly comparable to remaining body length.
4. Four lateral legs with long spread clawed toes, not soft mammal feet.
5. Rough granular scales; no crocodilian dorsal scute armour.
6. Loose throat folds rather than a huge iguana sail.
7. Juvenile slimmer, with yellowish spots/bands, less throat and belly mass.

## Body plan

| Part | Adult / B | Level0 direction |
|---|---|---|
| Head | .18–.24 | shorter muzzle, proportionally larger skull |
| Neck | .20–.28 | slender but distinct |
| Leg chain | .35–.50 | reduce muscle, preserve long digits |
| Tail | 1.00–1.35 | long and slender, not short adult stub |
| Trunk width / depth | .27–.38 / .22–.32 | narrower, less heavy abdomen |

Bulk belongs in the torso, proximal limbs and tail root, with a recognisable
neck constriction behind the skull. Walking has lateral flex and outward limbs;
the neck can lift without making the animal rear upright. Rump view should
read heavy root tapering to a long tail, not a crocodile's thin vertical blade.

## Comparison and image inspection

Compared [Ragunan Zoo photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Komodo_dragon_Varanus_komodoensis_Ragunan_Zoo_2.JPG)
and [Commons gallery](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Varanus_komodoensis).
The photograph's neck is clearly distinct and limbs spread from a low belly.
Generated adults read as monitors, but necks are short/thick in the rear view
and the head is slightly blunt; use the photo to retain length. Do not copy
the generator's highly regular wrinkle pattern as anatomy. The juvenile row
is slimmer and patterned, without oversized cartoon eyes; its exact band count
is invented. Forked yellow tongue is a valid optional feature per
[Smithsonian](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/komodo-dragon), but is not needed
for every frame. Eight whole animals present; walk poses are not gait proof.

## Ranked graph handoff

1. **Proportions:** independent long neck, long low muzzle and heavy adult body;
   use existing neck/reptile-head facilities before adding new primitives.
2. **Limbs:** sprawling monitor stance with long articulated clawed digits and
   support, not merely larger three-claw paw decorations.
3. **Age:** narrow patterned juvenile versus mature throat folds and belly;
   smooth regional tissue and pigment changes must not change species identity.

**MISSING GRAPH FEATURES:** reviewed monitor-sprawl/long-digit family, throat
fold/dewlap surface control; optional forked-tongue articulation. Neck, reptile
head, markings and generic claws already exist. No dorsal crest required.

torso and tail; (4) splayed long-clawed feet; (5) paired very fine sensory
filaments only at tongue tips; (6) healed scars and weathered throat folds.

proximal limb and throat mass1.1–1.2× and tongue reach up to.20B in display;
leave a fine tail taper. These are fantasy display targets, not animal measurements.
