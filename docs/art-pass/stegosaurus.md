## Identifying features

1. Tiny low head with blunt beak, not a giant reptile muzzle.
2. Long but slender forward neck, much smaller in mass than the torso.
3. Deep barrel trunk, hips distinctly higher than shoulders.
4. Short stout forelimbs and longer weight-bearing hindlimbs.
5. Two staggered rows of thin upright irregular plates, largest around rear trunk.
6. Long muscular tail with four terminal-region spikes in two lateral pairs.
7. Juvenile has reduced plates and less barrel mass; exact hatchling proportions
   and colour are reconstruction choices, not known measurements.

## Comparison and image inspection

Visually compared [museum skeleton photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stegosaurus_Sarah_in_the_Natural_History_Museum.jpg)
and [skull photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stegosaurus_skull_Senckenberg.jpg).
NHM's [anatomy account](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dino-directory/stegosaurus.html)
supports the tiny head and alternating skin-mounted plates; its
[Sophie specimen account](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/world_s-most-complete-stegosaurus-goes-on-show.html)
records 19 plates and four spikes. Do not turn the generator's visible plate
count into a new biological count or confuse overlapping far spikes with extras.
The generated plate preserves tiny head/high hips/barrel and whole tail, but
tail looks short and upturned in some cells, and plate rows are too symmetrical
in places. Fossil skeleton and checklist override these. Juvenile is explicitly
a concept, not photoreal evidence of a known hatchling. Eight whole animals
present; the stride is a plausible pose, not validated dinosaur gait.
