# Snake (jungle python) — skeleton target

The game record is explicitly a jungle python (`GENOME_SNAKE`), no longer a
grass snake. Target uses **green tree python** anatomy and colours; it is an
explicit reference anchor for the stylised jungle animal, not a taxonomy change.
Adult green / level0 yellow juvenile, both headings, resting/slithering.
[Method](l7-method.md). [Generated plate](https://github.com/edsaperia/witch/blob/claude/ed-shots/previews/art-pass/snake/refs-l7-v1.png).

## Identifying features

1. Long continuous muscular body; zero external limbs or shoulder masses.
2. Broad python head separated by a narrower neck, without cobra hood.
3. Smoothly changing girth, narrowing into a fine prehensile tail.
4. Round-to-slightly-angular cross-section and flat ventral contact surface.
5. Irregular white/yellow flecks on green adult, pale belly; no dorsal scute ridge.
6. Loose coils at rest versus extended S-curve while moving.
7. Juvenile yellow or red-brown in nature; use yellow reference, not mandatory
   green miniature. No rattle at any age.

## Body plan

| Part | Adult / B (uncoiled snout-to-vent) | Level0 direction |
|---|---|---|
| Head | .035–.055 | larger relative share, still snake skull |
| Narrow neck region | .06–.10 | narrow behind head, no separate mammal neck |
| Legs | 0 | 0 |
| Tail beyond vent | .15–.22 | retain taper and prehensile tip |
| Maximum diameter | .035–.055 | leaner, continuous along centreline |

Mass is distributed through the midbody, not a central ball with a cord tail.
Avoid pinching every curve into bead segments. A pose bends the existing body's
centreline, preserving its arc length and surface; moving S-curves are not
whole-body translations of one rigid coil. The generation is a floor study to
expose the body; the real animal often rests draped over a branch.

## Comparison and image inspection

Compared [Green Tree Python.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Green_Tree_Python.jpg),
[GreenTreePython2010.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:GreenTreePython2010.jpg)
and [gallery](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Morelia_viridis). Real photos
show tightly supported coils and a smaller, smoother head/neck transition than
the generated toward view. Generation exaggerates facial labial pits and
slightly angular brow; do not build a crocodile head. Adult flecks and yellow
juvenile are useful target cues. Juvenile colour/prehensile tail are supported
by [Smithsonian](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/green-tree-python).
All eight whole continuous snakes present; no limbs or rattle. Generated coils
do not prove self-contact, surface intersections, sidewinding or valid gait.

## Ranked graph handoff

1. **Continuous body:** length-preserving deformable centreline, continuous skin
   and controlled cross-sections with ground/coiling contact.
2. **Taper:** unbroken neck-to-midbody-to-tail mass falloff, not a quadruped
   torso with legs hidden. A serpent prior exists, but is not this validation.
3. **Head/age:** broad small python head, fine tail, pigment ontogeny; reuse
   reptile head/markings before new head dispatch.

**MISSING GRAPH FEATURES:** reviewed serpent locomotion/coiling with self-contact
and length invariants, ventral contact profile and prehensile tip pose. A serpent
body prior and tail chain already exist. No ears, horns, limbs or rattle.

## Legend concepts — proposed pick A, Ed to judge

[Three concepts, both headings](https://github.com/edsaperia/witch/blob/claude/ed-shots/previews/art-pass/snake/legends-l7-v1.png).
Jungle understory: leaf litter/moss, ferns, flowering shrubs, mossy ironwoods,
liana-hung vinewoods and bamboo (`art/areas.js` text). Lianas suggest continuous
curves, not permission to give the animal branches or wings.

| Concept | Animal nature and age | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| A: slough of epochs | A luminous hollow shed sleeve trails an old scarred python | **Proposed pick:** renewal and age are both visible, rooted in shedding |
| B: coil archivist | Great tower of coils with pale old scars and a tail offering | Powerful but mostly size; overlaps ordinary resting pose |
| C: canopy constellation | Dorsal flecks become tiny star points | Fits natural flecks, but celestial decoration is easier to make generic |

Checklist: (1) one continuous limbless python; (2) broad small head/narrow neck;
(3) muscular midbody; (4) fine tail; (5) ONE hollow thin old-skin sleeve, no
second ghost animal; (6) healed body scars and muted old pigment beneath it.
Signature: **luminous sloughed skin**. Keep B ratios; allow midbody thickness
1.15–1.3× adult and a display sleeve. The sleeve is a separate surface, not
a second spine or opaque armour. Do not add venom fangs/cobra hood to this python.

Additional graph needs: skin-shell/sleeve attachment with an open edge and
controlled separation, transparent/emissive surface support and bounded
deformation through the pose. This is different from salamander's living regrowth.
