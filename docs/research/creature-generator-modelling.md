# Sculpt masses, pin features, judge in pairs

Checked against primary sources, the earlier report's diagnosis holds up. **Species identity has to come from deliberately non-uniform masses and a few crisp, authored, exaggerated features, with the smooth blend acting only as glue. The AI art director should compare candidates in pairs and revert to the best so far, never hand out absolute scores.** Several specifics change, though, and one of them is a live bug risk. The smooth-minimum constant `k` has two incompatible conventions. In the classic form (Media Molecule's soft-min, printed verbatim in Evans's SIGGRAPH 2015 slides), `k` is the width of the blend band and the surface bulges by at most k/4. In the newer "normalised" forms, `k` is the bulge itself. The earlier blend-radius table is therefore correct as band widths but **4× too large if fed to a normalised quadratic smin**. Numerical checks also explain the bat and hydra failures. Inflation accumulates: twelve overlapping primitives in one blend group bulge by about 3k, which erases notches and swells small features. Other corrections: Dreams sculpts with flat ordered edit lists, not trees. The ellipsoid SDF overstates interior depth by up to 2.7×. The "per-vertebra radius" explanation of Spore's bellies was an unverified inference, although Spore's bean-first body is now confirmed. Riot never added a ponytail to Lee Sin. Dead Cells never claims final-size renders or outlines, and it openly accepted flickering, low-detail frames. Five critic-evidence numbers were misdescribed. PanTHERIA is CC BY 4.0 (with citation), not CC0. The rest of this report turns the verified material into rules the team can apply directly: a part-graph structure, exact blend formulas with a pixel-based unit system, per-species recipes, crisp-feature and fur rules, bake rules, and a fully specified art-director loop. Every item is tagged by evidence status.

## Start from a bean, a part tree and ordered edit lists

The verified sources agree on a structure that differs subtly from "one spine with a global smooth union". Spore's creature editor began with a whole-body mass, not a spine. Art director Ocean Quigley told Gingold that "artists traditionally start creating characters out of bean shapes", so he "created a new tool using a 3D bean shape as its basis" and later added pullable points that "looked like vertebrae, which also helped the animation system" **[V]** ([Gingold](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gingold-talks-i-spore-i-s-magic-crayon-approach)). The practical consequence **[I]**: each family's default torso is a bean or ellipsoid mass sized by the family template, and the spine nodes bend and locally resize it. A chain of bone-width capsules is the wrong default, and it is the most likely root of the lean-dog bear.

Infinigen (CVPR 2023) is the closest published match to the team's body graph, and its design decisions are verified from the paper **[V]** ([Raistrick et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09310)). Its genome is "a tree data-structure … Nodes contain part parameters, and edges specify part attachment". Each edge carries a socket coordinate **(u, v, r)**: travel u of the way along the parent's skeleton, raycast at angle 360°·v, and use r to "interpolate between the found surface point, and the corresponding skeleton point". That third number, **mount depth**, is what lets ears sit *on* a skull and limbs sink *into* a torso without a large blend. Infinigen's key negative finding explains part of the first attempt's failure. "Adding Gaussian noise to handle locations produces lumpy, unrealistic creatures", so it instead randomises an N×3 array of centre-line radii and angles, with profile shapes arranged along it. Species priors come from "a random convex combination of all values of a certain category", fitted to reference photos of wolves, tigers, cows, goats and others. Missing parts interpolate by scaling to zero, "which results in small vestigial arms or tails". Its parameter lists are a ready-made checklist. Quadruped body: start/end radius, ribcage proportions, flank proportions, spine coefficients, aspect ratio. Carnivore head: snout proportions plus lip, jaw and forehead muscle coefficients. Ear: start/end radius, depth, thickness, curl. Tail: start/end radius, curl, aspect ratio. Detail shapes come from "several tubes along their length to create muscles".

Dreams supplies the inner layer **[V]** ([Evans 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/AlexEvans_SIGGRAPH-2015-sml.pdf)). Sculptures are flat ordered lists of 1 to 100,000 "edits": add, subtract or colour, optionally soft-blended. Domain deformation and non-local effects were excluded on purpose "(much to the chagrin of z-brush experienced artists)" so that evaluation stays local and cullable. Combining the three gives the recommended architecture **[I]**. Each part holds an ordered edit list, where each edit carries an op (add, subtract, colour), a primitive and a blend band. A part tree with Infinigen-style (u, v, r) sockets composes the parts with hard or small-band unions. This replaces the earlier report's "BlobTree" with the shipped pattern and keeps its one essential rule: **features join only their parent**.

Two more verified sources fix where the masses go. Blender's Skin modifier confirms per-vertex radii and adds a useful detail: radii are per-axis, since "Equalize Radii" makes them "equal on each axis". So an elliptical (rx, ry) cross-section per spine node is standard practice **[V]** ([Blender Manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/skin.html)). The same page warns that branch points (three or more edges) are relaxed "with the side effect of shrinking it". The rule that follows **[I]** is that every branch node gets an explicit mass: shoulder and hip ellipsoids on quadrupeds, and a collar where hydra necks leave the body.

Spore's design-level lessons, now verified from Gingold's talk, give the part tree its semantics. Parts are curated "words, not letters": magnetic poetry works because "the content is carefully crafted". Semantic types let the system reason ("If we know it's a chimney, we can have smoke coming out of it"). Legs were "the last part of the editor to finish—and the hardest", solved with "a bunch of functions that were our fuzzy interpretation of what looks good". Symmetry was mandatory, and the benchmark was "make something cool in three clicks" ([Gingold](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gingold-talks-i-spore-i-s-magic-crayon-approach)). Translated into this generator **[I]**, those lessons become four rules. **Tag every socket and part** (head, neck, eye, mouth, ear, horn, wing, foot, tail tip), and let the tags drive blend rules, bake rules and animation: eyes become decals, ears never blend, mouths get a "bite" pose and each neck gets its own sway phase. **Run leg repair passes after sampling**, so feet reach the ground, hips sit under the mass rather than at the spine line, left and right lengths match, and thickness scales with body mass. **Mirror bilateral sockets by construction.** And **adopt an acceptance test**: any seed inside a family's ranges must pass the silhouette check at 48 px, or the ranges get narrowed.

Moskowitz's remark that "each creature would use a certain mouth" ([oral history](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/developing-i-spore-i-an-oral-sporal-history-10-years-on)) argues for a jaw part family with an open attack pose. At sprite scale an open mouth is a large value change, where a closed one is a 1 px line.

## Blend formulas: pick one convention and measure it in output pixels

The quadratic soft-min is the one formula with a primary source. Media Molecule's version, credited to Dave Smith, is printed in Evans's slides with a typo (`abs(a,b)`). The speaker notes correct it to `float e = max(r - abs(a - b), 0); return min(a, b) - e*e*0.25/r;` **[V]** ([Evans 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/AlexEvans_SIGGRAPH-2015-sml.pdf)). A third-party paper cites "Inigo Quilez. 2013. Smooth Minimum" as the polynomial form and calls it "an effective way to model smooth shapes". It also warns that soft-boolean outputs "are not signed distance functions … even though the input is" ([Liu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10954)). The remaining variants below are **prior knowledge of Quilez's article, not read from it**. Each was checked numerically for this report: the classic form equals Evans's form with r = k, and the normalised quadratic with k/4, to 4×10⁻¹⁶ over 100,000 random pairs. Every normalised variant dips by exactly k where a = b. The team should still paste these against iquilezles.org/articles/smin in a browser.

```glsl
// CLASSIC (2013) = Media Molecule soft_min.  k = BAND WIDTH.  Max bulge = k/4.  Hard min once |a-b| >= k.
float sminBand( float a, float b, float k ) {
    float h = clamp( 0.5+0.5*(b-a)/k, 0.0, 1.0 );
    return mix( b, a, h ) - k*h*(1.0-h);
}
// NORMALISED family.  k = MAX BULGE.  Band = 4k (quadratic), 6k (cubic, C2-smooth).
float sminQuad ( float a, float b, float k ){ k*=4.0; float h=max(k-abs(a-b),0.0)/k; return min(a,b)-h*h*k*0.25; }
float sminCubic( float a, float b, float k ){ k*=6.0; float h=max(k-abs(a-b),0.0)/k; return min(a,b)-h*h*h*k*(1.0/6.0); }
// EXPONENTIAL: associative (order-free) but GLOBAL (never reverts to hard min).  Dip = log2(n)*k for n equal inputs.
float sminExp  ( float a, float b, float k ){ return -k*log2( exp2(-a/k)+exp2(-b/k) ); }
// MIX FACTOR: .x = distance (same as sminQuad), .y = weight toward b, 0..1, = 0.5 at a==b.  Lerp colour / material / line-suppress by .y
vec2 sminMix( float a, float b, float k ){ k*=4.0; float h=max(k-abs(a-b),0.0)/k; float m=h*h*0.5; float s=m*k*0.5;
    return (a<b) ? vec2(a-s,m) : vec2(b-s,1.0-m); }
// Smooth max / subtraction: smax(a,b,k) = -smin(-a,-b,k);  carve b out of a = smax(a,-b,k)
```

The convention matters because the earlier report mixed the two. **Name the parameter for what it is.** Call it `blendBand` if you use the classic form, or `blendBulge` if you use a normalised one, and convert with bulge = band/4 (quadratic) or band/6 (cubic). Every number in this report is a **classic band width B**. Then express B in output pixels **[I]**. With `px = creatureWorldHeight / spritePixelHeight` (a 1.2 m bear baked at 60 px gives px = 2 cm), a feature band of B ≤ 1.5 px bulges by at most 0.375 px. That is effectively a hard union at bake scale, which is the intent.

| Variant | Reverts to hard min at \|a−b\| = | Associative? | Use it for [I] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic / quadratic | B (classic) = 4k (normalised) | No; grouping changes the result by ≈0.14k | Default for every join |
| Cubic | 6k | No; ≈0.06k | Large torso masses, where C2 smoothness gives cleaner toon bands |
| Exponential | never | Yes | Only unordered scatters (fur tufts); never the main body, because it breaks culling |

Locality is what makes a CPU baker cheap. Evans notes that soft blend "breaks ALL THE CULLING" unless the soft min "revert[s] to hard min/max once distance fields are sufficiently far apart", and that culling must "consider the amount of 'future soft blend'" **[V]** ([Evans 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/AlexEvans_SIGGRAPH-2015-sml.pdf)). So inflate each primitive's bounding box by its band (B, 4k or 6k) and cull per tile **[I]**. Fold in a fixed order (spine → masses → limbs → head → features), so that output is deterministic per seed. The associativity error of about 0.1k is invisible at pixel scale.

**The accumulation rule is the new, quantified explanation for the bat-blob and the hydra-sausage.** Folding n coincident primitives into one group multiplies the bulge:

| Primitives overlapping one point | Quadratic fold bulge | Exponential bulge |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1.00k | 1.00k |
| 3 | 1.56k | 1.58k |
| 5 | 2.20k | 2.32k |
| 8 | 2.69k | 3.00k |
| 12 | 3.03k | 3.58k |

These figures were computed for this report and match the research notes. A bat head built from cranium, snout, two ears, jaw and eye bumps in one group swells by 2–3k at its centre, so the ears' and snout's notches fill in. The rule **[I]**: no point should lie inside the band of more than 3–4 primitives of the same group. Hard-union heavily overlapping masses first, then blend the result as one.

The primitives themselves were checked numerically against brute force (prior-knowledge code, [U] as Quilez's). The **capsule** and **round cone** are exact. The round cone matched a dense sphere-sweep to 10⁻⁴ and is the workhorse: it encodes a radius profile directly, for thigh-to-shin, neck, snout, tail and digits. The **improved ellipsoid** `k0*(k0-1)/k1` (with `k0 = |p/r|`, `k1 = |p/r²|`) is safe outside (ratio 0.60–1.00) but overstates interior depth by up to 2.67×. Use it freely for silhouette masses, but **never take onion shells, inward fur or "depth below skin" shading from an ellipsoid's interior**. The 2D **vesica** (two discs of radius r with centres ±d, giving a shape 2(r−d) wide and 2√(r²−d²) tall) is exact and makes pointed ears, ear tufts and eye-decal shapes. `opOnion(d,t) = abs(d)−t` makes shells: intersect one with a half-space for an owl's facial-disc rim or a snail-shell lip. Dreams' primitive list adds superellipsoid "markoids … with variable power for x,y,z" **[V]** ([Evans 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/AlexEvans_SIGGRAPH-2015-sml.pdf)). These suit boxy-round masses such as a bear's chest, a hump or a square muzzle.

## A recipe book: blend levels, crisp features, fur and eight species

Everything in this section is **[I]**, assembled from the verified operators, Infinigen's part vocabulary, Blender's branch rule and Riot's tiering. No primary source gives per-species ratios or k values. Treat every number as a starting point for the art-director loop to tune. The notation is as follows. B is the classic band width. R is the radius of the smaller part at the joint. ry is vertical depth and rx is width. px is one output pixel in world units.

| Join level | Operator | Band B | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body masses ↔ spine | cubic or quadratic smin | 0.3–0.6 R | Masses overlap the spine by ≥ 0.5 R, so the blend only fills seams |
| Limb or neck root ↔ body | smin | 0.25–0.4 R(root) | Always add a branch mass (shoulder, hip, collar) |
| Within a limb chain | smin, or no blend | 0.15–0.3 R | Consecutive round cones that share end spheres need no blend at all |
| Head ↔ neck/body | smin | 0.15–0.25 R(neck) | Preserves the neck notch in silhouette |
| Snout/jaw ↔ cranium | smin | 0.1–0.2 R(snout) | Preserves the "stop" between muzzle and forehead |
| Features ↔ parent only | hard `min`, or B ≤ 1.5 px | — | Ears, horns, antlers, hooves, claws, beak, eye stalks, membranes |
| Carves (eye socket, mouth line) | smooth subtraction | ≈ 1 px | Toe and hoof splits are hard subtractions |
| Colour regions | `sminMix` weight, or a colour-only edit | — | Muzzle, belly, mask, facial disc, membrane |
| Between sibling necks or legs | hard `min` | 0 | Siblings never blend with each other |

The crisp-feature rules follow from the accumulation table and from pixel aliasing. First, **evaluate the body group completely, then attach each feature to the running result** with `min` or B ≤ 1.5 px, so that no feature ever sits inside a large band. Second, give every feature a **minimum thickness of about 1.5 px** at bake scale, or it aliases in and out between frames. Third, **seat features with mount depth, not blend**: ears sink 30–50% of their base radius into the skull, and limbs sink until the root sphere's centre is inside the torso. Fourth, build pointed ears from an elongated vesica or a flattened round cone. Build cupped ears from an onion shell cut by a half-space, so the inner face can take a darker colour region. Fifth, **eyes are tagged decals placed after the bake**. If they are modelled at all, use a small socket subtraction plus a colour region, never a bump. Sixth, snap ear tips, horn tips and antler tines to positions that break the outline in the chosen isometric facings.

Fur, ruffs and manes are a separate pass after the body blend, not more geometry in the body group. The base formula is `d_fur = d_body − furLen(p)`, where `furLen` is a per-region scalar field. Read it from the nearest socket or region ID, and smooth it across regions with the `sminMix` weights. Keep `|∇furLen| ≤ 0.5` and the noise amplitude at or below 25% of `furLen`, and march with step × 0.7, because offsets of a smin field are not true distances ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10954) for the non-SDF property). Since an offset under 1 px vanishes at bake, clamp any fur that must read to at least 1 px. The Dreams lesson applies here: crisp SDF renders looked like "untextured unreal-engine", and the fix was a different final renderer ([Evans 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/AlexEvans_SIGGRAPH-2015-sml.pdf)). For this pipeline that renderer is the pixel bake, so at the small end, fur belongs in the 2D pass.

| Fur element | Construction | Values |
|---|---|---|
| Body coat | Outward offset | 0.1–0.15 R (bear); 0.05 R (stag); skip below ~60 px tall |
| Neck ruff or mane | Torus or ellipsoid collar mass at the neck root, near-hard union, own colour; plus 5–9 tuft cones along its outward normal | Tufts unioned at B ≈ 1 px; tuft tips ≥ 2 px long so they notch the outline |
| Bushy tail | Thin round-cone tail chain plus an offset on the distal two-thirds, plus 3–5 tip tufts | Offset 0.8–1.5 × tail radius |
| Small sprites (30–60 px) | No geometric fur; 1 px silhouette dither or tuft stamps in the 2D bake | Geometric fur only pays off above ~80 px |
| Never | Inward offsets or onion shells on ellipsoids | Interior distance is up to 2.7× wrong |

The species recipes below put these rules into practice. The "protect" column is the Riot-style primary feature. Riot's rule that "a champion's primary feature should never be removed or significantly altered" is **[V]** ([Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)). In this generator, that means seed variation may never shrink the protected feature below its minimum pixel size. The choice of feature per species is **[I]** and must be validated with black-fill naming tests.

Generalising to 64 species works best through roughly 8–12 family templates (ursid, canid, cervid, chiropteran, strigiform, gastropod, arachnid, serpent/hydra, and so on), each holding 2–4 reference-fitted radius profiles. Species are then convex combinations plus an exaggeration of the protected feature, following Infinigen's prior scheme. Compton's warning applies to the between-species spacing: "It's more important to have a smaller number of distinct items than a nonillion items that most players can't tell apart" **[V]** ([Cross on Compton](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/practical-procedural-generation-for-everyone-)). Her Spore practice of whitelisting good seeds instead of perfecting the algorithm supports shipping only curated, test-passing seeds per species.

## Bake rules: low resolution, no AA, then deliberate post passes

Dead Cells is the only primary 3D-to-pixel source read in full, and its lesson is double-edged. Vasseur renders "in a very small size and without antialiasing", exports each frame "to a .png, along with its normal map", and shades in engine "using a basic toon shader" **[V]**. He also states that "we still haven't found any solution for flickering pixels", that cleaning by hand would defeat the point, and that "the disappointing level of details is and always will be an issue" ([Vasseur](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)). His character is "only 50 pixels" tall, in this project's range. A raw low-resolution toon render therefore will **not** produce clean animal faces on its own. The pipeline needs authored post passes and a temporal-stability check, and Dead Cells is evidence for that need, not a solution to it. Practitioner articles on Game Developer add verified ordering rules. McMahon works "in order of reads": silhouette, then shapes broken up "through color or separating lines", then detail, which "is the absolute last thing you should do". In an isometric view "the primary read is from the character's head and moves its way down" **[V]** ([McMahon](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/creating-an-art-style)). Riot's facing rule ("It should be clear which direction a champion is facing from their silhouette alone") and its practice of checking "every skin across all maps" are **[V]** ([Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)).

Testing closes the loop on the bake. Riot gave a skin the "'who's-this-champ?' test" using shadow-only images **[V]** ([Ask Riot](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/ask-riot-let-s-talk-clarity/)). Chessarama fixed a readability failure by testing a piece "from different angles" **[V2]** ([Chiquetano](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/do-chess-pieces-have-a-mood-the-moodboard-and-character-design-in-chessarama)). McMahon recommends thumbnail sheets of silhouettes before detail **[V2]**. In project terms **[I]**, there are four checks. Render each species as a black fill at game size in every isometric facing used. Lay all 64 out on one sheet to catch collisions (bear/dog, wolf/fox, stag/goat, owl/other bird, bat/bird). Composite every species on every biome tile. Measure frame-to-frame mask IoU, because Dead Cells shows flicker is the default outcome. Expression belongs in silhouette parameters (ear angle, head height, tail angle, raised ruff, open jaw, wing spread, coil tightness), since Riot treats animation as "a bonus differentiator rather than a main one" **[V]** ([Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)).

## The art director: forced-choice pairs, a bracket that includes the incumbent, and a golden check

The verified critic literature makes the earlier "compare, don't score" thesis stronger. GenArena queries a judge twice with the order swapped, and "a reliable preference is recorded only if the judge model consistently selects the same image across both permutations". Pairwise "consistently outperforms pointwise". Qwen3-VL-8B went from 49.1% to 60.5% human agreement on image generation and from 58.3% to 83.7% on editing **[V]**. Pointwise scoring on the editing benchmark tied 23.5% of distinct pairs and inverted 18.2%. Offering an explicit "tie" option was harmful: the judge said "tie" in nearly 40% of clear cases, and decisive-pair accuracy fell from 83.9% to 54.9%. Bigger judges agree more, from 60.9% at 4B to 68.0% at 32B ([GenArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06013)). GenArena is still a preprint, and its domain is photographs and edits, not sprites. Asset Forge is the sprite-domain evidence, and it shows what pointwise judging does at this scale. Even after five calibration rounds, its Opus 4.6 judge scored sprites humans rated 2.3–3.0 at 4.2–4.3, with a range of about 3.6–4.8 against the humans' 2.3–5.0 **[V]** ([Asset Forge](https://matthewdeaves.github.io/assetforge)). A pairwise judge never has to say "this is a 2". It only has to say which of two is better.

The perception evidence dictates what the judge must *not* be asked. On BlindTest, the best model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) reached 77.84% and the four-model mean was 58.07%. Accuracy approached 100% "when much more space is added to separate shapes", resolution "does not affect" most tasks, and "slow-thinking" variants did no better (71.59% against 72.75%) **[V]** ([v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06581v6)). No 2026 frontier-model figures were found. So the judge should not count legs, heads or touching parts. Compute those facts from the body graph and pass them in, and add an **exploded diagnostic view** with parts rendered slightly apart. Upscaling the sprite helps input hygiene, but it is not the cure. The claim that images under about 200 px degrade Claude's vision quality remains [U] (the docs host was not reachable), so upscale anyway.

Assembled, one species round runs as follows **[I]**. The proposer reads the previous round's reasons, writes its difference list, and emits 6–8 candidates: tweaks on odd rounds, leaps on even rounds, plus one or two exploratory edits. The pipeline renders each candidate's sheet, a large key frame, an exploded part view, the alpha mask and the index grid. Guards filter out failures. Survivors and the incumbent enter a knockout, where each match is two order-swapped forced-choice calls per criterion group. The incumbent survives any conflict. At 30–64 px the full index grid fits in the prompt. At 100–200 px (up to 40,000 characters) send the grid only for the region under critique, and rely on the image for the whole. Asset Forge only tested up to 64 px. Before trusting any of it, the team should calibrate the judge in rounds, changing one thing at a time (anchors, then inputs, then pre-checks) and reporting error per criterion. Asset Forge's dimensions moved independently, and averages hid regressions **[V]** ([Asset Forge](https://matthewdeaves.github.io/assetforge)). A gold set of 50–100 blind human A/B labels on the team's own sprites, re-run whenever the judge model changes, is the minimum **[I]**. The ordering constraint from the earlier report still stands: the loop cannot discover a hump primitive, a cranium-snout stop or a neck subtree that the body graph cannot express, so the structural changes come first.

The licence picture is now verified from publishers. SMAL's own licence page grants use "for the sole purpose of performing non-commercial scientific research, non-commercial education, or non-commercial artistic projects". Even using it as a proportion reference for a commercial game falls outside that grant. Quaternius states "All models are under the CC0 License", usable "in commercial, educational, and personal projects" without attribution **[V]** ([Quaternius FAQ](https://quaternius.com/faq.html)). PanTHERIA and EltonTraits both state "Copyright restrictions: None" and request citation, under an archive default of CC BY 4.0 **[V]** ([PanTHERIA](https://esapubs.org/archive/ecol/E090/184/metadata.htm); [ESA copyright](https://esapubs.org/archive/copyright.htm); [EltonTraits](https://esapubs.org/archive/ecol/E095/178/metadata.php)). ## Conclusion

Verification moved the most important lever from "which blend" to "how many things share a blend". The bat's blob face and the hydra's sausage are not failures of the smooth minimum itself. They are what happens when many primitives share one blend group and their bulges add up. That makes the fix mechanical and testable: count overlapping primitives per group, keep features out of the body group, and give every sibling (neck, leg, ear) a hard boundary. The convention mix-up is the cheapest bug to catch now. A team that copied "k ≈ 0.3–0.6 R" into a normalised `smin` has quadrupled every fillet, and that alone would turn lean bodies into dough. Spore's verified history adds a design-order insight: start with the bean and hang the spine on it, not the reverse.

The critic evidence also shifts in an instructive direction. Every correction made the case for pairwise, fact-fed judging stronger, and none weakened it. The decisive sprite-domain result is negative: a carefully calibrated pointwise judge still could not tell a 2.3 sprite from a 4.3. The three highest-value next steps all need a human with a browser rather than more agent research: compare the smin and primitive code against Quilez's pages and read his Happy Jumping scene function for per-joint k and ear and eyelid construction; retry chrishecker.com for the metaball falloff and N-leg gait phasing; and open the eLife gait figures. Everything else in this report can go straight into the generator and be tuned by the loop.
