Creature sizing (Ed, 2026-10-30; coordinator 6520479974 and 6520542543) uses the visible standing witch's **43 occupied pixels**. This supersedes the earlier game-wide projected-fit instruction: witch, house, props and other non-creature art retain the old renderer path. Only creatures opt into camera-projected support. Ellipsoids use oriented radii, rounded boxes their support, round cones endpoint spheres, and masked planes their clipping parallelogram. Ray acceleration and its 96-step budget remain unchanged.

`art/genome/size-ranks.js` assigns [broad body-mass classes](projected-fit-data/ranks.md) within each age. With class rank r from 0 to 1, baby targets are 0.75+0.2r witch heights through r=0.9, then 0.93+2.7(r-0.9); youth targets are 1.1+0.4r; adults 1.8+1.2r. Legends target at least 1.75 times the same species' rounded adult height. Targets measure the full occupied silhouette, including decoration. Nearby classes may tie after pixel rounding; tiger>cat, wolf>fox>stoat, elephant>rhino and class gaps of two or more must remain strict. Mythical and extinct placements are design choices, not measured living-animal masses.

Projected support supplies the initial scale. Bounded sparse rays find the first occupied top/bottom pixels and test deterministic mote-neighbour pixels, without shading the interior. Scale, sampling phase and small scale corrections use at most nineteen sparse probes, followed by one full native raster. Snow leopard's collision-aware mote sampler retains a bounded full-raster fallback (at most three). Final native sprites are reused, with no extra full redraw. Source models are restored between probes. A bounded 128-entry fallback cache retains only scale and phase. Generation cost is included in the [combined numeric audit](projected-fit-data/age-sizes.md); resource gates are not relaxed. `node tools/body-graph/age-size-audit.mjs <output-directory>` measures all 1,040 gait/facing cells, palette indexing and species pages. Its eight-largest-species scenario is an estimate, not measured runtime residency; rigs, gear and sleep/wake sets are excluded. The earlier [projected-only table](projected-fit-data/sizes.md) is historical and superseded, not evidence for the combined size model.

Coordinator 6523246311 permits at most **±1 occupied pixel** from a nominal target, only inside age bands, strict growth, legend/adult and class-order constraints. `sizePixelRange` restricts ordinary upper bounds to their nominal targets and lower bounds above the upper target two classes below; legends cannot fall below their 1.75× nominal adult budget. Band endpoints use native-pixel rounding. Accessories reuse naked-body scale rather than shrinking the creature under a hat. Texture-off fits against the same anatomical reference, then renders its smooth surface. Sleeping poses retain their standing height budget after deformation; cropping retains the real ground anchor rather than moving it.

The mole and quadruped `fit:"current"` fields remain valid, but final age sizing governs their output too. No gameplay radius or reach changes accompany the visual sizes. Snow leopard alone uses bounded, separated decorative-mote placement to preserve its attachment count; other species retain their previous sampler.

- **Mesh creature**: a creature drawn in code as a low-poly mesh on a real skeleton (Ed, 2026-11-11: "Is there no way that you can draw things yourself, instead of using an image generator?"; 2026-11-12: "I'm happy with these models, let's do the real build"; `art/mesh/`, the plan and its history in `docs/plans/creature-meshes.md`). A kind's own file (`art/mesh/species/<id>.js`; `species/index.js` lists them, written from the folder by `node tools/creature-mesh/index.mjs`) holds how tall it stands at each level, how it sleeps and four **plans**, baby to legend, each its own build from the **kit**'s parts (one a file, `art/mesh/kit/<part>.js`: a torso, legs, a head, tails, tufts, a dome, the gaits; how to write one: **Writing a mesh creature**, below); `shape.js` turns a plan into flat triangles each riding one bone, and `pose` puts its bones anywhere in its walk; `raster.js` draws that as sprites in the art worker (eight frames a walk and a ninth standing still, towards and away, each heading stood on its own bottom row, colour and the camera's normals, glowing parts at alpha 254); `plans.js` gathers which kinds have one and how tall (`MESH_HEIGHTS`). It stands on the ground and strides (Ed's playtest, 2026-11-12: "animals appear to float above the ground"; "they appear to vibrate along instead of walk"; "many small creatures bound rather than trot"): `shape.js` `grounded` holds the lowest point of its legs on the ground through its whole clip (or as high as its bound has it then), a plan's `gait.type` says how it goes (walk, trot, bound, glide, fly: the fox trots, the stoat, hare, squirrel and dormouse bound, the badger trots and the mole and hedgehog scurry, the turtle walks, the snail glides, the bee flies), and its feet plant (Ed, 2026-11-12, of the walk clip: "the feet don't plant, and the bodies seem rigid"; its legs "still slide or skate, mostly short-legged animals at speed"): `shape.js` `strides` puts each paw down on a spot of ground and keeps it there for its stance, finding the leg's joints from the paw (not turning them by a clock), so the paw goes back under the body at exactly the body's pace whatever the body does above it, and the body moves with the gait (a bob twice a cycle for a walk and a trot, a bound's rise and fall with its back gathering and stretching, a little pitch, roll and sway, the head turning most of it back, each tail joint following late); a legged kind has two paces, a slow one and a fast one (`gait.paces`: `kit/gaits.js` `PACES` holds each pace's numbers, and a kind's file changes them), each baked as its own eight frames (the slow cycle, the standing frame, then the fast cycle from `MESH_RUN`); and the view steps it with `art/mesh/stride.js` `stepStride`: the sprite goes forward in whole steps with its frames (drawn up to a step behind where the creature is), so a planted foot never slides, the fast clip takes over past a cycle and a half a second of the slow one, there is no cap on frames a second (the cap was feet sliding), and stopping from a walk it stands on a frame of that walk with every foot down (`meshGait` `rests`), from a run on its standing frame. `node tools/creature-mesh/slip.mjs` measures the slip, in the clip and as the view shows it; `clip.mjs` makes the before-and-after clip. A creature's size in the game is the height of what is drawn of it standing (a frame's `tall`, from `raster.js`), not its sprite's (Ed, 2026-11-12: sizes come "from the height actually drawn, not the canvas size"): the sprite is taller, with room for a leap. On by default (Ed, 2026-11-12: "meshes are great, we should aim to transition to them for all the creatures"; `?meshCreatures=0` shows the generated creatures, to compare): such a species' frames come from its mesh at every level, every tube with twice the sides its plan gives (`fine`) and a coat texture on adults and legends, wild, enraged (a darker coat, red lit eyes), in party gear (`gear.js` `dress`: collar, hat, glasses, shoes), asleep (`napping`, two breaths, eyes shut: "sphinx" on its front for the young and the grown, "curl" for a legend, in a ring like a sleeping dog, nose to tail with its legs under it (Ed, 2026-11-12: "It should be like references"; `gait.curl` tunes a species'), "shell" drawn into it for the snail and the turtle; Ed, 2026-11-12: "Most sleeping legends should curl up on their sides. Snail and turtle sleep in their shells."; a plan says with `gait.sleep`) and as a sleeping legend (`index.js` `meshSleep`: stone and moss, part sunk), and the live rig leaves it be; the bee is a kind, in the marten's place on home (it hovers, and lands to sleep), the marten is on the bench (`src/rules/kinds.ts` `BENCH`: kept whole, with no island), the turtle is a kind in the newt's place in the Fen (it sleeps in its shell), and the newt is on the bench with the marten; it has no idle fidget or glide of its own yet (its standing frame and its walk's frames stand in). A legend's **armour** is plate raised from its own torso's polygons with a lit rune on each plate, and nothing on it is a ball (Ed, 2026-11-11: "I don't like the random spheres. Armour should just be thickened polygons on their torso with a different colour and glowing runes."). `node tools/creature-mesh/make.mjs <species>-<level> out.glb` writes one as a rigged .glb; `sheet.mjs`, `sizes.mjs`, `frames.mjs` and `index.mjs` beside it are the rollout's commands (**Writing a mesh creature**).

- **Genome**: a species as data (#79; since 2026-10-13 one file a species, `art/creatures/<id>/genome.js`, assembled by `art/creatures/index.js`, which `node tools/creatures.mjs index` writes sorted by id, and re-exported as `GENOMES` by `art/genome/species.js`; Ed, 2026-10-13: every animal PR edited the one file and merges mangled neighbouring records. **A new creature** is a new folder with its `genome.js` (`export const GENOME_<ID> = { id, name, template, builder, ... }`, a named export: the Art Lab's build inlines every module into one page), its `sigil.js` (`SIGIL_<ID> = { neon, strokes }`, the strokes from `art/sigils/strokes.js`'s building blocks; `art/sigils.js` assembles `SIGILS` and `SIGIL_NEON` from them), its `legend.js` (`LEGEND_<ID>`, the sleeping form `art/legends.js` documents) its `nap.js` (`NAP_<ID>`, `art/naps.js`) and its `creature.js` (`CREATURE_<ID>`: the config files' per-species entries, section by section: `movement`, `radius` and `legendMoves` (`config/movement.json`'s), `attacks`, `strength`, `ranged`, `kite`, `temperament` and `traits` (`config/combat.json`'s), `voice` and `character`, and `pack` (`config/packs.json`'s kinds: its pack combat); the readers (`rules/creatureConfig.ts`) assemble the tables the rules always had, the schema is `config/schema/creature.schema.json`; `node tools/creatures.mjs show <id>` prints everything about one species), and a run of the tool. Still in their tables: its island in its area's `art/areas/<id>/kind.json` (its line of the kinds table: it takes a placeholder's slot, and that placeholder's `config/kinds/<id>.json` goes) and the tuning file's `army.heavy.species` and `population.swarm.classes` lists (Ed's knobs); `node tools/creatures.mjs list | check | adopt` list them, check the index and the records (CI, `src/creatures.test.ts`), and move any record a merge pastes back into `species.js`'s array): its template, builder, palette, proportions (`body`, `head`), parts (`parts`: ears, tail, feet, horns, antlers, tusks) and markings (`coat`); `speciesOf` makes the builders' species object from it, `genomeHash` its bake-cache key. Edit the record, not the builder, to change a species.
- **Template**: a body plan (`art/genome/templates.js`): quadruped, avian, flyer, serpent, insectoid, squat; its builders, its sockets and the part tags each allows, tags that exclude each other, its gait (for the live rig) and, for the quadruped, its **size curves** (four numbers, baby to legend, for each proportion that changes with level).
- **Material mask / palette row**: a creature sprite baked once as which material each pixel is (and whether it's an outline), coloured at draw time from a palette row (`art/genome/palette.js`: `bakeMask`, `paletteRow`, `paintPixels`, `PALETTE_GLSL`); a **palette variant** is a curated coat (`PALETTE_VARIANTS`: frost, ember, shadow, gold, moss, rose, ash) that recolours a species with no rebake.
- **Surface texture**: a creature's fur, feathers, scales, plates, shell or bristles (`art/genome/texture.js`; Ed, 2026-10-05, #119): its coat's pixels gathered into small cells shaped by its kind (strokes running back and down the body, scallops, a diamond lattice, segments, bands round a whorl, long spines), each sharing one normal so the game's stepped light breaks along tufts rather than smooth contours; three or four chosen tones by a fixed light from above (the lifted top `M.BODYL`, the body, its shade, a core shadow), cell by cell; dark seams at a cell's lower or trailing edge; and lumps on its volumes. A baby's coarse and soft, a legend's fine and rich. The genome's `texture` (`kind`, `size`, `stretch`, `flatten`, `contrast`, `seam`, `lumps`, `tones`) over its template's; the rig's pieces wear it too. `?texture=0` (the style's `texture` knob) draws creatures as before. Under the art director's stylised bake (`?style=bold|ref`, `style.artStyle`) the stamps only shape the normals (and seams, by `texDetail`), and the bake lays the tones; `texDetail` (0 cel shapes to 1 fine strokes) and `hueShift` are the style's knobs.
- **Evolution kit**: a species' genome `levels` (four records, baby to legend, any of them null): each level's own proportions (its `body`, `head`, `coat` and `parts` over the species' own: `humpK`, `front`, `neckAng`, `tuskSize`, `tuskCurl`, `bands`...) and its own `features` (`GENOME_FEATURE_KINDS`: `mane`, tapered curved spikes along the back or belly; `wisps`, flames off its tusks, horns or spine; `eyeglint`, its eyes shown past a hump; `stones`, standing stones on its back with moonlit runes; `claws`, long claws off its feet; `moss`, moss and heather on its flanks; `tails`, brush tails fanned along its rump (a kitsune's seven, fox-fire tips; `replace` for its own tail); `ruff`, locks round its neck; `brambles`, thorny vines with berries (the genome palette's `flower`) over its body; and the head and coat knobs `mask` and `beads`; the twisted horn, `horn3d`, is a part: `parts.horns: "twist"` with `head.horn`, a ram's curl by `ease`, `tighten` and `thorns`), drawn by `evolve3d` (`art/creatures3d.js`) from the model's landmarks, each feature on its rig piece (Ed, 2026-10-05: "the evolved design per stage, not just being larger"). It replaces a legend's single `legend` feature where a species has it. The boar is the pilot; the badger (the moor's walking stones) the snail (a tower the forest built; its builder reads `body.shell`) and the fox (a kitsune) the ram (bronze thorned horns, brambles) the woodlouse (its builder reads `body.plates`: a fortress with moonstone spires) the wolf (spirit-fire over a spiky mane; `frost` on its muzzle) and the owl (its builder reads `down`, `tufts`, `discRim`, `bars`, `cloak`, `eyesRing`) follow. # Revised body-size candidate (Ed, 2026-10-31)

Ed resolved the size-range hold: small adults must remain readable, babies and
youths distinguishable, adults larger where useful, and legends notably larger
within a species but smaller overall. The smallest legends may match the largest
adults. This supersedes the former universal age bands and 1.75 legend minimum
for the revised sizes. Ed adopted them in game at 13:26 UTC, cancelling the
mockups. Revised sizes are the default; `?creatureSizeCandidate=0` compares
the previous size model. Existing redesign selection is unchanged.

`art/genome/size-candidate.js` maps the existing mass cohorts to adult body
targets 65–200 native pixels at the actual 43px standing witch, with wolf120.
Baby/youth use 45%/70% adult; legend targets200–280. Calibration excludes volumes already marked
`extra` by the anatomy builders and floating motes; the complete original model
is drawn at that scale, preserving ornaments, gear, anatomy and gameplay radii.
`sizeFit.bodyHeight` reports the measured anatomical silhouette separately.
CI remains the release gate; the cancelled scene captures are not required.
The fixture manifest is `tools/body-graph/size-candidate-scenes.json`.

Size-adoption CI regressions (2026-10-31): `eyesOn` records the head's union
group so expressions find the same blended surface as rendering; the smaller
heron legend's dazed brows remain visible. Nap crops retain blank native canvas
for a ground anchor below the opaque box, preserving the anchor and every drawn
pixel; the existing awake-height budget includes this margin. Carriers retain
their original limits, with at most two additional measured correction passes
after the original two guesses when integer native-size steps overshoot.

Rig capture-only forms use the same sparse occupied-body fit as whole sprites,
but consume only its scale and geometry. They omit the full body raster that
the rig immediately discarded; ordinary sprites and snow-leopard mote fallback
still draw normally. This preserves all-species/all-age Stiltwalkers coverage
without increasing test timeouts. Paired native captures of wolf, woodlouse and
rhino legends kept exactly the same scales and geometry records; measured
construction fell from1092/1843/4270ms to147/168/88ms on this laptop (sample
timings, not a runtime frame-rate guarantee).

# Writing a mesh creature (how-to)

For a batch of the rollout (`docs/plans/creature-mesh-rollout.md`; Ed, 2026-11-12: "meshes are great, we should aim to transition to them for all the creatures"). A kind is one new file and no edit to a shared list, so batches run side by side. Read **Mesh creature** above first.

1. **Sizes.** `node tools/creature-mesh/sizes.mjs <kind>...` prints today's drawn height of each kind at each level, meshes off, and the `heights` line to paste (Ed, 2026-11-12: "keep the sizes"). It counts drawn rows; the sprite is a few rows taller.
2. **The file.** `art/mesh/species/<id>.js`, the kind's id as the game has it (`art/creatures/<id>/`). It exports `MESH = { id, heights: [baby, young, adult, legend], plans: { baby, young, adult, legend }, curl? }`. Copy the nearest of home's: `fox.js` (a trotter built part by part), `stoat.js` (`kit/weasel.js`, a bound), `hare.js` (`kit/quad.js`, long hind legs), `badger.js` (low and broad), `turtle.js` (a dome), `snail.js` and `bee.js` (no kit skeleton: their own bones).
3. **A plan** is a function returning `{ name, bones, lofts, cones, armour, gait, anchors }`; `kit/plan.js` `plan(name, body, skull, parts, gait, extra)` joins the kit's parts into one. Each level is its own build, not the last one enlarged (Ed, 2026-10-05: "the evolved design per stage (not just being larger)"): change proportions, add the signature, and make the legend its animal grown strange.
4. **Kit parts** are one a file in `art/mesh/kit/`: `torso`, `leg` (and `fourLegs`), `head`, `tail`, `tufts`, `band`, `ball`, `dome`, `plate`, `whiskers`, `quad` (a four-legged build in one go), `weasel`, `gaits`, `plan`, `bib`, `paint` (a mask on a face, bands across a back), `ringtail` (a thick tail ringed dark and light), `rudder` (an otter's tapering tail). A part your batch needs and the kit lacks (hooves, antlers, a wing, a trunk) is a new file there, imported by the kinds that use it; don't add it to another part's file.
5. **The list.** `node tools/creature-mesh/index.mjs` rewrites `art/mesh/species/index.js` from the folder (`--check` in the test). Never edit it by hand. A kind in the list is drawn as its mesh at once (`hasMesh`), in the game and on the sheet, so the batch's branch is combined only when Ed has passed its models. A batch whose models wait for Ed keeps them off by parking their files in `art/mesh/pending/` (the tool lists only `species/`): to draw the sheet or switch them on, `git mv art/mesh/pending/*.js art/mesh/species/` and run the tool (batch A, 2026-11-13). Then `node tools/creature-mesh/lengths.mjs` rewrites `config/mesh-lengths.json`, each mesh kind's drawn length at each level, which her line of followers is spaced by (Ed, 2026-11-13; `src/render/meshLengths.test.ts` fails while it is stale): run it again whenever a kind is added or its size or shape changes.
6. **Pictures.** `node tools/creature-mesh/sheet.mjs <kind>...` writes one sheet for the batch (`previews/creature-mesh/sheet-<kinds>.png`): each kind's four sizes beside today's drawing at the same scale, its legend turned away (its armour), its adult and legend asleep, and its adult's walk frame by frame with its gait and stride. Publish it with `node tools/shots/push.mjs`, then stop for Ed.
7. **Others untouched.** `node tools/creature-mesh/frames.mjs --out before.json` before you start and `--compare before.json` after: every other kind's frames must be identical (`--levels 0,1,2` leaves out the legends, which are most of the time).
8. **The cats' parts** (batch B, 2026-11-13): `kit/feline.js` (a cat's build, tail and plan, and how cats go: `stalks`, a prowl and a trot, `springs` and `sprints`, a prowl and a bound; asleep a cat of any age curls), `kit/mane.js` (rows of locks round the neck and a crown of them on the head) and `kit/markings.js` (`marked` gives the skin more rings and paints `stripes`, `spots` or `tears` on them, keeping armour and anchors where they were; `ringed` for a tail's rings and a leg's bars). A kind that adds its gait adds it to `GAITS` in `src/render/meshCreatures.test.ts`.
9. **The heavy and the gnawing kinds' parts** (batch D, 2026-11-13): `kit/trunk.js` (an elephant's trunk on a chain of its own bones off the head; hand its `chain` to the gait's `tails` and it swings behind the walk and lies along the ground asleep), `kit/horn.js` (one horn on the middle of the nose, with a legend's bands and lit point), `kit/flattail.js` (`kit/tail.js`'s tail as a paddle: a beaver's scaled one, a flying squirrel's feather) and `kit/glideskin.js` (a sheet down each flank from wrist to ankle, its ends riding the legs). What stands or hangs off a head points at the ground when the head goes down to sleep, and a sleeper rests on the lowest point of all of it (`shape.js` `grounded`): the rhino and the elephant sleep `"curl"` with a small `MESH.curl.turn` (over on their sides, bent a little), which lays the head level.
10. **The cold-blooded kinds' parts** (batch E, 2026-11-13): `kit/sprawl.js` (`sprawlLeg`, a leg that goes out sideways from the flank and down to a flat foot, with the kit's own bone names so the kit's leg timings plant it; `sprawler`, a low broad build on four of them; `sprawls`, how a sprawler goes: a creep one foot after another and a scuttle on diagonal pairs, its hips and shoulders swinging opposite ways, type "trot"; `tucked`, its legs laid back along its sides asleep), `kit/scutes.js` (a row of scutes, stones or flames standing along a back) and `kit/serpent.js` (`serpent`, a whole legless plan: one tube with a bone a ring, its forepart reared, a rattle if asked; it goes by "glide", its curves a wave that stands still on the ground while it goes forward through them, its `gait.stride` that wave's length, so no part of it slides sideways: `node tools/creature-mesh/slither.mjs` measures that, 0 is right; and `coil`, how it sleeps). A kind whose body the three sleeping poses don't fit gives its gait a `nap`: `art/mesh/gear.js` `napping` hands it the pose made so far (`{ plan, how, rots, poss }`) to change. A long sprite: seven kinds were too many for one sheet (the PNG encoder stops at 16.7 million pixels), so the batch drew two.
11. **The winged kinds' parts** (batch F, 2026-11-13): `kit/bird.js` (`bird`, a whole bird as a plan: a body carried at a slope on two legs, a neck with an S in it if asked, a head, beak, eyes, a fan of a tail, two wings shut along its sides; it goes on the ground by its `walk`, a walk or a two-footed hop on planted feet, and its fast pace flies: that pace has no legs and says `aloft`, so `shape.js` `grounded` carries it that much higher than it stands and leaves its wings free; with `run` it keeps to its feet, as the penguin does. Its gait's own `legs` is empty because its paces differ, each saying its own; asleep it roosts, `gait.sleep` "roost", by its `nap`), `kit/birdleg.js` (a bird's leg with the kit's hind-leg bone names, so `strides` plants it; `twoLegs` its timings), `kit/featherwing.js` (a wing on three bones laid out spread, then each bone's part carried whole to where it lies folded, so the plan draws it shut and `fly` gives the turns that open and beat it), `kit/flipper.js` (a penguin's), `kit/skinwing.js` (a bat's: skin between its flank, arm and three fingers; `flap` and `wrap`), `kit/insect.js` and `kit/insectwing.js` (the bee's body, wings and hover made parts: panels of colour on a wing's ground for a butterfly, veins and tatters for a dragonfly; `hovers` the gait). A bird's wings beat from level upward and it flies low: a frame's sprite stands on its bottom row, so anything drawn below a walking bird's feet (a near wing on its downstroke) would lift the walker off the ground, and the test allows no more than 15% of the sprite's height of air under any frame; check a new bird at all four levels, both headings. `gear.js` `napping` calls a gait's `nap` after a flyer's wings are folded, so a flyer may fold its own. `sheet.mjs --fast` adds a strip of the fast pace's frames (a flight).
12. **The many-legged kinds' parts** (batch G, 2026-11-13): `kit/jointleg.js` (`jointLeg`, an insect's, spider's or crab's leg: out from the body and up to a knee, down to a pointed tip, on four bones of its own names, leg, shin, foot and toe, which `shape.js` `strides` plants as it does a paw), `kit/manylegs.js` (`legPairs`, any number of pairs of them, the hindmost first, with `across: "z"` for a crab; the timings `sets`, alternating sets, so six legs go as two tripods and eight as two fours, and `ripple`, a woodlouse's fourteen one after another from the hindmost; `scuttles`, how they go: a crawl with most feet down and a scuttle, type "trot", or "walk" for a ripple, each pace's stance kept inside what a leg standing out sideways can reach, which `strides` itself doesn't work out; `folds`, a gait's `nap`: each leg folded flat at its side, with `also` for what else the kind does), `kit/pincers.js` (`pincer`: an arm, a hand and two fingers, one hinged on a bone of its own to snap), `kit/sting.js` (`stingTail`, a scorpion's tail of beads arched over its back to a bulb and sting, its `chain` for the gait's `tails`; `laidTail`, the same let down and laid round its side asleep) and `kit/turns.js` (the turns those poses are worked out with). None of the five has a back to curl, so all sleep `"sphinx"` at every level with a `nap` of their own: the woodlouse, a plate a bone, rolls into a ball. **The crab goes sideways**: the clip always goes along +z, so it is built facing across it (its face along -x, which the camera's two headings both look at) with its legs standing out fore and aft. `src/render/meshCreatures.test.ts` `LEGS` says how many feet a kind plants where it isn't four.
13. **The wonders' parts** (batch H, 2026-11-13): `kit/necks.js` (`neck`, one more neck with a serpent's head on it, on a chain of its own bones off any bone: the hydra's side heads, standing from the shoulders of a `kit/serpent.js` body whose own reared forepart is its middle neck; it hands back `sways` for a gait's `extra` and `pose` for a still pose, "rear", "snap" or "watch"; `crest`, the spines and frills a head wears), `kit/backplates.js` (`backPlates`, a stegosaurus's kites on edge in two rows, left and right in turn, at the stations `alongTail` and `alongBack` give; `vein` lights a line up each face) and `kit/thagomizer.js` (pairs of spikes near the end of a `kit/tail.js` tail, riding its bones). **Raised wings**: a legend that holds its wings spread (griffin, sphinx, feathered serpent) is drawn so, by `kit/featherwing.js`'s own `fold` with a negative `roll` (so it stands, walks and is sized with them up, as today's drawings are), and its gait's `nap` lets them down; none of the three curls onto a spread wing: the griffin and the sphinx sleep couched, `"sphinx"`, the serpents coil. The stegosaurus sleeps on its belly for its plates. **Heads that strike in turn** (Ed, 2026-11-13: "it would be cool if the hydra did attacks with alternating heads"): a plan's `gait.strike` is `{ heads: [three bones' names], pose(k, phase) }`, a still pose for head `k` drawn back ("a", "b": the watchers sway between the two) and struck ("snap"); `art/mesh/index.js` `meshWalk` draws them after the walk's frames (`raster.js` `more`), `meshGait` says where (`strike: { from, heads, each }`), and `src/render/headStrike.ts` picks one from the rules' spells: the first head back through the tell, each head struck as its shot is born (`Body.head`, from `emit.heads` in `config/species-attacks.json`), the next back while another shot is pending. `tools/creature-mesh/strike.mjs` makes a clip of it.

## The checklist, a kind

- **Ground contact** (Ed, 2026-11-12: "animals appear to float above the ground"). Its feet are on the ground walking, standing and asleep. `shape.js` `grounded` does it from the plan's legs (`gait.legs`), so name them; what hangs below its feet as it stands (a skirt, a belly) rests on the ground instead. A legless or flying kind: `hover` for the air under a flyer, nothing for a glider. `src/render/meshCreatures.test.ts` fails a kind whose lowest point leaves the ground or whose sprite has empty rows under its feet.
- **Stride and planted feet** (Ed: "their moving animations don't seem to animate their legs and are very short; they appear to vibrate along instead of walk"; then, of the clip, "the feet don't plant, and the bodies seem rigid"). You don't animate legs: name them (`gait.legs`, which the kit's gaits do) and `shape.js` `strides` plants each paw for its stance and solves the leg. What you choose is the pace's numbers (`kit/gaits.js` `PACES`: `duty`, `reach`, `step`, `leap`, and its `body`'s `bob`, `pitch`, `roll`, `sway`, `yaw`, `flex`, `head`, `tail`, `lag`, `wag`), by passing your own over the kit's (`hop({ run: { duty: 0.2, body: { flex: 0.3 } } })`) and `lively` (how much the body moves at every pace: more for a baby, less for a legend). Run `node tools/creature-mesh/slip.mjs <kind>`: the model slip and the shown slip should both be 0 art px (a leg asked to reach further than it can is dragged, and shows there), and read the cycles a second at 150 art px a second (a fight's run): under about 4 reads as running, over it as a whirr, which only a longer run stride cures (a lower `duty`, so more of the cycle in the air, or a bound in place of a trot). The sheet prints the fast pace's stride as a share of the body.
- **Gait** (Ed: "many small creatures bound rather than trot"). `gait.type` says how it goes at speed: `"bound"` for the small and springy (`hop()` or `lope()`: a slow hop, and a bound with its back gathering and stretching and a long moment in the air), `"trot"` for the heavier and the low (`trots()`, `trundle()`, `scurry()`: a rolling walk, then a trot or a patter), `"walk"` for a turtle (`plod()`), `"glide"` for the legless, `"fly"` for wings (the view beats a flyer's wings by the clock). Its slow pace should have frames with every foot down (a walk's `duty` of 0.8 gives four of the eight; the test checks): it stops on one.
- **Asleep** (Ed, 2026-11-12): "Most sleeping legends should curl up on their sides", "It should be like references", "Snail and turtle sleep in their shells." A legend curls unless its plan says otherwise (`gait.sleep`: "shell", "sphinx"); `MESH.curl` tunes the ring (`turn`, `side`, `ears`). A kind that can't curl needs its own pose, said in the report. Look at the sheet's two sleepers.
- **Sizes** (Ed, 2026-11-12): "keep the sizes". `heights` from step 1, and the sheet shows mesh and today's drawing side by side.
- **Nothing else**: no change to a kind's id, island, attack or numbers, nothing in `src/rules/`, no other batch's kinds.
- **Checks**: `npm run typecheck`, `npx vitest run src/render/meshCreatures.test.ts`, `node tools/creatures.mjs check`, and the branch's bench declaration (`frames` only).
