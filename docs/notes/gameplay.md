# Creatures in play

Part of Witch's notes (CLAUDE.md is the index): inviting, the leash and sigils, combat and sieges, creature states, the legends and their quests. Moved here from CLAUDE.md as it stood (housekeeping, Ed 2026-10-23: a note an area, so pull requests add to their own area's file, not one shared file); every rule kept. Add to this file, not CLAUDE.md, when you change this area.

- **Talk / invite**: inviting is by 💌s (issue #87; `invites`, `src/rules/invites.ts`): on the ground she throws them at the cursor (left click or 1), one at a time; each that lands fills the creature's meter (`invites.hits`, by level), and full, a wild one is happy (healed to full) and its sigil drops as a rune at its feet, which she picks up (E) to leash it. Wild legends can't be invited. The old proximity talk (on the ground she talked by herself, no button: Ed, v244; emoji bubbles taking turns for its talk time, babies 3 s, young 6 s, adults 12 s; `invite.talkRange`, `invite.cancelDistance`, `invite.talkTime`) runs only with `invites.on: false`.

- **Babies on her leash** (Ed, 2026-10-11; docs/backlog/gameplay.md "Babies"; `src/rules/babies.ts`): following her on the ground, her babies walk in single file along where she's been (her `leash.trail`), in stack order from the bottom, `leash.babies.gap` apart; while she's in a fight (a wild creature within `combat.defendRadius` after her or hers) they scatter `leash.babies.scatter` (55 m) to a quarter more off to the side away from it (the way fixed when it starts: `leash.scatter`), off the screen (Ed, 2026-10-26: "they should scatter further, e.g. outside of my camera view": at zoom 3 on his 774×448 canvas the ground in view reaches 31 m to each side of her, 24.5 m below and 49.6 m above, so 55 m clears every edge), kept on the island's land (pulled back toward her till it is: `LeashControls.land`, 6 m in from the coast) and pulling on her not at all while scattered (`loadOf`'s `scattering`); Ed: "no areas are dangerous for babies", so where they run doesn't matter otherwise; one more than `leash.babies.farAt` from her moves at `leash.babies.farSpeed` (travelling too). A baby eats only at its placed sigil, a berry within `berries.babyReach` of it (the berries she'd feed it glow brightly while a baby's sigil is next to go down), and `berries.babyCost` (1) berry makes it a youth. Its weight ignores its species' strength: `leash.weight.levels[0]` in the treetops, `leash.weight.babyGround` on the ground (8 fill either allowance), its leash's tension measured from its place in the line.

No sigil is put down and her stack and leash are unchanged; off the treehouse they're hers again and follow her. Per witch, by whether that witch is perched.

- **Loose sigil** (`g.loose`, `c.loose`): a friend's sigil scattered over a stone's clearing at its conversion (never in the wedge north of the soundsystem; `from`, the stack slot it fell from, for the view's arc; the "converted" leash event marks the moment), for any witch to pick up (leashing it); stays even if the soundsystem falls.
- **Legend tether** (`leash.tether`, `{ id, length }`): a sleeping legend's loose sigil picked up: she can't go further from the legend than where it lay; put down anywhere to free her.
- **Sigil stack**: the sigils of the creatures leashed to the witch, floating above her head, newest at the bottom. The Sigil button (E; gamepad X; touch "sigil") puts the bottom one down as a rune on the ground (a **leash point**) or picks a placed one back up. A carried relic's sigil (the gold flask) is one of the stack (Ed's playtest, 2026-10-08: "sit in the stack, and cycle when it cycles"; `stackOrder`, `cycleStack`, `leash.relicAt` in rules/leash.ts): picked up, it goes on at the bottom, cycles with the rest, and the bottom sigil is what E puts down (Ed, 2026-10-08: "if the flask is in the bottom slot, it's what you put down when you press e"): a flask, given to a sleeping legend in its clearing, else on the ground where she stands (the relic `dropped`: its sigil there, picked up again like any sigil); a creature's, as ever (in a clearing too, for its quest).
- **Rank sigils** (Ed, 2026-10-27, after mocks 3 to 9 in `previews/ed/youth-dial-*` and `rank-sigils-*`; `leash.ranks`; the default since Ed, 2026-10-27: "go ahead and push all the new sigil work", `?sigils=old` the old look and its 4 m hex grid; render/leash/ranks.ts): every leash sigil (her stack, placed sigils, happy runes, the ghost, the treetop projection, the bond and the sigils' light) is its species' bare glyph, no ring and no disc, in its rank's colour (`leash.ranks.colours`, baby to legend: turquoise, purple, red and yellow when this was written, 2026-10-27; the species' neon stays the world's: borders, rune stones, fireworks, the dancefloor, holograms, the chart), over a dark shade in the glyph's own shape, with embers rising off its strokes (more, bigger, brighter and higher each rank; a legend's white-hot). On the ground the glyph is `leash.ranks.glyph` metres across and the hex grid `leash.ranks.hex` (6). A youth in her stack has two rows of round dots off to its right, one a berry it eats on the move (`toEvolve` less the last, eaten only at a placed sigil: `lastBerry`), at most four a row, bright eaten, dim not, pulsing when ready; evolving at its placed sigil, dots ring the glyph and slide in as it whitens, a brief white flare, then it settles to its new rank's colour. Its area's rune stone (Ed, 2026-10-27: "Runestone sigils adopt the new colour scheme; they are the colour of the highest level animal in their area") is in the rank colour of the highest-level animal that lives there now (ranks.ts `areaTopRanks`, by `c.cell`): yellow while its legend is home (asleep or awake), else the top among its residents, worked out every frame, so it changes as they grow, are recruited away or come home. Her own don't count (leashed: carried or left at a sigil), nor any gone, fleeing, travelling or off dancing. With nobody home the rune is unlit (`EMPTY_STONE`: dark carving, a dim cold light, beam and motes at 0.3), rare, since an area with none of its wild youths and adults left clears and its stone turns to a soundsystem (rules/clear.ts). The stone's rune, its light, column, laser, motes and flare take the colour; the ley line, wave numbers, its pins and the rest of the per-area neon don't. Each stone is baked in the four colours and unlit (markers.ts `stoneJob`, five times the stone frames) only while the switch is on. Its rune glows brighter than baked (Ed, 2026-10-27: "Make runestone sigils glow brighter"): the stones' batch's glowing pixels times `leash.ranks.stoneGlow` (2.4; the pause page's Rune stone glow slider, live), hue kept (sprites.ts `uGlowGain`, divided back under 1 by its brightest channel), and a lit rank rune's brightest pixels go only 0.3 toward white (`STONE_WHITE`), so the rank's colour holds.
- **Sides and sizes** (Ed, 2026-10-28, settling the runestone mocks in `previews/ed/runestone-stacks/`): a sigil's colour says its side and rank. Since Ed's playtest (2026-10-29: "it's hard to tell friendly vs enemy sigils apart, especially purples/pinks/reds ... maybe baby sigils are purple (neither friend nor enemy), and the rest are more strongly red or blue"): every baby's sigil is one purple, whatever its state (wild, happy, loose), the only purple anywhere; friends' youths, adults and legends (hers, and the invited) three blues (`leash.ranks.colours`), enemies' (the wild still at their stone) three reds (`leash.ranks.enemy`), red and blue the pair colour-blind eyes keep apart. Each side ramps by intensity, not lightness (Ed, 2026-10-29, on the legend: "the colour doesn't say 'elite' to me. It should be shiny or very saturated or clearly at the end of the colour progression"): a youth's muted, an adult's strong, a legend's the most saturated and vivid of its side (a blazing red, a pure brilliant blue). Before: a lightness ramp ending in a pale legend; before that, friends pink, light purple, light blue and turquoise, enemies crimson, red, orange and yellow. Every rank sigil is its rank's size against an adult's (`leash.ranks.sizes`: 0.81, 0.9, 1, 1.35), in her stack, on the ground and over the stones: a legend's 1.35 so it's unmistakable in a stack (Ed: "Bigger is ok"). - **Sigil motes** (#1398; the coordinator, 2026-10-29, from Ed: "Given current settings, the motes from sigils don't do much"): three kinds, each with its knobs. The **embers** off rank sigils (`leash.ranks.embers`: count, size, rise by rank; `bright` times their colour; `crowd`: past that many of her sigils put down, each one's thinned by the square root, so a crowd isn't noise; render/leash/ranks.ts drawEmbers), the **burst** as a sigil's put down or picked up (`leash.placeMotes`: count, size, bright, rise, life; render/leash.ts, drawn in render/leash/combat.ts "motes"), and the **stone motes** off the next wave's runestone (`runeMarkers.awake` motes, moteBuild, `moteSize` in the picture's pixels, `moteBright`, `moteRise`; render/view/home.ts, render/markers.ts). `node tools/shot/motes.cjs` draws each on and off at the same moment on the playtest's island and reads what they add off the picture (energy, pixels lifted, peak), before (the old knobs) and after (the build's). - **Cast ring**: while she casts at a stone, or has cast some of it (rules `castShown`: within `cast.show` of it, her progress `k`, after a hit `lostFrom` and `hitAt` on her clock), a ring of `bars` (36) rainbow bars `radius` m round the stone's foot, as much of it lit as the cast has filled (clockwise from the front), the part a hit lost red for 1.2 s. The bars are an analyser of the music playing (Ed, 2026-10-28: "should move with the music"): the mix's spectrum (platform/audio/music.ts `spectrum`, an AnalyserNode of 64 bins tapped before the master gain, made on first use; read once a frame by main.ts only while the ring shows, and only while the audio runs), mirrored left and right of the ring's front, the bass at the front and the treble at the back (`barLevel`), each rising at once and falling slowly; with no audio running, a gentle idle. Rainbow motes rise out of the stone, more and brighter as it fills. The ring is stretched north-south (`ringTilt`, 1.6) against the camera's foreshortening, and its far half, behind the stone from the camera, is drawn over the stone (the `over` batch), so the whole dial reads (Ed's playtest, 2026-10-29: "I can usually only see the beginning and the end of the dial").
- **Conversion** (`convert` s, 2.4): when a stone's friend sigils fall loose (rules `g.loose`, each with the stack slot it fell from, `from`), the stone flares, each arcs down off its place in the column to where it lies (changing from the enemy colour to the friend's as it goes, if it was wild), and the column's enemies fade. **Loose sigils** then lie on the ground as friend-coloured ground sigils with a glint and embers, to pick up (a sleeping legend's a quarter bigger).
- **Chains** (Ed, 2026-10-28; render/leash/chain.ts, `leash.ranks.chain`): with rank sigils on, every leash, from its sigil in her stack (it follows that sigil as the stack cycles: the stack's own easing slides it) or its placed sigil to its creature, is a chain of links in its rank's friend colour, a link `link` m by rank, youth to legend (0.62, 0.78, 1), alternately face on and edge on. Real chain physics (Ed: "a) real chain physics that still arc above the ground"): verlet points, the links' lengths held (`iterations` passes), both ends pinned, `gravity`, and a spring (`arcPull`) toward a bow up off the ground, high while slack and flattening as it goes taut (the threads' `bond.threadArc*`); the step clamped to 1/20 s in substeps of 1/120 s, every point kept 0.15 m above the ground, laid afresh when her end jumps over `jump` m, and `damping` (0.96) for calm or lively. A slack leash is `slack` (0.12) longer than its span; it gains or loses links at the creature's end. A sleeping legend's sigil she carries (rules `W.leash.tether`) is drawn at the bottom of her stack (never cycled), its tether a chain of links `tether` (1.3) times a legend's from there to the legend, as many as its length when she picked it up, so it hangs slack as she comes closer and, brighter, pulls straight when she's held at its end (`W.pinned`). Babies have no leash and no chain (Ed, 2026-10-11; again 2026-10-28: "babies stay unleashed and chain-free"). Each link is turned along its span as the screen shows it (one of `LINK_ANGLES`, 8, over half a turn: chain.ts `linkAngle`), so a chain reads as joined whichever way it runs (Ed's playtest, 2026-10-29: "the links don't join together when seen from all directions, esp north-south").

- **Attack grammar**: attacks as small trees of composable modules (Ed, 2026-10-27: "Put the focus on composability for these attack systems — and on telegraphing"; `docs/plans/attack-grammar.md`, `src/rules/attacks/`): cast (windup, cooldown, casts before a rest, the body's tell), telegraph, emit pattern, body, path terms, speed profile, life, hit (damage, effect, friendly fire, blink-dodgeable), `on` events spawning child attacks, pack interplay, beams, areas, traps. `config/attacks.json` is the source (an attack may `base` on another); `node tools/attacks/compile.mjs` compiles the ones today's runtime runs into `config/combat.json`'s `attacks` (run it after editing; `src/rules/attacks/attacks.test.ts` fails until it's run). `parseAttack`/`serialiseAttack` read and write them as JSON for the Fight Arena; every attack's first warning comes at least the **reaction floor** (`REACTION_FLOOR`, 0.45 s: DECISION FOR ED) before its first harm, through every child (`readability`; under it, lengthened or, `strict`, refused). The **engine** (`rules/attacks/engine.ts`) runs what today's runtime can't: a cast's telegraphs from its windup's start (plain geometry and timing, `CombatState.spells.telegraphs`) and the caster's tell (`spells.tells`), then at the release its pattern's bodies flying their path terms at their speed profile, hitting, piercing, bouncing, sticking, raising `on` events whose children are made and released in turn (capped: `CAPS`), areas striking once and zones ticking; pooled, seeded, its events in `spells.events`. A creature given one (`Creature.spell`, by name from the library: the arena's `?arena=...&attack=<name|JSON>`, or `window.witch.arenaAttack(json)`) winds up as any does and fights on its own, without its kind's special move or its pack's turns. Every species has its own in the grammar since #1326 (Ed, 2026-10-27: species defaults by biome and island phase; `config/species-attacks.json`, `rules/attacks/species.ts`, `speciesSpell` in rules/attacks/combatWorld.ts; the tuning's `attacks.species`, `?attacks=old` the flat ones from config/combat.json): its ordinary strike, its own moves (charge, leap, swoop, burrow) kept, and every kind's old attack against a soundsystem (rules/combat.ts); `docs/plans/attack-species.md` maps them.
- **Siege**: a woken area's wild creatures marching on its new soundsystem (`combat.sounds`, health `combat.soundsystemHealth`; home is "home", the dancefloor). A fallen one is **ruined** (its party over, no wave wakes it again); its besiegers march on to the next-nearest. The run is **over** (`game.over`) when none stands. When a soundsystem comes, its area's wild babies turn happy and dance; when it falls, its happy babies run off home for good (leashed ones stay hers), and its besiegers, marching on, no longer count as their home area's kin (Ed, 2026-10-06).
- **Wild watch / chase**: coming down in a wild area, its animals stare at her for `wildWatch.time` (the warning, its red rising) before they attack; she can walk or rise out in time and nobody follows (`rules/wildWatch.ts`). Once they attack, they chase her out past their edge as far as `combat.leaveArea`, and a 💌 of hers landing on one provokes it (`wildWatch.provoked` seconds) to come out after her too, so sniping or kiting from just outside the edge doesn't work (Ed, 2026-10-13; the pause page's "#770 Chase" sliders).
