# The world's art

- **Collectible mushroom art generator** (Ed, 2026-10-30): `art/mushrooms.js`, exported from `art/generator.js`, makes seeded single-cap mushrooms in nine colour families at half to two-thirds of `witchHeight(style)` on the same art grid. Stem bend/twist, width, height, cap shape and glowing spots vary; `bakeMushroom` returns standard albedo/normal/mirrored-normal canvases plus a ground anchor at scale 1. The spots use existing emissive materials. Initial biome colours are editable in `BIOME_MUSHROOMS`; no recipe or collectible placement change is implied. Preview and check: `tools/mushrooms/README.md`.

Part of Witch's notes (CLAUDE.md is the index): area types and recipes, layouts, flora and trees, props and scenes, party objects, the dancefloor and soundsystems, paths, the art style, the pixel wind. Moved here from CLAUDE.md as it stood (housekeeping, Ed 2026-10-23: a note an area, so pull requests add to their own area's file, not one shared file); every rule kept. Add to this file, not CLAUDE.md, when you change this area.

Saved drawn pictures now use one source fingerprint per art-job kind (Ed, 2026-10-30), built by `tools/artJobFingerprints.ts` from the actual `ArtJob` union and `runJob` branches. Drawing inputs follow referenced bindings and re-exports; unknown namespace uses or guards retain whole modules, and unresolved dynamic imports fail closed. Code evaluated during module initialization is shared conservatively, including its referenced declarations, declaration kinds and evaluation order, without binding unrelated drawing-function bodies from the same file. `tools/art-cache/map.mjs <output.json>` writes reusable whole-file and conditional declaration maps; `affectedArtJobs` compares actual fingerprints because filename-only maps are upper bounds. Per-species registry pruning is deferred until it can preserve initialization semantics.

`render/artCache.ts` keeps the existing style, job id, seed, scale and biome inputs, and party looks remain unpersisted. The `art-kind-v1` key prefix causes one cold migration without deleting IndexedDB. Missing job hashes skip persistence safely. Only short hashes are injected into the game; analysis runs at build time. Mechanical helper moves preserve the old artBuild exports; `tools/art-cache/parity.mjs` compares complete house/creature/area job outputs without a browser. This changes cache reuse, not drawing algorithms, worker scheduling or arrival readiness; no loading-time speedup is inferred from source-history counts.

- **Area type**: one of the kinds of area: Ed's 30, defined with their art in `art/areas.js` (his columns: floor, wall objects, small objects, big objects, historical set piece, creature; set pieces are phased out of normal play, Ed 2026-10-29). `config/area-types.json` adds the game's own numbers per type (how thick its big objects stand).
- **Wall objects, small objects, big objects (legacy set pieces)**: Ed's columns. Big objects stand like trees (trees split into top and bottom halves; mounds, boulders and logs whole); small objects scatter like undergrowth; wall objects stand where areas meet and block nothing; set pieces are phased out of normal generation (`setPieceChance` 0, Ed 2026-10-29); their recipes and art remain for comparisons and previews.
- **Layout**: an area type's `layout` in `art/areas.js`: how its trees and undergrowth are arranged (pattern, density, clumping, glades, height mix, lean, terrain, decorations, a one-line feel); data the prototype's layout engine reads. Its **pattern** is one of eleven (`LAYOUT_PATTERNS`: scatter, groves, rows, rings, thicket, lone, stands, edgeOnly, and since 2026-10-13 glades, a full wood with round holes in it; hedges, trees along thin winding lines with open ground between; grid, rows crossing rows along `along`, an orchard), each a density mask the engine lays over the area (`patternMask` in `rules/forest.ts`, `src/rules/layoutPatterns.test.ts`). The art check's layouts line wants every area's layout its own (its pattern, density, clump, glade count, tallest class and terrain together unlike any other's: Ed's intent, each area different) and no pattern on more than a quarter of the areas (8 at the least: the old cap of 8 couldn't hold 61 areas over 8 patterns), so a new area takes a pattern few have.
- **Area blend**: the planting blends across area borders, look only (`plantType` in `rules/forest.ts`; the tuning's `areaEdgeBlend`): each tree and bush takes its area type from a point up to `width` metres off (80 since Ed, 2026-10-26: "The effect that makes tree placement from neighbouring areas blend should be increased"; 40 before, 20 before #887), mostly its own random draw (`stray`) so kinds interleave plant by plant, and the whole band can wander by `warp` metres over a `warpScale` noise (0 since Ed, 2026-10-26, on v4618: "I don't think tree blending borders need to wander, as the area edges themselves are already noisy"; it was 30). The pause page's Area blend slider sets `width` (it reloads). Which area you're in, creatures, partifying and the party border keep the exact partition; so do the tufts by default (`tufts` 0: the share of the band they blend over, `rules/groundcover.ts`; at 1 they blend as the trees, but a cave mouth's bare floor taking a grassland's thick grass read as wrong), and a tuft's cell (a legend circle's floor, an arena) always.

- **Country pieces**: the farm and street decorations and the small scenes' own pieces (`art/country.js`): tractor, bales, fences, gate, signs, lampposts, a bus shelter and the like; single pieces, overgrown, no text.
- **Scene**: a few related pieces arranged together that count as one piece on the map (`art/scenes.js`), listed as reused piece sprites with positions in metres and a footprint, never baked as one sprite; it faces one of two ways (as authored or mirrored). Groups of fences and of hay bales are scenes. Large scenes (cemetery, car park, scrap yard, places of worship, castle and classical ruins) are built from **landmark pieces** (`art/landmarks.js`); a building you can fly into is two pieces, its far and near halves. The **classical ruins from a seed** (`CLASSICAL_PLANS` in `art/landmarks.js`, `"<plan>~<k>"`, 3 seeds each; Ed, 2026-10-11): large plans (tholos, colonnade, podium, pediment, portico, theatre) as scenes `temple-<plan>~<k>`, in the featured pool (`scenes.featured.ids`, `"temple-*"`), and small ones (columns, lone, doorway, altar) as scenes `ruin-<plan>~<k>` in many area types; every classical piece draws whole, never split like a tree.
- **Party objects**: the small pieces scattered many times over a partified area (`art/party.js`) so it reads as party ground, even in the dark: litter, balloons (shiny, never glowing, recoloured by palette, bobbing), light sources (neon or warm), campfires (animated, with point lights), furniture and set dressing, the picnics (class `picnic`, Ed's round 11: blankets in gingham, stripes, tartan and plain with baskets, cups, cakes, plates and fruit on them, and picnic tables with benches or chairs round them, a cloth on some, each made by a small generator from its pattern and contents), tall balloons (single, weighted and bunches high on long strings), and the home stretch's decorations (class `home`: stepping stones, a mushroom ring, lantern posts, path lights, crates, flower pots, a signpost, a branch arch; Ed's scenery review, 2026-10-19, cut ten: the fairy bush and branch, the LED cube, the standing mirror ball, the UV rock and stump, the hanging fairy lights, the party tent, the inflatable ring and the speaker stack), by size class; neon ones bake in any party neon. With the prop generator (the tuning's `partyObjects.generated`, which the game always sets) the prop generator's seeded variants (`gen-bunting-*`, `gen-balloons-*`, `gen-lanterns-*`, `gen-lanterns-hanging-*`: `PARTY_PROP_GENOMES` in `art/props/genomes.js`, built in `art/party.js` `PARTY_GEN`) stand in for the hand-made bunting, balloon bunches and paper lanterns they `replace`. **Jack-o'-lanterns** (Ed, 2026-10-26: "a few kinds of jack-o-lanterns that cast a bit of warm light"; class `small`, so loose pieces in any party area and at home): `jack-o-lantern` (a grin), `jack-o-lantern-grim` (tall, slanted eyes, a jagged mouth), `jack-o-lanterns` (a big one and a small surprised one) and `jack-o-lantern-stack` (three stacked, each its own face); giant pumpkins (about 0.8 m across) so a carved face reads at all, built by `pJack` from ribs round a middle, each face (`JACK_FACES`) carved on the side the sprites are drawn from (model3d.js `YAW`, `PITCH`) in candle gold (`GLOW`, its core `RUNE`), and each with a warm orange `pointLight` (3 to 3.5 m), so it's one of an area's real lights (`partyObjects.lightsPerArea`). **Party clusters** group 3 to 8 of them in the scenes' format.
- **Attack effects**: the art a fight draws (`art/effects.js`, `effectSprite(id, { frame, variant, zoom })`): projectiles, beams (start, a repeating loop, end), pulses (the quake ring, the screech, upheaval and slam rings, a spore cloud), webs, the mole's mound, charge dust, stun stars, strong ("!!") and resisted ("tink") hits, trait marks, telegraphs (a target circle, a line, a wind-up flash, the quake's reach), hit sparks and the slow and knockback marks; in greys with a tint mask (the wild red, the party a creature's neon), light (for the dark) and dark (for bright ground), at a ground and a treetop zoom. `ATTACK_EFFECTS` says which each attack uses.
- **Attack look**: how an attack is dressed, whatever its delivery (Ed, 2026-10-27, the spell gallery: previews/ed/spell-gallery): four layers, its delivery and side effect from the rules, its **element** (one of twelve: fire, frost, venom, moonlight, storm, earth, shadow, bloom, and since the species picks (2026-10-27) water, sand, wood, wind; an attack's own `body.element` tag (`config/species-attacks.json`) first, its other names through `aliases` (night→shadow, web→moonlight, claw→wind, gold and bone→sand, seed and thorn→wood...), else `config/attack-look.json` `bySpecies`, else its kind's biome by `byBiome`, else `default`; `elementOf` in `src/rules/attackLook.ts`) and its power by level (`spellPower`). An element is a palette of four (dark, mid, bright, hot) and its parts by name: core, trail, impact, bits, mark, beam, area (`attackLook.ts` lists the names; `spellsProblems` checks the file, in `npm test`). `art/attackLook.js` bakes every element's parts into one 768² atlas at load (tallest cells packed first) (`attackLookAtlas`: cores in 4 frames at an adult's and a legend's size, 3 bits and 3 beam beads in 2 frames, a ground mark and 2 area tiles; each in colour and in grey for a party animal's neon), with the **danger frame**'s pieces (white `warn` pixels in a dark edge, strung into marching dashes; pips, brackets, the wind-up's tick, chevrons, the safe circle's hatch, the "now" flash; never red, the adult rank colour) and the side effects' motifs (slow chains, stun stars, knockback shockwave, confuse spiral). `node tools/attack-look/sheet.mjs <png> [scale]` draws the atlas on night ground and lit grass. In play, `render/leash/attackLook.ts` draws it all from one pool (5 instanced layers: light and paint, standing and on the ground, and the danger frame over the crowns), each atlas pixel `scale` (2) art pixels so it reads at the play zoom; `render/leash/combat.ts` dresses today's attacks with it (`?look=old`: the red dots, lines and rings it replaced) and `render/leash/attackSpells.ts` the attack grammar's spells (bodies, zones, beams, telegraphs moved with what they follow, tells, events). `AttackLook.telegraph` draws any of the grammar's telegraph kinds from its shape alone. `?lookdemo=telegraphs` loops every telegraph kind round her, `?lookdemo=elements` every element's bolt, hit, beam and quake, `?lookdemo=species` sixteen species' attacks (one of each look, and the new elements) flying and landing. Tools: `node tools/attack-look/shots.cjs` (frames of the showcase or, `DEMO=none QUERY=...`, any page: `QUERY='arena=wolf*1@1,toad*3@2&attack=<json>'` a grammar attack in the arena) and `node tools/attack-look/cost.cjs` (the leash view's draw time and the look's quads a frame, with and without the showcases; 2026-10-27 on the cloud: none 0.2 ms median, elements 0.5 ms median and 2.1 ms p95 at about 720 quads, 1,200 at most). A body's **look** tag (`looks`: acorn, pinecone, log, pebble, dung, can, leaf, spike, beak, sting, feather, icicle, roll) draws its core as that thing (`LOOK_CORES`, baked in its own palette and in grey: none of its own, it takes its element's bright colour), mirrored as it flies left; its trail and hit stay its element's. (Not to be confused with her hat and broom **spells**, `src/rules/spells.ts`.)
- **Path kinds**: the ten kinds of path (`art/paths.js`), each a ground-space strip the prototype sweeps along a spline, with end and junction patches and 3D edge props; the railway adds points, a broken end, a crossing and landmarks.
- **Hero dancefloor**: the round floor of 30-across glass tiles in a carved stone rim (`art/dancefloor.js`), lit by the prototype's tile-lighting engine: a pattern library (shapes, loops, fills, the switch-on sequence, one shape per area from its creature's sigil, and the World Tree's; in play only this island's kinds' shapes, `islandKinds` in `rules/dancefloor.ts`: Ed, 2026-10-11), each with its beats, palette of neons and party level (1 calm to 4 full rave), transitions (wipe, iris, dissolve, burst), and the tile looks (unlit, lit at three intensities, grout, rim).
- **Soundsystem generator**: every area's soundsystem its own (Ed, 2026-10-08: "a variety, for them all to point towards the dancefloor ... the ones further away to be larger ... we don't see any exactly identical ones on the map"; `art/soundsystemGen.js`): a genome per area, seeded from it (profile stepped, straight, tower or wall; 2 to 5 tiers, more further out, walls at most 4; each tier's kind, blocks and sizes; one of 11 crystals and 6 stones, never a crystal near a warm stone's hue; moss, shards, runes, a lean; a **projector** on top, lens, crystal, orb or prism, its top the sky hologram's anchor), dealt as one set a map so no two alike (`soundsystemSet`, every pair 2+ visible traits apart); turned along the line to the dancefloor showing its front (Ed: "always show the front"; a soft limit by profile, walls least) and sized ×0.8 to ×1.6 by distance (the tuning's `soundsystemGen`); three damage stages from its health before destroyed (`DAMAGE_STAGES`), each stack breaking its own way. - **Dancefloor speakers**: the ring of 12 single-column speakers round the dancefloor, replacing its stones (`dancefloorSpeakerSprite` in `art/soundsystem.js`), the far half facing the centre and the near half facing out, so every one shows its front; drawn at three angles and mirrored; playing, damaged and destroyed.
- **Tufts**: tiny ground-cover sprites (8 to 13 px) the prototype scatters thickly over an area's floor (`art/tufts.js`, `tuftSprites`/`bakeTufts`): grass, long grass, fern, heather, rushes, moss, clover, needles, litter, pebbles, and rarer flowers and mushrooms, each with its share of the area's tufts.
- **Sway mask**: a map the size of a sprite (`art/sway.js`), its grey (R, B) 0 where it stays still in the wind (trunks, rock) up to 255 at the leafy tips, its G the **pixel-wind code** (`swayCode`: a region's phase and stiffness); trees (whole, top and bottom), leafy props and tufts carry one, exported as `.sway.png`.
- **Pixel wind**: the game's sway (`src/render/sprites.ts`, by the code in the sway mask's G): each region of a sprite (a crown's blob from the genome generator, else a cell of about 10 by 8 px) moves whole, a whole art pixel at a time (at most 2), out of step with the others by its own phase, as far as its stiffness lets it. `windShift` (`art/sway.js`) is the same rule on the CPU, for previews and the check.
- **Tall pieces**: the sparse, tall things in the open areas without trees (`art/tall.js`): snags in the log pile, cairns and standing stones on the moor, pillars and a tall cairn at the stone shrine, rock spires and a stalagmite in the ravine; `big` entries flagged `sparse` (their share of the area's big objects).
- **Tree species**: the kinds of tree in `art/trees.js` (`TREE_SPECIES`): the original six and fifteen UK species (oak, beech, ash, lime, sycamore, horse chestnut, rowan, alder, Scots pine, yew, hawthorn, holly, hazel, weeping birch, larch), and the journey's since (53 in all); each area names a main species and often a minor one.
- **Area flora**: each wooded area type's trees (`AREA_FLORA` in `art/flora/areas.js`): 3 to 6 species with their shares, its main kind first, and its palette (leaf saturation and brightness, times the species' own; the hue is the area's `leaf`); `areaTreeVariants` deals the species into the area's ten tree variants by share (`floraSlots`), each with the area's own tree character. The fantasy species grow only as a minority where magic suits (glowcap in the hazel rings, vinewood in the garden and the holly thicket, the crystal-leaf tree at the cave mouth).
- **Plant genome**: a tree or bush species as data (`PLANT_GENOMES`, `BUSH_GENOMES` in `art/flora/genomes.js`): its form, the generator that grows it and its params, the style rules it bends, its life below the crown, its colours and its height-class growth. `TREE_SPECIES` and `bush` are grown from them. **One file a tree species** (Ed, 2026-10-13, so builders adding trees don't collide): `art/flora/species/<id>.js` exports its record as `PLANT_<ID>` (a named export: the Art Lab's inliner can't inline default ones), gathered by the generated `art/flora/species/index.js` (`FLORA_GENOMES`, sorted by id) that `PLANT_GENOMES` spreads; the bushes stay in `genomes.js`. Its baseline is one file a species too, `art/flora/baseline/<id>.json` (`bush.json` the bushes'), written by `node art/flora/fingerprint.mjs` and read merged by `art/check.mjs` (`art/flora/baseline.mjs`). Add a species as a new file, then `node tools/data.mjs flora index`; `flora check` (in `npm test`, `src/creatures.test.ts`) fails on a stale index, a record left in `genomes.js` (a merge: `flora adopt` moves it out), a file without its export, an old one-file `baseline.json` back, or a baseline file no species owns. The **genome generator** (`art/flora/blob.js`, generator `"blob"`) grows a tree from levels of branches (Weber–Penn style) under a crown of 3 to 9 lit **blobs** filled with **leaf stamps** (t3ssel8r style); it draws the ten newer species, three of them fantasy (glowcap, crystal-leaf tree, vinewood), and since #119 the twelve that had bespoke generators (broad, fir, willow, birch, tree fern, field maple, alder, pine, yew, holly, weeping birch, larch), with crowns filled through an envelope (`crown.fill`: tiers, cone, column, dome; `crownLine`), curtains, trunk bands, a pine's orange upper trunk (since Ed's 2026-10-29 report of a light brown band at the top of the Old pinewood's trunks: a darker warm brown, value .48 not .72, keeping the bark's dark marks, and coming in over an 8 px ordered dither rather than at a line, as in ground mode the crown's cut away and the hard-edged flat orange read as a band; 72% fewer orange pixels in a shot there), frond scars and fronds. Only `broadleaf` (nine species) is still its own generator. Under the pixel-art styles (`style.artStyle`, the game's `?style=bold|ref`, art/stylise.js) the blob trees stylise themselves: leaves and bark in 3 hue-shifted tones (shadow towards blue-violet or red-brown, light towards cream; `ref` one tone family), the crowns in bigger, fewer stamps lit with no jitter and clustered (`clusterLeaves`: no lone leaf pixel), and their sprites marked `stylised` so bake's post-pass leaves their tones be. `node tools/flora/species-sheet.mjs <png> <species|all> <set/stylise,...>` draws species side by side. `?flora=new|fantasy|all|<ids>` grows those species in every wooded area (carried to the art worker in the style); the lab's Flora panel shows them.
- **Art style / art pixel**: the pixel-art treatment baked into every sprite (`art/stylise.js`, applied in `bake` when the style's `artStyle` is set): `?style=ref` (bold, the default: 3 hue-shifted tones per material, clusters, a selective outline; ref: Ed's boar reference, one tone family, a strong near-black outline with interior lines) and `?px=3|4|5` (the tuning's `pixelSize` for one load). - **Prop generator**: props built from a genome and a seed, like the plants (`art/props/`, #119): `PROP_GENOMES` (`genomes.js`) gives each kind its ranges, choices and colours in metres; `propPiece(kind, { seed, ... })` (`generator.js`) builds a variant in 3D at the witch's scale. A pool's shore, a mushroom ring's grass and a stone circle's floor take the area's ground colours (`groundColours`, `art/ground.js`). Always on in the game (`style.propGen`; the hand-made `?props=hand` retired, 2026-10-07), it stands in for the areas' standing stones, cairns, pools (`water`), stumps, logs, fungi, henges, boulders and mounds (`propFor`), three shapes of each. `node art/preview.mjs props all <png>` and `areaprops moor,muddy-forest <png>` draw them; `node tools/props/ingame.cjs <area> <dir> "props=gen"` shoots them in play.
