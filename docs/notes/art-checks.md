**Art on pull requests** (Ed, 2026-11-12, answering #1975's question 5: "(b) also run a narrowed art check on PRs that touch art"): **Art check** (`.github/workflows/art-check.yml`) also runs on every pull request, its own workflow beside CI, so CI's run ends without it and no build, deploy or release waits on it (Ed, 2026-11-01: "I don't think art checks should hold up a build"); a failure still shows red among the PR's checks. Its `art plan` job's first step diffs the PR against its base's tip (the first parent of GitHub's merge) with `node tools/checks/which.mjs --base HEAD^1 --json`: `config/checks.json`'s art-check paths say whether the change needs the art check, and `tools/art-check/scope.mjs` how much. A PR that changes no art skips at once (the plan job alone, under a minute; its summary says why). Creature and area folders alone run `node art/check.mjs --only <ids> --no-lab --times` in the `narrowed` job on one runner (the report's FAIL and summary lines in the job summary, the whole log and the silhouette sheet in its `art-check-narrowed` artifact). Shared art code takes the nightly's full path: the fingerprint plan runs, over the three shards, only the tasks whose inputs changed since a cached success, and the `art check` job proves the coverage (artifacts as below). Release PRs are narrowed over their whole diff, the art all their PRs changed, combined; a publish PR (`claude/publish-snapshot` or a `Publish:` title, prototype into main) skips, carrying releases their own release PRs checked. Timings, measured on #1980 (2026-11-12): a PR with no art, 14 s for the whole run (the plan job 10 s); `--only fox --no-lab`, 9 min 17 s for the whole run, the check itself 8 min 8 s (sprite checks 383 s over three pages, the art lint 44 s); the nightly full check, about 42 minutes over three shards (the longest 40 minutes; about 86 runner-minutes). On that day both failed on the witch-on-foot sprite check, red nightly since 2026-11-08 (issue1476's standing comment), so every art PR shows red until it is fixed.

Art sharding (Ed, 4 November: “split, keep nightly”; draft #1834): the advisory art workflow pins every job to one resolved source SHA. It partitions the complete fingerprint plan into three shards, retains the auxiliary/lab checks as one unit, and publishes a checked-tree proof only after every required shard and task record validates. Exact-tree and all-cached runs still skip rendering; unknown dependency analysis retains a whole-check fallback. Nightly/manual scheduling and the single standing nightly failure/recovery comment remain; no release waits on art. Historical durations guide packing, not a measured speed claim. Local validation is source/orchestration only; actual cloud art execution remains unverified. Logs and available silhouette sheets are retained in each art-check-shard artifact; the combined art-check artifact holds the plan and collected logs.

The art generator (`art/`, entry `art/generator.js`) and Witch Art Lab (`tools/art-lab/`) are checked in full by **Art check** (`.github/workflows/art-check.yml`) nightly at03:17UTC against the exact current `claude/prototype` SHA, or by manual dispatch on any chosen ref (Ed, 2026-11-01). No build, deploy or release waits on it, and builders are not required to run it. Nightly failures update one standing comment on issue1476 with the source SHA and failed coverage; the coordinator routes the follow-up. Fingerprint caching through `tools/art-check/affected.mjs` and validated exact-tree reuse remain: missing task results run, unknown dependency layouts fall back to full coverage, and lab pages/non-task checks remain a conservative separate unit. The plan, log and available silhouette sheet remain in the run's `art-check` artifact.

- `node art/check.mjs` — builds the lab, opens the source page and the built page in headless Chromium (no script errors, a bestiary card for every species, the scene drawn; each on a page of its own, drawing while the rest runs), and draws everything the art generators make (every creature at every level, face, texture and silhouette, the trees and bushes, the area types and their ground cover, the witch and the party witches in every pose, the hats, the sigils, the dancefloor and its speakers, the treehouse, the props, scenes, relics, paths and party objects, the attack effects, the sleeping legends, the live rig) and holds each to its rules: sizes by level and against the witch, what may glow, anchors inside the sprite, strips and loops repeating exactly, no flecks, silhouettes apart, every material coloured, and the art lint. What it checks and every number it holds them to are the check's own, not copied here (the docs pass, 2026-10-28: a 12 KB copy of them stood here, its numbers drifting from the code's): `node art/check.mjs --list` names its tasks, each line of its report says what it checked, and each check's comment in `art/check.mjs` says why. The rulings in it: the style's `creatureScale` 1.5 that its sizes against the witch are times (Ed, 2026-10-08); the party relics at a third of their built size (2.3 to 5.4 m across: Ed, 2026-10-11); two tree species growing in one area looking alike from the treetops a warning, not a failure (the coordinator for Ed, 2026-10-14; before that every pair across all the trees had to differ); a species' growth that is only scale a warning (`WARN growth, not scale`) until every species clears it (the menagerie review, docs/art-review/menagerie-review.md). **Its speed** (the coordinator's ask, 2026-10-13: a full run took about 2 h on one browser page and died at the cloud's session limits): the sprite checks are named tasks and the art lint goes a species or an area at a time, dealt out over a pool of pages (`--pages N`, by default the machine's cores less one; each page its own renderer process) so every core draws, the report put back in the tasks' order so the output reads the same; `--only fox,fen` keeps every check that walks the species or the areas to those ids (a builder's own creature and area), `--no-lab` skips the two lab pages, `--tasks a,b` runs only those tasks (`--list` names them), and `--times` prints what each task, lint unit and pass took. A builder's quick run: `node art/check.mjs --only <species>,<area> --no-lab`: about a minute when written; on 2026-11-12 `--only fox --no-lab` took 20 minutes on a four-core cloud session (`--times`: most of it the `generators`, `stumps` and `party-gear` tasks, which `--only` does not narrow; the art lint and the flora baseline the rest).

**Style bible**: `docs/STYLE.md`, the short rulebook every piece of art follows (the grid and whole-scale drawing, the master palette and area ramps in `docs/style/`, outlines, scale against the witch, the readability ladder, contact sheets); art builders apply it to every batch. **Art guide**: `docs/ART-GUIDE.md`, the living style guide from the art director's reviews (what reads at each size, palette, silhouettes, clumps, how an area carries into its creature and legend, past failures, proposed checks). Builders read it before each art batch.

`node tools/genome/compare.mjs <dir>` compares every creature sprite (all species, levels, frames, facings, plain, in gear and woken, and the sleeping legends) with those a checkout of the old art in `<dir>` draws, pixel for pixel: run it on any change to the genomes or builders that shouldn't change the art (`git worktree add <dir> origin/claude/prototype`).

Related commands: `node tools/art-lab/build.mjs` writes the self-contained lab page to `tools/art-lab/dist/witch-art-lab.html` (git-ignored; published from `dist/` by `npm run build`, `tools/labs/publish.mjs`); `node art/export.mjs [style.json] [out dir]` exports every asset as albedo and normal-map PNGs with `manifest.json` (default `art/out/`, not committed); `node art/preview.mjs animals|trees|wind|treeheights|areas|sets|lights|soundsystems|sigils|witch|home|relics|grounds|decor|country|landmarks|scenes|partyobjects|partypatch|lineup|lake|paths|pathpieces|species|canopy|speakers|ring|disco|discostrip|discolooks|tufts|sway|partywitches|legends|effects|genome|silhouettes|partyrelics|witchgen|faces|textures <list> <png> [scale]` renders lit preview sheets. They need Playwright's Chromium (in cloud sessions, under `/opt/pw-browsers`).

**Bold sigils** (Ed, 2026-10-29: the monoline sigils can't be read at stack size, about 12 px with 1 px strokes; on the options sheet, `previews/ed/sigil-legibility/` on `claude/ed-shots`: "2x is too much, but perhaps 1.5x (or nearby sensible multiple) and then redraw?"). Each species gets a second, bold sigil for where the leash shows it small: her stack, the rune stones' columns, runes, placed and loose sigils, and the pointers (`render/leash.ts` `bareSlot`, `render/indicator.ts`). The monoline sigil stays on the rune stones' carved runes and the chart, and wherever a species has no bold one yet. They show at the stopgap's size (`leash.ranks.standScale`, 1.5). The switch is `leash.ranks.bold`, on since all 64 were drawn (Golf, 2026-10-29: `art/creatures/<id>/bold.js`, each species reduced to one feature; `?sigils=thin` brings back the monoline glyphs to compare).
- Ed's approved design pass (2026-10-29, PR #1467): polar bear's five-toed paw, unicorn's horn alone, beaver's incisors, wolf's cleaner face and squirrel's spiral tail. "Picking distinctive body parts will be key"; the other proposed replacements were not approved. Review sheets: `previews/ed/sigil-pass/` on `claude/ed-shots`. `node --test tools/leash/sigil-pass.test.mjs` checks candidate grid limits and the approved squirrel/snail and horn/hat separation; the coordinator captures the combined build in play.
- The brief: The animal reduced to its one most recognisable feature (antlers, ears, tusks, shell, wings, eye rings, a snout...): what tells it from the 63 others at a glance. A face front-on for most, the animal whole (top-down, symmetrical) where its head isn't its identity, as the monoline set does. Fewer marks than the monoline sigil: three to six strokes and a dot or two.
- The grid: Drawn for a 24 px square (`BOLD_GRID`), 2 px strokes (`BOLD_STROKE`, 2/24 of the box) and dots 3 px across (`BOLD_DOT`): `art/sigils.js` `boldGlyph` rasterises it pixel for pixel into the atlas's 32 px slot, centred. Keep at least 2 px of dark between parallel strokes (closer and they merge into a blob at stack size), and fill the box: at least 16 px across and down.
- The files: `art/creatures/<id>/bold.js`: `export const BOLD_<ID> = { strokes: [...] }` (the id in capitals), the strokes in the unit box (x right, y down, 0 to 1) from `art/sigils/strokes.js`'s building blocks, as `sigil.js` uses them: `L` polylines, `A` arcs, `C` circles, `E` ellipses, `D` dots, `pair` for a stroke and its mirror. Then `node tools/creatures.mjs index` (it adds the species to `CREATURE_BOLD` in `art/creatures/index.js`; never edit that by hand).
- The samples (the rendering builder's, 2026-10-29; Golf's to keep or redraw): cat (whiskers under pointed ears on a round head), lynx (tall tufted ears, a flared cheek ruff), owl (two great eye rings, a hooked beak, ear tufts), bear (round ears, a round muzzle with its nose), boar (a flat snout with nostrils, tusks curving up beside it), in their `bold.js`.
- The checks: `node tools/leash/bold-sheet.mjs <out.png> [id,...]` draws each species' monoline sigil as the stack showed it, its bold sigil on its grid, and the bold one at stack size in purple, blue and red: read the last three columns, not the big one. `src/render/leash/bold.test.ts` (in `npm test`) fails on a point outside the box, a sigil filling less than 16 px of its grid, ink outside a tenth to a half of it, 1 px lines (over 8% of its pixels), or two sigils sharing more than two thirds of their ink. `node tools/creatures.mjs check` and `node art/check.mjs` as for any art. In game: `?sigils=bold`.

**The witch's poses** (2026-11-12, after the nightly check failed from 2026-11-08 on a fixed pose list missing inBed, bedStretch and brush): the `witch-poses` task checks every pose `WITCH_FOOT_POSES` (`art/witch.js`) defines, so a new pose is drawn and checked without editing the check. Its `pinned` table holds the frame counts other code relies on; a pinned pose that disappears or changes its count fails, a new one not pinned passes and is named in the report's "counts not pinned".
