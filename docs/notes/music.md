The music (`src/rules/musicPlan.ts`, `src/rules/musicScore.ts`, `src/platform/audio/musicEngine.ts`, `config/music-style.json`): A sleeping legend's clearing on the ground (Ed, 2026-10-06) muffles the music (`muffled`, rules/music.ts; the tuning's `music.circle`) under that legend's own layer (`legendCircleAt`, rules/musicPlan.ts; the style's `circle`: every legend's parts and its voice family's, each species its own patterns, melody, rhythm and octave in the music's key and chords, played over the muffle on the engine's second output); There the world slows to about a tenth (the game's time scale, `g.timeScale`) and the music follows it (Ed, 2026-10-06: "the music audibly slows down"): the engine runs on game time at the time scale's rate, so the bars and the waves stay in step, its notes' pitch dropping like a slowing tape (`tapePitch`, the tuning's `music.slow`), while the legend's layer plays on its own clock at full speed, starting in step with the music and taking its chords (`only`/`circleStep` in `notesAt`): slow, spacious four-bar phrases. `node tools/music-lab/circles.mjs [species,...]` renders each walking in, the slowing and the way out (`SLOW=0` without the slowing) and writes `previews/music/circles/<species>.mp3`. The home speakers' boot (Ed, 2026-10-06) builds it: silent until the first speaker turns (`g.speakerBoot`), then the intro's twelve layers (its parts' `from`) one a speaker, each on the bar line after it turns (`bootLayers`, rules/musicPlan.ts), the low-pass opening with them; `node tools/music-lab/boot.mjs [bars]` renders the boot sped up to `previews/music/boot-build.mp3`. The music never waits for the game (Ed's playtest, 2026-10-06: "The music cuts in and out a lot"): a frame past the rules' `MAX_STEP` (0.1 s) loses game time, and the engine used to re-anchor on it and leave a silence at every hitch; now it schedules 0.6 s ahead, plays on through a hitch and eases back into step at most 5% faster or slower (`MusicEngine.slew`), holding (not dropping) a sixteenth a long stall made late, and re-anchors only on a real jump (`musicEngine.stats` counts re-anchorings, held sixteenths and unscheduled seconds); the damage drop-outs are gone (its crunch, wobble and crackle stay). After a long frame it schedules further ahead, up to `MusicEngine.maxAhead` (2 s), easing back by `aheadEase` a second, so a run of long stalls (the art baking, a new place's first frames) cuts it only once (Ed, round 16: "Music is still starting and stopping unexpectedly"); that's the live engine's way, now only `?music=live`'s and wherever the ahead player hands back (`aheadAllowed`: the slowed world, a sung voice not a recording, the pause page's Music Lab open): by default the music's drawn ahead (see **Music ahead**); the playtest log's audio samples carry its stats (`gap`, `resyncs`, `late`, `ahead`). By the sea the party gives way to the waves: within `music.beach.fade` metres of the water its volume falls to `music.beach.quiet` of itself (`musicMix`); `src/rules/musicZones.test.ts` plays the mix at the dancefloor, its ring, a partified area, the wild, the beach and the sea's edge. What actually leaves the game is measured too (Ed, round 16: "Is there a way for the game to know if anything is being sent to the speakers or not?"; `src/platform/audio/outputMeter.ts`): an analyser on everything that reaches the destination (the music's master, its legend-circle layer, the sound effects' limiter), read about ten times a second (what played since the last read), the context's state, latencies and clock against the page's (a stalled audio thread); the debug overlay's `audio` line shows them and `SILENT x s` when it's under -60 dBFS while the music should be heard (`musicExpected` in main.ts), and every such silence over 0.3 s goes in the playtest log (`silences`: game time, place, area, the mix's volume, the context's state and clock, the nearest stall). It measures what the game hands the browser, not the device's volume or whether speakers are on. Why the sound cut out (Ed, 2026-10-07: "the music is still cutting out"; `ClockWatch` in `outputMeter.ts`): the audio clock against the page's, a second at a time, slow (under 0.8) and fast (over 1.2) windows counted apart, its drift over the run by the context's clock and by `getOutputTimestamp` (what the device played), and Chrome's `playbackStats` under-runs where it has them; the overlay's `clock` line and each playtest sample's `audio.clock` give the verdict (under-runs: time lost for good; clock steps: slow seconds paid back by fast ones, nothing lost; or a true clock), and each logged silence a `cause` (`game`: the clock ran true, the game stopped sending; `clock`: the device or browser under-ran). The context's `latencyHint` is the tuning's `music.latency` (`balanced`: more room for the audio thread before it under-runs; `?latency=interactive|balanced|playback` for one load). The **safety valve** (Ed's mic test, 2026-10-07: 155 s of under-runs in 160, "almost no sound at all"; `src/platform/audio/shed.ts`): once a second it reads the ClockWatch (the browser's under-run count, else the audio clock falling behind for good) and the music's resyncs (3 in 5 s: Ed's v2186 pattern) and, while the audio thread can't keep up, sheds a step (1 the beds: ambience, meadow, pond, picnic, sea; 2 every synth and sung note one oscillator, shorter tails; 3 the reverbs short and no delay), never stepping back within a load, each step in the playtest log's `audio` and each sample's `shed`; `?audio=lite` starts at step 3, `?audio=full` turns it off. The music's steady cost (same session): its synth filters and the choir's vibrato are worked out once a 128-sample block (`kRate` in musicEngine.ts: a moving filter otherwise recomputes every sample) and its notes stop at 2 releases (−52 dB), not 4: about a quarter off the audio thread's load on wave 7 (Chromium's AudioOutputDevice thread from /proc, 27% of a core to 20% here). A note's unison voices go through one stereo panner a side at their mean place (not one a voice), and a part's filter wobble is one LFO shared by its notes (let go of each as it ends): foxtrot's measure (#523, `tools/music-lab/cost.cjs`) found those next, another 5 to 10% off the heaviest sections offline. Then (same round, to the coordinator's target of every section under about 0.5 offline) parts share what never changes between notes: a drum part's filters (each hit's envelope into them), a sung part's formant bank by vowel and its vibrato (a note's envelope before the formants; its breath stays its own: one shared noise summed differently over overlapping notes, 1 to 2 dB on the mix), and a synth part's struck chord one filter, envelope and pair of panners (not with drive or glide). Every section's loudness in the music check stays within 0.07 dB of before; offline, the whole arrangement p50 0.53 to 0.29, worst 0.79 to 0.45, breaksDark 1.15 to about 0.5. `?micCheck=1` (debug only, never otherwise asking for the microphone) also listens to the mic (no echo cancelling, noise suppression or gain control) beside the output block by block on the audio thread, finds the device's lag (0 to 300 ms) by their envelopes, and logs (`mic`; measured on the audio thread by an AudioWorklet, never a ScriptProcessor, which made the audio thread wait on the game's frames) a drop to room level the output doesn't have (a dropout after the game: the OS, the device, Bluetooth) and the mic hearing what the output didn't send; only levels are kept, it needs speakers (not headphones), and room noise and speech confuse it. `node tools/music-lab/silence.cjs [dist]` cuts the sound inside the graph for 1.5 s and checks the meter logs it (`MIC=1`, `MIC_WAV=<wav>`: with Chromium's fake microphone). `node tools/music-lab/flight.cjs [dist] [seconds]` drives the built game's music on the real AudioContext at 60 frames a second with real main-thread stalls (0.15 to 1.2 s), failing on gaps. `node tools/music-lab/build.mjs` writes the self-contained Music Lab to `tools/music-lab/dist/witch-music-lab.html` (git-ignored: `npm run build` builds it and the Art Lab's page, `tools/labs/publish.mjs`, into `dist/`, so the Pages deploy publishes them beside the game at https://edsaperia.github.io/witch/witch-music-lab.html and `/witch-art-lab.html`, a PR's under `/pr-<number>/`; Ed, 2026-10-13: the committed pages conflicted on every rebuild).

- **Music style**: `config/music-style.json`, the music as data, like the art's style file: tempo (it sets `beat.bpm`), swing, key and scales, chord progressions, synth patches, parts with their patterns, sections and the arc; every sound synthesised (`src/platform/audio/musicEngine.ts`), every tune generated from the run's seed (`src/rules/musicScore.ts`); one scale, dorian (Ed, 2026-10-09: "Keep it all in the dorian"), each stage in its own key (its `transpose`: the key changes between stages stay); a night's set darkens its breakdowns to aeolian and plays witch house in phrygian (Ed, 2026-10-11: "drop to relative minor/darker mode in breakdowns").
- **Section**: a stretch of the track (intro, forest, deep, breakdown, build, drop, dark, industrial, breaks, ether, chip, phonk, whisper, knockout…): which parts play which patterns, its chords and its low-pass. Sections change only on 4-bar lines (`blockBars`), so every change lands on a phrase. A section can lift the key (`transpose`, semitones over its stage's, still dorian). **Victory** (wave 2's finale, Ed approved, 2026-10-11; the style's `victory`, the musicPlan cue "victory" while the lap and the win play) is the drop a whole step up, with the choir, strings and a sixteenth arp over it and the claps in a conga line ("one, two, three, kick!"). It has no fill, so it loops clean as the conga dances on (`src/rules/musicVictory.test.ts`).
- **Boot-up**: the home speaker ring powering on, one by one, before the first wave's pulse sets off (at `boot.speed`: the 179 m boot path in about 45 s at 4 m/s, Ed 2026-10-09; the waves' pulse 1 m/s since 2026-10-18); it waits for the **party spell** and for her to leave her decks (`party.bootFrom`, set by `stepParty` at her first step after the cast; in a game without the spell, her first step), the pulse waiting at the treehouse till then; the first stone turns about 3 s after she leaves (Ed, 2026-10-06; `bootPulseAt` in `rules/bootRing.ts`), the boot over as the pulse comes back round to where it came on to the ring; the animals still attack, nothing grows; the wave pointer first appears when it ends (Ed, 2026-10-06: "The wave pointer first appears when bootup finishes"; the dancefloor's boot ring shows the boot), fading in as the ley line's pulse sets off, and a word under the game clock says when the speakers are up.
- **Form**: the melodies' shape (Ed, 2026-10-09: "Melodies should be 8 bars long, with a 32 bar ABAC structure, and include rests"; the style's `form`, `formPhrase`/`formChord`/`formNoteAt` in `src/rules/musicScore.ts`): 8-bar phrases A, B, A, C, round and round on the music's bars, mostly slow (Ed, the same day: "keep the melodies relatively slow, but not always. Use whole notes and triplets quite often": bar-long rhythm cells, whole notes and quarter- and eighth-note triplets often among halves, dotted and plainer rhythms (the triplets played straight), a part's `pace` of its bars from the busier cells), each half-phrase ending on rests; A is played on one instrument the seed picks from pluck, lead, chiplead, vox, bell and lgHorn and comes back the same; B (a question, ending on the 2nd or 5th) and C (its answer, on the root) are sung by the `soprano` (a female voice: the formant voice higher, softer, a new vowel each note, a wide vibrato). A section's melodic parts (role `motif`) say only where the melody plays and how loud; the form's chords (A's progression, B's ending on the question chord, C's on the answer) are every part's, the bass, arps, pads and a legend's circle layer too. The sections follow it too (Ed, the same day: "ABAC; the first two sections should be 4 on the floor, the third sections breakbeats, and the fourth a breakdown"): each arc step's `sections` name its `land` (the first time round, as the wave lands), `four`, `breaks` and `breakdown` sections, A and B one 16-bar four-on-the-floor block, the second A a breakbeat one, C a breakdown, a list's next each pass (`planBlock` in rules/musicPlan.ts; the build into the next wave still cuts in before it lands). The form's clock (`formClock`) starts at 0 through the boot, again on the block line each wave lands on, and on each knockdown's new record. A knockdown (Ed, 2026-10-09: "the music stops when she gets knocked down, and she comes back to restart it by putting on a new record"): the record scratched as she goes down (`recordScratch` in `platform/audio/deck.ts`, `sfx.deck.knock`) and the music cut there and silent (`silentAt`; the engine's gate, the watchdog told it's meant); her wait ends on the bar line nearest `knockout.respawn` (`hitWitch` snaps `ko.backAt`; `g.knockdowns`' `back`, the cue's `knockdowns`), and the new record starts on it from the top (the stage's landing section, the form's A), re-seeded (`musicSeed`: new melodies, a new A instrument, new chords) at the new tempo; the sad trumpet plays over the silence and is let go as the needle drops (the rewind at the cut is gone). `node tools/music-lab/form.mjs [section|form] [seed] [bars] [knockdown bar] [wave]` renders it to `previews/music/form-*.mp3`.
- **Night's set**: the music's arc made for each night (Ed, 2026-10-11: "Each night should be a full set, starting quite gently and building to a climax, with build-ups and breakdowns within it"; "noticeably different each wave"; `src/rules/musicSet.ts`, `g.musicSet`, planned in `newGame` by `nightFor`): a step a wave (step 0 the countdown after the boot), each dealt from the style's **genre families** (`families` in `config/music-style.json`, which names them all; each its palette window and energy range, its sections on the form, its build, bass patches, leads and voices; `calm` ones for breakdowns), by the night's **energy curve** over its time (each wave as long as its ley-line link: `energyAt`, a build, peak and breakdown cycle every `set.cycleWaves` 10 waves, each peaking higher, the last ending in the climax on the final wave) and its **palette** by the absolute wave on the 64-wave master curve (early organic and dreamy, middle techno, electro and breakbeat, late industrial, phonk, hyperpop and hard). Rules (the style's `set`): never a family twice running; a neighbouring key (a fifth either way, or the same), lifted 1 or 2 into peaks, darker (aeolian) in breakdowns; the lead (`ArcStep.lead`, the form's A instrument), bass patch (`bass`, on the section's bass parts), singer (`vocal`: a voice patch or none) and tune (`tune`, new melodies and chords) dealt so every wave differs from the last in at least two of genre, key, lead, bass, voice and density (`stepChanges`). Never the tempo (Ed, 2026-10-11: "tempo is set by the gameplay": the beat clock's), so each family sounds right at any tempo the game plays (a half-time feel where its genre wants a slower pulse: dub, witch house, phonk). The first home night (home, 10 waves) is hand-picked (the style's `curated`). The music plays `nightStyle(style, set)` (`src/app/sound.ts`). Deterministic by seed, island and waves; `src/rules/musicSet.test.ts`. The live Music Lab's **set view** shows and plays any night in miniature; `node tools/music-lab/set.mjs [island] [waves] [seed] [bars a wave] [encore bars]` renders one to `previews/music/sets/`.
- **Arc**: the music's steps through a run, one per wave (the last repeats): each wave's sections on the form (see Form), and the build that leads into the next wave and ends as it lands (`src/rules/musicPlan.ts`, the conductor). The home speakers' boot plays the intro. Hooks: while she's knocked out (`ko`) the knockout section plays; a standing soundsystem with wild creatures fighting at it adds the siege parts, by how near it is (`siegeNear`).
- **Beat clock**: `g.beat` (`src/rules/beat.ts`), beats by game time: the tempo is the style's 120 the whole run (Ed, 2026-10-09: "it stays at 120 unless you are knocked down, in which case it gains 5bpm"; no arc step has its own `bpm` now, so `beat.tempos` is 120 throughout; and the gameplay owns it, never the music: Ed, 2026-10-11, "tempo is set by the gameplay"), each knockdown adding `knockout.bpmStep` (5, no cap) from the next bar line (`knockdownTempo`), the beat continuous through every change. **Beat-time** (`beatTime(g.beat, time)`) is game time as it would run at the base tempo: anything that reckons beats as time × `beat.bpm` / 60 (lasers, the dancefloor, dancing, pulses) is given beat-time instead of time, and so follows the tempo.
- **Music mood** (Ed, 2026-10-04): the two best references for the overall mood are Grimes' "4ÆM" and Grimes and Janelle Monáe's "Venus Fly" (dreamy, ethereal layers crashing into hard, distorted, industrial drums and breakbeat energy); only their genres are used, never their sounds or tunes. `node tools/music-lab/analyse.cjs` measures every section (loudness, bands, each part soloed, spectrograms) for tuning the mix.
- **FOF voice**: the music's sung voice from Faust's singing model (Ed, 2026-10-11: "replace Pink Trombone ... with Faust's physmodels.lib FOF singing model"): Mike Olsen's `pm.SFFormantModelFofSmooth` (5 voice types, vowels morphing smoothly, breath), three detuned singers spread across the stereo field with a vibrato creeping in (`tools/voice/fof/fof.dsp`), compiled to WebAssembly by `node tools/voice/fof/build.mjs` (the Faust compiler from npm, `@grame/faustwasm`, in a temporary folder) into `src/platform/audio/fof/fofModule.ts` (committed; the Faust libraries are LGPL with GRAME's exception for compiled code, credited in `samples/CREDITS.md`), and run by our own AudioWorklet (`src/platform/audio/fof/fof.ts`: a pool of voices in one worklet, notes on the audio clock; no Faust runtime shipped). The engine's patch kind `fof` (`fofSoprano`, `fofAlto`, `fofChoir` in the style: `voiceType`, `vowels`, `detune`, `vibrato` in cents, `vibratoRate`, `vibratoDelay`, `breath`, `attack`, `release`, `octave`), the formant voice singing its notes until the worklet's loaded or with the sound shed to step 2; an offline render awaits `MusicEngine.fofReady(ctx)` first, on a secure page (localhost). FOF isn't in the game: in Ed's A/B it didn't read as a voice (its vowels' formants measure right, a at 880 Hz and 2.9 kHz, i at 2.2 kHz, u low; its line came out about 10 dB under the recorded voice's, buried in the mix). **The game's singer** (Ed, 2026-10-11: "e Recorded + ethereal will do for now ... a bit quieter", then "stick to aa and oo ... Can it go laa?" and "quieter and ... even more reverb"): the patch kind `sample`, `recordedVoice` (the woman's G4 retuned note by note, a 3 kHz presence lift; its vowels `aa` and `oo`, the recording's own dark vowel opened by lifts at aa's formants or closed by a low-pass; its `onset` `l`, a filter opening over each note's first 80 ms: "laa", "loo"), loaded with the whispers by `src/platform/audio/voiceSamples.ts` (the formant soprano singing till they're in), through the style's `voiceFx` (the ethereal preset, more hall, shimmer and freeze), the voice line joining after the music's limiter through a soft clipper (linear to 0.9: the music alone untouched), about 2.8 dB (LUFS) over the music under it, 5 dB under the A/B's e; the night's voices `recordedVoice` or none. **Voice effects** (`src/platform/audio/voiceFx.ts`, the style's `voiceFx`, on the sung part and any listed): chorus, saturation, air, a dotted-eighth ping-pong, a dark hall, shimmer (a reverb fed back through an octave-up Doppler shifter), ducking by the kick, reverse swells into phrases, frozen phrase ends, a whisper vocoder (14 bands, the CC0 whispers the modulator) and an octave double, each a level 0 to 1, none by default. The live Music Lab's Voice effects card and its FOF voice and choir; `node tools/music-lab/voices.mjs` renders the voices' A/B (Pink Trombone, the recorded G4, FOF dry, FOF and recorded through the effects, each alone and in the music) to `previews/ed/music/voices/`.
- **The singer's redesign** (Ed, 2026-10-14, docs/backlog/music.md "The singer, and the music round it"): Ed finds the vocalise unpleasant (its timbre and melody, often out of tune, too plain; it should be gentler, breathier, more dynamic). Measured: the sung line sits 7 to 16 semitones above the one G4 recording, and the chorus, octave double and shimmer smear each note's tuning by up to a third of a semitone. Approved: the **alto** (an octave down), two lead voices **A/B'd live in the Music Lab** (a sampled alto set built offline with formant-preserving shift, GPL tools offline only, against a vocal-tract worklet from Paul Batchelor's `voc`, MIT and public domain), a **narrow stepwise melody** with long swelling notes, a returning hook per night and chord-holds in breakdowns, **two effect chains by place** (pure and wet in breakdowns and builds, aimed at Lisa Gerrard's alto in Julianna Barwick's layers; processed, pitched down and chopped, in legend circles and the dark families, aimed at Holy Other and Salem), the voice out of the drops, and a tuning check; the cheap fixes (the alto, G3 to G4; the chorus and octave double off; the stepwise melody) go into the game first, the A/B after, the breakdown's wash three voices, breath a slider in the lab. Voices belong in breakdowns, builds, legend circles and the dark, not everywhere. Then voices as texture by family, then **mode by family** before any drone-and-ostinato record.

## Samples

Samples are allowed (Ed, 2026-10-11; docs/backlog/music.md, docs/music/research.md); the game plays only the voices' (`src/platform/audio/voiceSamples.ts`: the woman's G4 the singer retunes, the whispers and the alto set), the kit none:
- `samples/` holds them, trimmed and encoded (Opus in Ogg), built by `node tools/samples/build.mjs` from `samples/sources.json`. Each source is pinned to its repository's commit.
- `samples/manifest.json` (each file's source, licence, author, credit line, the source's hash and processing) and `samples/CREDITS.md` are both written by the build. Never edit either by hand.
- `tools/samples/check.mjs` (in `npm test`, `src/samples.test.ts`) fails on any of:
  - a file not in the manifest;
  - an entry with no file;
  - a licence that isn't CC0, public domain or CC-BY with a credit line (never NC);
  - stale credits;
  - the size budget passed (3 MB in all, the first kit 300 KB).
- The first kit is 19 one-shots from VCSL (CC0), about 83 KB. The dance kicks stay synthesised: no ripped drum machines.
- **Voices** (`samples/voices/`, about 414 KB): five CC0 recordings from Freesound, each its uploader's own, its licence checked on its page. The file is its public high-quality preview (the original needs a login).
  - a crowd singing "uh" on F (measured at MIDI 53.2);
  - four whispering voices;
  - soft female vocal chops (their key not yet measured, so unused);
  - a woman's steady sung note (G4, measured at MIDI 67.2), for a sampled vocalise;
  - throat singing in a cave (about 42.8);
  - a low kargyraa chant (about 37.1).

  Uploads that are a commercial product's output (a choir plugin's demo, a Pocket Operator kit) are left out, CC0 or not.
- **The alto set** (`samples/alto/`, 12 files, about 230 KB; the singer's A/B, Ed 2026-10-14): the woman's G4 shifted to every other note from G3 (MIDI 55) to G5 (79) with its formants kept, so the alto's notes aren't a slowed tape. They're **derived** entries in `sources.json` (`from` another entry, `skip`, `semitones`, `max`), made by the build with ffmpeg's Rubber Band filter (GPL: offline only, nothing of it shipped), and inherit the source's licence, author and credit; the manifest's `processing` says how.

**Voices**: `tools/voice/sing.mjs` sings a note list offline with Pink Trombone, vendored in `tools/voice/pink-trombone/` (MIT; changes marked "Witch:"):
- vowels gliding, the voice firming up through each note, vibrato creeping in;
- onsets ("m", "n", "l");
- a soprano's shorter tract (36), or a man's (44).

The library itself runs on a ScriptProcessor; the game's port is an AudioWorklet (`src/platform/audio/tract/tract.ts`, see **The singer's A/B**).

**A/B renders**: `node tools/music-lab/ab.mjs [out] [wave] [seed] [bars]` renders one passage of the game's music as it is, then with one ingredient more each time. The steps are a drone, frame drums, tape, the CC0 kit, and the vocalise (Pink Trombone, or a recorded woman's note retuned to each note), then recorded voices, and a witch-house take. Ed cut the chant, 2026-10-11. Every render is loudness-matched. There are also solo stems at the level each plays in the mix. All of it goes into `previews/ed/music/` (`tools/music-lab/ab.ts`, lab-only: it lays the ingredients over the engine without changing the game).
