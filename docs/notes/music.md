The music (`src/rules/musicPlan.ts`, `src/rules/musicScore.ts`, `src/platform/audio/musicEngine.ts`, `config/music-style.json`): A sleeping legend's clearing on the ground (Ed, 2026-10-06) muffles the music (`muffled`, rules/music.ts; the tuning's `music.circle`) under that legend's own layer (`legendCircleAt`, rules/musicPlan.ts; the style's `circle`: every legend's parts and its voice family's, each species its own patterns, melody, rhythm and octave in the music's key and chords, played over the muffle on the engine's second output); There the world slows to about a tenth (the game's time scale, `g.timeScale`) and the music follows it (Ed, 2026-10-06: "the music audibly slows down"): the engine runs on game time at the time scale's rate, so the bars and the waves stay in step, its notes' pitch dropping like a slowing tape (`tapePitch`, the tuning's `music.slow`), while the legend's layer plays on its own clock at full speed, starting in step with the music and taking its chords (`only`/`circleStep` in `notesAt`): slow, spacious four-bar phrases. `node tools/music-lab/circles.mjs [species,...]` renders each walking in, the slowing and the way out (`SLOW=0` without the slowing) and writes `previews/music/circles/<species>.mp3`. The home speakers' boot (Ed, 2026-10-06) builds it: silent until the first speaker turns (`g.speakerBoot`), then the intro's twelve layers (its parts' `from`) one a speaker, each on the bar line after it turns (`bootLayers`, rules/musicPlan.ts), the low-pass opening with them; `node tools/music-lab/boot.mjs [bars]` renders the boot sped up to `previews/music/boot-build.mp3`. The music never waits for the game (Ed's playtest, 2026-10-06: "The music cuts in and out a lot"): a frame past the rules' `MAX_STEP` (0.1 s) loses game time, and the engine used to re-anchor on it and leave a silence at every hitch; now it schedules 0.6 s ahead, plays on through a hitch and eases back into step at most 5% faster or slower (`MusicEngine.slew`), holding (not dropping) a sixteenth a long stall made late, and re-anchors only on a real jump (`musicEngine.stats` counts re-anchorings, held sixteenths and unscheduled seconds); the damage drop-outs are gone (its crunch, wobble and crackle stay). After a long frame it schedules further ahead, up to `MusicEngine.maxAhead` (2 s), easing back by `aheadEase` a second, so a run of long stalls (the art baking, a new place's first frames) cuts it only once (Ed, round 16: "Music is still starting and stopping unexpectedly"); that's the live engine's way, now only `?music=live`'s and wherever the ahead player hands back (`aheadAllowed`: the slowed world, a sung voice not a recording, the pause page's Music Lab open): by default the music's drawn ahead (see **Music ahead**); the playtest log's audio samples carry its stats (`gap`, `resyncs`, `late`, `ahead`). By the sea the party gives way to the waves: within `music.beach.fade` metres of the water its volume falls to `music.beach.quiet` of itself (`musicMix`); `src/rules/musicZones.test.ts` plays the mix at the dancefloor, its ring, a partified area, the wild, the beach and the sea's edge. What actually leaves the game is measured too (Ed, round 16: "Is there a way for the game to know if anything is being sent to the speakers or not?"; `src/platform/audio/outputMeter.ts`): an analyser on everything that reaches the destination (the music's master, its legend-circle layer, the sound effects' limiter), read about ten times a second (what played since the last read), the context's state, latencies and clock against the page's (a stalled audio thread); the debug overlay's `audio` line shows them and `SILENT x s` when it's under -60 dBFS while the music should be heard (`musicExpected` in main.ts), and every such silence over 0.3 s goes in the playtest log (`silences`: game time, place, area, the mix's volume, the context's state and clock, the nearest stall). It measures what the game hands the browser, not the device's volume or whether speakers are on. Why the sound cut out (Ed, 2026-10-07: "the music is still cutting out"; `ClockWatch` in `outputMeter.ts`): the audio clock against the page's, a second at a time, slow (under 0.8) and fast (over 1.2) windows counted apart, its drift over the run by the context's clock and by `getOutputTimestamp` (what the device played), and Chrome's `playbackStats` under-runs where it has them; the overlay's `clock` line and each playtest sample's `audio.clock` give the verdict (under-runs: time lost for good; clock steps: slow seconds paid back by fast ones, nothing lost; or a true clock), and each logged silence a `cause` (`game`: the clock ran true, the game stopped sending; `clock`: the device or browser under-ran). The context's `latencyHint` is the tuning's `music.latency` (`balanced`: more room for the audio thread before it under-runs; `?latency=interactive|balanced|playback` for one load). The **safety valve** (Ed's mic test, 2026-10-07: 155 s of under-runs in 160, "almost no sound at all"; `src/platform/audio/shed.ts`): once a second it reads the ClockWatch (the browser's under-run count, else the audio clock falling behind for good) and the music's resyncs (3 in 5 s: Ed's v2186 pattern) and, while the audio thread can't keep up, sheds a step (1 the beds: ambience, meadow, pond, picnic, sea; 2 every synth and sung note one oscillator, shorter tails; 3 the reverbs short and no delay), never stepping back within a load, each step in the playtest log's `audio` and each sample's `shed`; `?audio=lite` starts at step 3, `?audio=full` turns it off. The music's steady cost (same session): its synth filters and the choir's vibrato are worked out once a 128-sample block (`kRate` in musicEngine.ts: a moving filter otherwise recomputes every sample) and its notes stop at 2 releases (−52 dB), not 4: about a quarter off the audio thread's load on wave 7 (Chromium's AudioOutputDevice thread from /proc, 27% of a core to 20% here). A note's unison voices go through one stereo panner a side at their mean place (not one a voice), and a part's filter wobble is one LFO shared by its notes (let go of each as it ends): foxtrot's measure (#523, `tools/music-lab/cost.cjs`) found those next, another 5 to 10% off the heaviest sections offline. - **The dance way** (Ed, 2026-11-08, of the composer's first melodies: "more lyrical but not very dancey", wanting inspiration from trance anthem melodies, abstract hip-hop, witch house and Grimes: the styles' traits only, never a song's melody, riff or hook): the composer's default (`compose.melody` "dance", `compose.dance`, `DanceDef`; "lyrical" the planned phrases above, kept). Dance comes from repetition and syncopation. Where the instrument plays (every place but the singer's), it plays a **riff** (`makeRiff`, `riffLine`, `riffPhrase`): a bar repeated every bar, each bar's notes moved onto its chord with the shape kept (a note on a beat a chord tone, nudged to the nearest where the move lands off one), the fourth and eighth bars an answer that breathes (a rest at its end) and comes home to the chord's root, B the riff turned over, C its answers falling home. Its kind, dealt by the wave's seed from the night's family's list (`riffs.families`; else the section's, `riffs.sections`; else `riffs.default`): **trance**, groups of 3, 3 and 2 sixteenths (or 3, 3, 3, 3, 2, 2; dotted eighths), the answer the bar's first half then an octave leap up to a held high note, the second half of the phrase an octave up at `octaveUp` where nothing goes shrill; **pedal**, the moving notes on the even sixteenths against a gated pedal (the key's octave or fifth) on the odd ones; **poly**, Grimes' three-note arpeggio going round against the four-beat bar (sixteenths or eighths), each beat on the next of the three; **chop**, abstract hip-hop's half-bar fragment of two to four notes looped twice a bar with rests, laid back `late` (a fifth of a sixteenth); **ostinato**, witch house's sparse half-time figure: a chord tone, a step above or below it, the tone again, a fifth under. Every riff arranges the wave's **pitch cell** (`pitchCell`: four chord tones). The singer sings the planned phrase as before, or at `hook` a wave **a short high hook** (`hookLine`, `hookPhrase`: Grimes' traits, two to four pentatonic notes in the top of her window, a bar repeated with rests, ending on the question or the root, the night's own opening every wave's). **The anthem's trick** in a haunted section: the voice sings the riff's pitch cell slowly on the held rhythms (`hauntedPhrase` with a `cell`; the night's cell in B and C, so it's the returning hook), and from the drum & bass drop the instrument plays the same cell as the riff, the voice resting. ## Samples

Samples are allowed (Ed, 2026-10-11; docs/backlog/music.md, docs/music/research.md); the game plays only the voices' (`src/platform/audio/voiceSamples.ts`: Chimera's notes the singer sings, the woman's G4 the old singer retunes, the whispers and the alto set), the kit none:
- `samples/` holds them, trimmed and encoded (Opus in Ogg), built by `node tools/samples/build.mjs` from `samples/sources.json`. Each source is pinned to its repository's commit.
- `samples/manifest.json` (each file's source, licence, author, credit line, the source's hash and processing) and `samples/CREDITS.md` are both written by the build. Never edit either by hand.
- `tools/samples/check.mjs` (in `npm test`, `src/samples.test.ts`) fails on any of:
  - a file not in the manifest;
  - an entry with no file;
  - a licence that isn't CC0, public domain or CC-BY with a credit line (never NC), or our own work (below);
  - stale credits;
  - the size budget passed (3 MB in all, the first kit 300 KB; about 2.2 MB in all since the Chimera set).
- The first kit is 19 one-shots from VCSL (CC0), about 83 KB. The dance kicks stay synthesised: no ripped drum machines.
- **Voices** (`samples/voices/`, about 414 KB): five CC0 recordings from Freesound, each its uploader's own, its licence checked on its page. The file is its public high-quality preview (the original needs a login).
  - a crowd singing "uh" on F (measured at MIDI 53.2);
  - four whispering voices;
  - soft female vocal chops (their key not yet measured, so unused);
  - a woman's steady sung note (G4, measured at MIDI 67.2), for a sampled vocalise;
  - throat singing in a cave (about 42.8);
  - a low kargyraa chant (about 37.1).

  Uploads that are a commercial product's output (a choir plugin's demo, a Pocket Operator kit) are left out, CC0 or not.
- **The alto set** (`samples/alto/`, 12 files, about 230 KB; the singer's A/B, Ed 2026-10-14): the woman's G4 shifted to every other note from G3 (MIDI 55) to G5 (79) with its formants kept, so the alto's notes aren't a slowed tape. They're **derived** entries in `sources.json` (`from` another entry, `skip`, `semitones`, `max`), made by the build with ffmpeg's Rubber Band filter (GPL: offline only, nothing of it shipped), and inherit the source's licence, author and credit; the manifest's `processing` says how. Kept, to A/B, since the Chimera set replaced it in the game.
- **The Chimera set** (`samples/chimera/`, 50 files, about 1.4 MB; Ed, 2026-11-08: "Go ahead and swap them over"): Chimera's female voice singing every semitone from G3 (MIDI 55) to G5 (79), once on "ah" and once on "ooh", each note alone, 2.7 s (past the longest sung note: a whole bar at the slowest record's 100 bpm, 2.4 s), with its own onset, vibrato (30, as in the vocalise Ed heard) and release. Our own audio, not downloaded: a **generated** source in `sources.json` (the `chimera` repo entry, `"kind": "generated"`: the tool's repository, its commit 09fb776 pinned, its render options and the loudness to level to; each set entry a `lyric`, its `vowel` and a `midi` range, a file a semitone). The build renders each note with `tools/voice/chimera-notes.mjs` (`CHIMERA=<path to Chimera's built CLI> node tools/samples/build.mjs chimera-ah,chimera-ooh`; Chimera is jamesondh's physical-modelling voice synthesizer, MIT, https://github.com/jamesondh/chimera-vocal-synth, built outside the repository: neither its code nor its binary is committed), then trims, cuts and fades it as any sample, levels it to the alto set's loudness (-10 LUFS, their mean -9.8) at most -1 dBFS peak, and encodes it (Opus 64 kbps). The renders are deterministic: the same commit and options give byte-identical WAVs (the manifest's `sourceSha256`). Licence "own work, rendered with Chimera (MIT)", credited to Chimera by jamesondh; `check.mjs` takes that licence only on an entry with its render recorded (`generated`: the tool, its author, MIT, its URL and a pinned commit), its source the repository at that commit and a credit line naming Chimera, jamesondh and MIT, and refuses a generated entry under any other licence.

**Voices**: `tools/voice/chimera-notes.mjs <chimera binary> [out dir] [55-79] [ah,ooh]` renders the singer's notes with Chimera as raw WAVs to listen to (the samples build calls its `renderNote`). `tools/voice/sing.mjs` sings a note list offline with Pink Trombone, vendored in `tools/voice/pink-trombone/` (MIT; changes marked "Witch:"):
- vowels gliding, the voice firming up through each note, vibrato creeping in;
- onsets ("m", "n", "l");
- a soprano's shorter tract (36), or a man's (44).

The library itself runs on a ScriptProcessor; the game's port is an AudioWorklet (`src/platform/audio/tract/tract.ts`, see **The singer's A/B**).

**A/B renders**: `node tools/music-lab/ab.mjs [out] [wave] [seed] [bars]` renders one passage of the game's music as it is, then with one ingredient more each time. The steps are a drone, frame drums, tape, the CC0 kit, and the vocalise (Pink Trombone, or a recorded woman's note retuned to each note), then recorded voices, and a witch-house take. Ed cut the chant, 2026-10-11. Every render is loudness-matched. There are also solo stems at the level each plays in the mix. All of it goes into `previews/ed/music/` (`tools/music-lab/ab.ts`, lab-only: it lays the ingredients over the engine without changing the game).
