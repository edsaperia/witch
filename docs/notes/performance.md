**The View's build in slices** (`render/slices.ts`; Ed's laptop, 2026-10-20, `?timing=1` on v3973: `witch:view` 9.9 s, one 10 s task arriving at home after a sail, the bedroom's alarm, its music and every animation stopped): the View's island build (`View.create`, `View.build`) and a new night in place (`View.setGameSliced`) are generators run a frame's budget (8 ms) at a time, the page's frames between; the same steps in the same order as `new View` and `setGame` run straight through (three.js breaks draw-order ties by creation order: nothing is made in another order, only later), and nothing's drawn till it's whole. The heavy parts went to steps (the hills' first fill a few rows a step, the tufts and runestone atlases packed a few sprites a step, the paths' and holograms' bakes a step each) or to the art workers (`render/islandArt.ts`: the dancefloor's looks, started as the ground is made and put in at the build's end, `Ground.setDisco`; the island's runestones; the kraken and the dolphins, drawn only once they're back), which start with the page (`prewarmArtWorkers`), the build's own asked ahead of the start's art (`AssetLibrary.early`); `prepare` warms what its first frame would make (the forest round her and the party's areas, the legends' carvings) and fills the ground and its forced refresh in slices too. Each stage marks `witch:build-<stage>` (in the L log with `?timing=1`, with each build's slices and longest step). `node tools/view/build-budget.cjs [dist] [query] [throttle]` loads Ed's case (world 1268398697, home, seed 821186) and fails if a step of the build passes 100 ms (`LIMIT`, times the throttle); `SWAP=<seed>[,island]` with `debug=sail` in the query measures a new night in place too; the start's own renders are frames, reported (`PREP=1` to fail on them).

**The start's shaders** (Ed's laptop, 2026-10-21, v4022, Intel UHD: `prep-compiled` 4 s after `prep-compile0` every time, then a 5 s task freezing the chart on the first load and 2 s after a sail in place): `View.compileAll` compiles the scene's programs, the smooth effects layer's and the post passes' (`Post.compile`: on their own quad, never in the scene) in the background (`compileAsync`) and waits for them all with no fixed cap (60 s at most), then in slices reads each program's logs, uploads each texture the materials hold, and draws the first frame ahead (`warmDraws`: one object for each program and draw state, then the post passes one a step, at a sixteenth of the size, into targets), since a GL's first draw of a program does its own work then. A night in place keeps the last night's materials till the new night's are compiled (`render/retire.ts`, `holdDisposals`), so the programs both use aren't freed and made again (11 new programs a sail to 1). `build-budget.cjs` also fails on a task over 1000 ms (`FIRST`) on play's first frame, the start's and the new night's, and counts the new night's programs.
**The stall hunt** (`node tools/bench/stalls.cjs [out]`, the coordinator's ask from Ed's overlay, 2026-10-13: 24 stalls over 100 ms and 18% of frames over 20 ms in the treetops, seed 2267, Old pinewood, 56 s into wave 0): the game's own loop run free at a spot (`SEED`, `X`, `Z`, `ZOOM`; `DIST=dist-prof` from `npx vite build --minify false --outDir dist-prof`, so the functions have names) under Chromium's sampling profiler, the GPU draw a no-op (`NODRAW=1`) so a wall second is a game second, and a report of where the JavaScript's time went: self time by function, call chains, runs of JavaScript over `RUN_MS`, the browser's long animation frames and the game's own stall log (`<out>/stalls.json`, `stalls.cpuprofile`). `HEAP=1` samples the allocations too (`stalls.heapprofile`: the top allocators a frame, self and by call chain, KB a frame), `HEAP=live` only what's still alive at the end (the heap's growth, by function), and every run reports the frames' own gaps (over 20, 50 and 100 ms) and the heap's floor every 5 s. `COUNT=1` counts the live objects by kind at the start and the end, `SNAP=1` takes a heap snapshot at each (`tools/bench/heapdiff.cjs start end` says what grew, by kind, and who holds the biggest), and its no-draw mode still uploads each new texture as the first draw that used it would. **The churn hunt** (the coordinator, 2026-10-13, after the stalls: the 100 to 140 ms collections left): the frame's own garbage was 235 KB (the wave pointer's ring a hypot, an atan2 and a colour list a pixel a frame, the creatures' and berries' records and lists, the party pieces' closures and spreads, a record a light for the budget's sort, the lasers' and clouds' lists, a closure a height lookup, an update range a buffer), now about 175 KB with those kept and refilled (an inlined call's double arguments are boxed, so what's left is mostly numbers crossing calls: `inView`, the glow dots' `add`); but the collections were the heap outside JavaScript's: the atlases' pixels (albedo and normal) kept on the page after the GPU had them, 360 MB and 70 MB a minute more as art was baked ahead, which V8 answers with a full, synchronous collection each time it grows past its limit. The atlases let their pixels go once uploaded now (`render/atlas.ts`, `?pixels=keep` for the smoke test's floating check, which reads them), and a restored GL context reloads the page. What it found: every art set arriving was written to the art cache (IndexedDB) on the page's thread, 30 to 70 ms a set and the garbage collector's after it (the art workers keep the cache now, `render/artWorker.ts`); the music engine's reverb impulses and the voice effects' (a Math.pow a sample, 135 and 190 ms frames at the first speaker and the first sung note: table-driven, shared and warmed a frame at a time now); and the map table's miniature building every island's ground meshes at the decks (it keeps its round coasts). **The GPU hunt** (`node tools/bench/gpu.cjs [out]`, the coordinator's ask from Ed's overlay, 2026-10-13: ground mode in the Bluebell glade, seed 569166, at 2204, 2253, zoom 3, wave 9: "gpu 21.4 ms median", 96% of frames over 20 ms while the page's own work was 11 ms): the built game at a spot on foot (`SEED`, `X`, `Z`, `ZOOM`, `WAVES` brought on with N, `SIZE`), really drawn, with one thing switched off at a time by the tuning's live knobs and the view's own objects (the cast-shadow map, the trunk shadows, the blobs, the bloom, the tilt-shift, the ground cover; the see-through pass, the point lights, the pixel wind: `ONLY=base,nofade,...`), each for `FRAMES` frames, and a table of each frame's gap, its draw on the page, the scene pass, the GPU's own time (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`, which the cloud's software renderer has: there a frame's drawing is seconds, so it's shares, not milliseconds, that carry over to a real GPU), the draw calls and the triangles; the GPU's time by render call (the cast map, the scene, the effects layer, each post pass) and, `OBJECTS=1`, by draw call (the batches are named: `SpriteBatch.name`). What it found: the scene pass was nearly all of it, and three quarters of that one draw, the area's scenery batch, drawn twice over (its see-through pass over every quad again, discarding nearly every pixel after its taps) with the crowns hidden in the hole round her still rasterised; the see-through pass now draws only the quads that could have a see-through pixel (over her circle and nearer than her, a crown or cut trunk whose fade reaches the hole's band, scenery at the budget's edge or still appearing) and a crown wholly inside the hole isn't drawn at all (`sprites.ts` VERT, conservative: the picture is the same to the pixel). The cast map was a twentieth; `shadows.castRes` (1024) halves its side for a weaker GPU. **The ground's cost** (fix C of the perf pass, 2026-10-23: the ground's draw was 130 to 300 ms a frame on the cloud renderer, 60% of the frame at the decks): `node tools/bench/ground.cjs [out]` (`SPOTS=decks,ground:dx,dz,treetop:dx,dz,coast:<deg>`, `ONLY=`, `FRAMES=`, `QUERY=`, `GROUND_ONLY=1`) times the ground's own draw call on the GPU with each part of its pixel shader switched off in turn (its source rewritten in the page) and compares each frame with the base's, and each variant drawn back to back with the shader as built in one call (nothing stepped between: the proof a change leaves the picture as it was). What it found: code no pixel ran still cost a quarter to two fifths of the ground's time, so the lakes' code is compiled only on an island with lakes (`GROUND_LAKES`) and the beach and sea's only into a second program over the same uniforms, drawn while the beach is on (`GROUND_BEACH`, `Ground.setBeach`; a hidden twin mesh holds it so the start compiles and warms it): 16 to 24% off the ground's draw, the same pixels. **The wave landing** (`LAND=1` on `tools/bench/stalls.cjs`, Ed's ask from his L log, 2026-10-14: the audio under-ran in bursts of 40 to 140 at the wave landings of every night's first ten minutes, the audio clock seconds behind each time, and never after): the next wave brought on `LAND_AT` seconds into the profile and the seconds round it accounted for one by one (the page's work by kind, the art sets the workers sent and their time, the textures uploaded, the sound's own work, the music's continuity, the audio clock). What it found: the page's own work at a landing is small (a tenth of a core, no long frames); the workers were the load: every core but the page's busy for the night's first minutes baking every species' creature art (`prepare` asked for all sixty: 1 to 14 s each here), a queue of 50 to 100 sets the woken looks, the party witches and each landing's soundsystem then waited behind, and one rig page (the mole's, a squat with no rig parts) asked for again every frame all night. Now `prepare` asks only for the island's own species (its area types' and the night's legends'), a species without rig parts is never asked for, and while the night plays (the party spell cast) the art drawn ahead of need keeps to half the workers (`Assets.playing`, `dispatch`: an urgent ask, what the view needs now, takes them all), so the audio thread keeps a core. The benchmark and pixel-match harness (`tools/bench/`, for changes that should change nothing in the game: the housekeeping round, #122): `npm run build && npm run bench [out dir]` runs the rules on fixed seeds to wave 30 (timing every step, and a fingerprint of the game's state every 300 steps) and drives the built game frame by frame at a fixed 1/60 s through seven fixed scenes (a settled screenshot each, and each frame's own work for the two flights); `npm run bench:compare <before> <after>` checks the state part by part and the pictures pixel by pixel (a `diff-<scene>.png` for any that differ) and puts the timings side by side, failing on any mismatch. Two runs of one build match exactly (art the view needs now, asked for while the frames step, is waited for before the next frame, so it lands on the same frame on any machine: the wave-fight flake, 2026-10-21, a creature's rig page landing a chunk earlier or later moving its legs; each scene's `landed` frames are in `frames.json`, and `bench:compare` says when two runs' differ). About 45 minutes to 2 hours in the cloud's software renderer (run nothing else heavy alongside: a starved browser can die mid-run); `--rules-only`, and `ONLY=<scene>,…` for the frames (compare an `ONLY` run only with one of the same scenes: the scenes before warm the art caches, so a far crown or two can differ). **Quick** (for every refactor PR): `npm run bench:quick [out]`, about 5 minutes: the rules to wave 10, and three scenes at 960×540 with `?quick=1` (the boot, a wave fight in a debug arena, the bedroom); the optional **Bench** workflow (`.github/workflows/bench.yml`) runs it on each PR's base and head and compares, so a PR shows by itself whether the game is unchanged. **A PR that means to change the game declares what** (the coordinator's housekeeping, 2026-10-23: "Make the 'game unchanged' bench mean something"): `tools/bench/expect/<its branch>.json` (e.g. `tools/bench/expect/claude/my-branch.json`), `{ "reason": "what it changes", "frames": { "<scene>": "why" }, "rules": { "<state part>": "why" } }` (`"*"` for every scene or part; the scenes are boot, wave-fight and bedroom, a state part is what the compare names, e.g. creatures), and the Bench is green when only what's declared differs (each with its reason in the summary) and red on anything else; a declaration that didn't differ is listed as such. Keyed by the branch, so one PR's never excuses another's (`tools/bench/expect.cjs`, `compare.cjs --expect`).

**The boot freeze** (the coordinator, 2026-10-25, from Ed's playtest of v4497: "Page Unresponsive" over a black screen for a long while at the start, then it loaded): one task of 50 to 75 s on the page's thread 6 to 12 s after the load, on any machine (JavaScript, not the GPU), there since the oak house reached main (#1118; `node tools/bench/bootstall.cjs [seconds] [link]`, a 50 ms heartbeat from the page's first script and every wait over half a second, bisected across main's publish merges; a CPU profile of a `--minify false` build). **The GPU memory budget** (the coordinator's ask, Ed-approved 2026-10-26, after the zoo fix; `node tools/gpu/budget.cjs [out]`, ONLY=night,sail,zoo,tight, SEED, ISLAND, SECS, DRAW, TIGHT, WAIT): every texture and render buffer the page's WebGL context makes is counted where it's made (`render/gpuLedger.ts`: its bytes at `texImage2D/3D`, `texStorage2D/3D`, `copyTexImage2D`, `generateMipmap` and `renderbufferStorage`, by its own internal format and type, so R8 palette indices count a byte a texel; freed at `deleteTexture`/`deleteRenderbuffer`), by kind (`gpuKind` labels found through the renderer's properties: the atlases by their job's kind, the ground's, crown's, house's and ui's textures; anything drawn into, and every render buffer, "targets"; the rest "other"; the canvas apart). Its line shows on the pause page's stamp and the `~` overlay, and goes into each playtest-log sample (`gpu`), with its trouble (`run.gpu`: an out-of-memory error, a lost context). The budget (`render/view/gpuBudget.ts`, the tuning's `gpu`) follows Google Maps' rule: `mb` for the reference window's physical pixels, scaled by this window's, between `floor` and `ceil`; checked every `every` s, over it the longest-unused tree types, creature sets (a legend's batch with its kind's art) and rig pages are let go till it's under `headroom`, none used in the last `keep` s, so nothing on screen goes; each comes back from the art cache, fading in. It replaced the zoo's `ZOO_TYPES_MB`. GL's `OUT_OF_MEMORY`, read every `errorEvery` s (a read waits on the GPU), lowers the budget to `headroom` of what was held then. A lost context can't be mended in place (the atlases free their pixels once uploaded; the ground's tiles live only on the GPU), so once it's restored the night loads again from its repro link (main.ts `onContextRestored`). Measured (cloud, 1280x720, so the floor's 512 MB; seed 123): a night held 114 MB at the start (trees 47, house 23, ground 14, props 12, targets 8) and 159 MB after a minute's loop in the treetops (trees 90), nothing let go; the full zoo 75 MB at the start and 481 MB after 90 s out to its farthest islands (trees 303, creatures 76, props 61); the night under a 120 MB budget peaked at 117 MB, six tree sets let go after 27 s to 15 min unused (none within `keep`), the home's back from the cache as she returned; no pops in any.
