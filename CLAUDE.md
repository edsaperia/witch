# Witch

Witch is a new videogame by Ed Saperia (Ed, 2026-10-03). Its design so far is `DESIGN.md`. Engine (Ed, 2026-10-03): prototype in Three.js in the browser, port to Godot once Ed is happy; so the game's state and rules live in `src/rules/` with no Three.js in them, and `src/render/` draws them. Anything else not yet decided: ask Ed rather than assume, and record his answers here.

Work on Witch follows the shared rules in [edsaperia/dev-ops](https://github.com/edsaperia/dev-ops): `AGENTS.md`, `CONVENTIONS.md` and `claude/QUESTIONS-PAGE.md`. This file adds what is specific to Witch and wins where the two disagree.

## Deploys

The playable game is a static site on GitHub Pages, served from the `gh-pages` branch by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`:

- a push or merge to `main` builds the game and publishes it at the site's root, https://edsaperia.github.io/witch/ — **merging to `main` deploys**;
- each pull request from this repository is built and published under `/pr-<number>/` (https://edsaperia.github.io/witch/pr-<number>/), and the workflow comments that link on the PR; closing the PR removes it;
- documents-only changes still rebuild and republish the same game (no `docs` lane yet).
- every build writes `version.json` beside the game (its label, `vite.config.ts` `versionFile`; main's at the root, a PR's in its `pr-<number>/`); a page left open reads it on load, on pausing and every five minutes, never from a cache, and while a newer build is up says so in the bedroom and on the pause page: "A newer version (v3712) is out", with a reload button (Ed, 2026-10-14; `src/app/newBuild.ts`), and so does a menu whose code a deploy has replaced under it (Hotel's #906).
- many deploys at once are fine: each writes only its own folder (main the root, a PR its `pr-<number>/`), and one whose push to `gh-pages` is beaten fetches the newer branch and tries again (up to ten times).

Pages must be switched on once in the repository's settings (Source: *Deploy from a branch*, `gh-pages`, `/ (root)`); until then the links return 404. A deploy is verified by opening the link and checking the seed and the game load.

## The Making Of

A static site about how the game was made, at https://edsaperia.github.io/witch/making-of/ (and `/pr-<number>/making-of/` on a PR's build): `making-of/` (`site.json` the chapters, `pages/<slug>.html` each page's body, `media/` its pictures listed with captions in `media/media.json`, `assets/` the stylesheet and the game's pixel fonts), built by `tools/making-of/build.mjs` into `dist/making-of/` as the last step of `npm run build`; `node tools/making-of/build.mjs --check` (in `npm test`, `src/makingOf.test.ts`) fails on a malformed builder's note, a missing picture or link, an unlisted picture or a model's name. **Previews**: the tools write their pictures, sheets and sounds to `previews/`, which git ignores (Ed's overnight programme, 2026-10-07: the folder had grown to 416 MB): post them in the PR's comments (the REPORT or FINAL), never commit them; the best of the old ones now live, captioned, in `making-of/media/`. **Builders' notes**: as you finish a piece of work, add your own `making-of/builders/<name>.json` (what you built, what you learned, your best previews; the format is in `making-of/builders/README.md`); never edit anyone else's.

## Samples

Samples are allowed (Ed, 2026-10-11; docs/backlog/music.md, docs/music/research.md), none in the game yet:
- `samples/` holds them, trimmed and encoded (Opus in Ogg), built by `node tools/samples/build.mjs` from `samples/sources.json`. Each source is pinned to its repository's commit.
- `samples/manifest.json` (each file's source, licence, author, credit line, the source's hash and processing) and `samples/CREDITS.md` are both written by the build. Never edit either by hand.
- `tools/samples/check.mjs` (in `npm test`, `src/samples.test.ts`) fails on any of:
  - a file not in the manifest;
  - an entry with no file;
  - a licence that isn't CC0, public domain or CC-BY with a credit line (never NC);
  - stale credits;
  - the size budget passed (3 MB in all, the first kit 300 KB).
- The first kit is 19 one-shots from VCSL (CC0), about 83 KB. The dance kicks stay synthesised: no ripped drum machines.
- **Voices** (`samples/voices/`, about 414 KB): five CC0 recordings from Freesound, each its uploader's own, its licence checked on its page. The file is its public high-quality preview (the original needs a login).
  - a crowd singing "uh" on F (measured at MIDI 53.2);
  - four whispering voices;
  - soft female vocal chops (their key not yet measured, so unused);
  - a woman's steady sung note (G4, measured at MIDI 67.2), for a sampled vocalise;
  - throat singing in a cave (about 42.8);
  - a low kargyraa chant (about 37.1).

  Uploads that are a commercial product's output (a choir plugin's demo, a Pocket Operator kit) are left out, CC0 or not.
- **The alto set** (`samples/alto/`, 12 files, about 230 KB; the singer's A/B, Ed 2026-10-14): the woman's G4 shifted to every other note from G3 (MIDI 55) to G5 (79) with its formants kept, so the alto's notes aren't a slowed tape. They're **derived** entries in `sources.json` (`from` another entry, `skip`, `semitones`, `max`), made by the build with ffmpeg's Rubber Band filter (GPL: offline only, nothing of it shipped), and inherit the source's licence, author and credit; the manifest's `processing` says how.

**Voices**: `tools/voice/sing.mjs` sings a note list offline with Pink Trombone, vendored in `tools/voice/pink-trombone/` (MIT; changes marked "Witch:"):
- vowels gliding, the voice firming up through each note, vibrato creeping in;
- onsets ("m", "n", "l");
- a soprano's shorter tract (36), or a man's (44).

The library itself runs on a ScriptProcessor, so the game would need an AudioWorklet port first.

**A/B renders**: `node tools/music-lab/ab.mjs [out] [wave] [seed] [bars]` renders one passage of the game's music as it is, then with one ingredient more each time. The steps are a drone, frame drums, tape, the CC0 kit, and the vocalise (Pink Trombone, or a recorded woman's note retuned to each note), then recorded voices, and a witch-house take. Ed cut the chant, 2026-10-11. Every render is loudness-matched. There are also solo stems at the level each plays in the mix. All of it goes into `previews/ed/music/` (`tools/music-lab/ab.ts`, lab-only: it lays the ingredients over the engine without changing the game).

## What's new

The start screen shows a **What's new** panel: the last three entries of the changelog, newest first. The changelog is **one small file per change** in `config/changelog/` (Ed, 2026-10-05: so builders working in parallel don't conflict), collected at build time by `src/changelog.ts`:

- Every push that changes something Ed can see **adds a new fragment file**, `config/changelog/<yyyy-mm-dd>-<slug>.json` (e.g. `2026-10-05-bigger-areas.json`), holding `{ "version": null, "items": ["…"] }`: its bullets in plain player-facing words ("Trees no longer pop in and out"), not commit-speak. Several pushes of one piece of work may add to that same fragment.
- Once its build has a number (the playable-link comment), **write that number into your own fragment's `version`** on your next push. Never edit anyone else's fragment.
- `config/changelog/archive.json` holds everything up to round 9 and is frozen: never edit it. There is no shared list to edit.
- Fragments with the same version make one entry (the newest file's items first); the current build's (`version: null`) shows as the build being played. `src/changelog.test.ts` checks every file's shape.

## Fixing bugs (Ed, 2026-10-13)

Bring a bug back to Ed early rather than guess: he can answer questions, take a log or test in play faster than a builder can work round a gap.

- **Check the report first.** If it lacks something you need (the version, what he was doing, a screenshot, the L log), ask straight away, through the coordinator.
- **15 minutes to reproduce.** Can't make it happen by then? Stop and report what you tried and exactly what you need from Ed: a question, a log taken a certain way, or something to try in play.
- **No unconfirmed "fixed".** If you can't see the bug go away yourself, say so in the PR and give Ed a short in-game check (e.g. "load v36xx with an empty cache, start a night: is the witch visible?").
- **Make the next sighting count.** If the L log wouldn't show what went wrong, add the logging that would.
- **Visual bugs: measure, don't eyeball** (Ed, 2026-10-14, after the circle hunt). First write down what should hold (e.g. "see-through at full opacity draws the same as see-through off"), render both and pixel-diff them; bisect with that number, not by comparing screenshots in a dark forest. Try it on the cloud renderer before assuming it's Ed's machine.
- **No fix on an untested theory.** Push a fix only once the measurement changes; otherwise send Ed one build with every switch in it, not one build per guess.

**The phases of a run** (Ed's names, 2026-10-07), in order, and what marks each in the code:

- **Bedroom**: the character creator (`ui/creator.ts`), open at every load and the loading screen too: she walks her room (WASD, `ui/roomWalk.ts`) and dresses up while the forest grows behind it. `?creator=0` skips it (the tools and smoke runs).

- **Archipelago**: where every island lies on the sea chart, how far its shape is turned and the patch it grows in (`src/rules/archipelago.ts`; Ed, 2026-10-12, the journey's tessellation: "the rings can be very relaxed, a pleasing layout is much more important", "you can make the sea larger so that they fit", "keep arms and irregularity" and coasts that mirror each other "like jigsaw pieces pulled apart"; then v3: "more open sea", "the phase rings should hardly show", a minimum gap between islands, islands turned to fit, "you can have larger gaps", and "add back the arms towards the edges of the map, since there's nothing to fit with there"): made once a world (`worldOf(world)` in rules/rosters.ts, about 1.2 to 1.4 s, remembered). A packing of the islands' **foreseen coasts**: each island's own shape (its phase's lobes, arms and bays, `reachOf` with its seed) scaled to its areas (home at its full 64, so it grows into its place as islands are found), plus its buffer ring (`COAST_PAD` by phase, measured against the real coasts). Home in the middle; every island starts at its kind's bearing, as far out as its phase's share of the disc strayed by its seed (`MINGLE` 1.2 of the gap between phase rings either way, so phases mingle); then, round after round, any two foreseen coasts nearer than their sea (`GAP` 1.3 to 3.2 straits, wider where a slow seeded noise opens the sea) are pushed apart, each island pulled a little back toward its bearing and its own distance, its body (`CORE`: its coast trimmed to 1.15 times its median reach) kept a strait in from the bodies' rim and its arms from the disc's (the last `ARM_RING`, 1 km, left to the outer islands' arms out to sea), and every 30 rounds turned (`Island.turn`, 16 tries) to the turn that fits its neighbours best, its arms into their bays (an outer island's longest arm out to sea).
