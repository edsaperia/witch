A night-time pixel-art party game, built in public

The Making of Coven Rush

You are a witch with a pair of decks in a treehouse. Cast the party spell, and the party spreads out across a forest island, area by area, wave by wave, while you fly ahead inviting its creatures, calming its sleeping legends and keeping the soundsystems standing. This site is the story of how it was made.

The opening, phase by phase

Bedroom

Every run opens straight into the witch's messy treehouse bedroom, where you dress her while the forest grows out of sight. The fairy lights along the walls are the loading bar. It began on 5 October as a character creator that doubled as the loading screen {#117}, became an isometric bedroom that evening {#143}, grew to twice the size so you could walk her round it with WASD {#229}, and on the last evening was laid out at one pixel scale with the menu, under the title Coven Rush {#333} {#341}.

The Cast

The party spell is a scroll with 🎶 on it, bottom right. It stays rolled shut until loading is done. As you near it, it glows and trembles. Click it and it fills the screen, crackles and bursts, and as it clears the spell is cast. On 6 October it started out as a plain "CAST THE PARTY SPELL" button {#270} {#276}, and became the scroll that afternoon from Ed's description {#315}.

At the Decks

She stands behind her decks in the treehouse's cutaway DJ booth, the camera close in, and plays her casting pose for 1.2 s while amber and rose sparkles burst round her. The clock starts at 00:00 from the cast. The treehouse moved due north of the dancefloor on 5 October {#118}. Knocked down, she now comes back here too {#362}, and a DJing animation was being made as the four days ended {#356}.

Step-off

Her first move or rise gets her up, and the opening close-up eases out to the normal view. That first step is what starts the boot {#332}. The ley line leaves the treehouse's front, goes round the ring of speakers and off to the right, never across the dancefloor {#329}. A day earlier it had run straight south across the floor {#118}.

First Speaker

Three seconds after she leaves her decks, the pulse reaches the first of twelve small runestones round the dancefloor. The stone glows white, stretches up and springs into a speaker {#272}, crackling into life {#273}. Until then the music is silent. This speaker brings in the first of the intro's twelve layers {#277}. Ed's playtest set the three seconds: before, the pulse took about 40 s to reach the first stone {#332}.

The Boot

For five minutes the pulse runs clockwise round the ring, turning each stone into its speaker {#282}. Each speaker adds a layer of music on the next bar line, from pad and choir to gated stabs {#277}, and the dancefloor shows only the moon's phase in muted twilight tiles {#252}. The boot was Ed's answer to "the game is hard!": one baby an area and five minutes' grace, during which nothing grows {#129}.

First Countdown

With the twelfth speaker up, a word under the game clock says so, and the 🎶 wave pointer fades in {#265} {#270}. The ley line's pulse sets off toward the first runestone, timed to arrive with the wave, while the line itself grows ahead at three times the pulse's pace {#269}. Each runestone's column of light first rises as the line reaches it {#283}. The countdown is five minutes, so wave one lands about ten minutes after she steps off.

Wave One

The pulse arrives, the first new soundsystem appears, and the area is partified. On the dancefloor the full moon flares out into the party's switch-on {#252}. The new area's wild babies turn happy and dance {#254}, while its other wild creatures are enraged and march on the soundsystem {#96}. On the first day waves came every 30 seconds and covered the map in about six and a half minutes {#6}. Now they come every five minutes, along a planned spiral route {#314}.

The Party's Over

When every soundsystem and all twelve home speakers are down, there is no game-over screen. The music winds down like a stopped record, the lights go out in a ripple from home, every creature strolls home and falls asleep under little 😴 bubbles, and each area plays its own night sounds while she walks the map in peace {#323} {#318} {#320}. That came from Ed on the evening of 6 October, and it needed a sleeping pose for every creature at every level {#322}.
